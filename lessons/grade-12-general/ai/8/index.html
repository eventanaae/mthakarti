Responsible AI principles & AI use guidelines

🤖 الذكاء الاصطناعي والتكنولوجيا🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: AI L03 · slides 4–20

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي
Responsible AI (الذكاء الاصطناعي المسؤول) means designing, developing and using AI in a way that is ethical, safe, fair, transparent and beneficial to people and society. It is a set of principles and guidelines that helps people trust AI.
Key wordMeaning
Framework (إطار عمل)A structured set of processes, guidelines or principles used to manage a system
Auditing (التدقيق)Checking whether an AI system works fairly, safely and correctly
Compliance (الامتثال)Following rules, laws, policies or ethical standards
Governance (الحوكمة)The rules, policies, oversight and processes that guide and monitor how organisations or technologies operate responsibly
PrincipleMeaningHow it is achieved
Accuracy and reliabilityCorrect, dependable results; fewer mistakesTest with accurate, diverse data; monitor; fix errors
Fairness and bias preventionTreat all people fairly; do not favour any groupBalanced training data; test for unfair outcomes; review decisions
Privacy and data protectionKeep personal information safeCollect only needed data; secure storage; authorised access; approved purposes only
Transparency and explainabilityExplain how and why decisions are madeUnderstandable explanations; show the factors used; allow users to question decisions
Human oversight and accountabilityHumans monitor AI and stay responsibleHumans approve important decisions; clear responsibility
Safety, security and monitoringProtected from misuse; works safely; checked regularlyTest before and after deployment; protect from cyber threats; update
School admissions AI (analyses grades, attendance, entrance test scores, achievements):
Accurate – tested regularly, no calculation errors · Fair – no preference by gender, nationality, language or family background · Privacy – records stored securely, only authorised staff · Transparent – the school explains it uses grades, attendance and test scores · Human oversight – teachers and administrators review recommendations before final decisions · Safe – protected against hacking, updated and monitored.

Without responsible AI: students may be unfairly rejected, personal information could leak, decisions may be biased, and students and parents may lose trust.

AI use guideline = Principle → Rule → Check. Example: Principle: Fairness · Rule: "Test the model's accuracy separately for each demographic group before launch." · Check: "A tester compares approval rates across groups and flags any gap greater than 5%."
⚠️ A good rule must be checkable – "be fair" is a principle, not a rule.

Examples from the slides (consequence → prevention):

  • Accuracy: an AI misses lung infections on X-rays → patients get late treatment → regular testing, monitoring, review by doctors.
  • Fairness: a hiring AI trained mostly on one group rejects suitable candidates → unfair exclusion → balanced data, bias tests, human review.
  • Privacy: a banking AI must protect account details → stolen data causes financial loss and loss of trust → strong security, limited access.
  • Transparency: a grading AI explains marks from assignments, tests and criteria → otherwise students won't trust it.
  • Human oversight: doctors review AI recommendations before treatment.
  • Safety: self-driving cars are tested in different road conditions and monitored.

Starter scenario: an AI recommends business courses to a student whose maths grades recently improved and who wants engineering. The counsellor should not rely only on AI – it used mainly old performance (missing recent evidence). The school should explain how it works, humans remain responsible, and the system should be checked over time.

Activity 3.1 – hospital AI (symptoms, history, appointment type, emergency level). Example guideline: Principle: Privacy · Rule: "Only doctors and nurses on the patient's team can view their records." · Check: "Access logs are reviewed weekly for unauthorised access."
