Crises, crisis response & crisis management

Lesson 1 – AI for Global Challenges: how AI supports crisis response, humanitarian work, public health and environmental analysis by analysing data, predicting risks and supporting decisions.
Key wordMeaning
Humanitarian aid (المساعدات الإنسانية)Actions taken to help people affected by disasters, conflicts or health emergencies
Crisis (أزمة)A serious situation that needs immediate action to prevent harm to people or the environment
Environmental analysis (التحليل البيئي)Studying data about the natural world to understand changes and identify risks (pollution, drought, deforestation)
Predictive modelling (النمذجة التنبؤية)Using data to forecast what is likely to happen in the future

A crisis is a serious and unexpected event that creates a dangerous situation for individuals, communities or even the whole world. It needs urgent decisions and fast action to reduce harm.

Type of crisisExamples
Natural disastersFloods, earthquakes, hurricanes
Human-made incidentsIndustrial accidents
Public health emergenciesPandemics, disease outbreaks
Technological failuresSystem failures, cyberattacks

Crisis response (الاستجابة للأزمة) = the immediate action taken to protect lives, reduce harm and restore safety: evacuation, search and rescue, medical assistance, emergency alerts, damage assessment and control.

Crisis management (إدارة الأزمات) = the complete process of preparing for, responding to, recovering from and learning after a crisis. Its four stages:

  1. Planning – developing strategies and protocols before the crisis happens.
  2. Response – managing the situation effectively during the crisis.
  3. Recovery – organising support and rebuilding after the crisis.
  4. Evaluation – reviewing what happened to improve future planning.
Starter scenario: at 6:15 a.m. a strong earthquake hits a coastal city: buildings damaged, roads blocked, no electricity or internet in some areas, hospitals full. In the first 24 hours teams must decide which areas need help first, which roads are safe, which hospitals can still treat patients, and where to send food, water and medicine.
AI could help by analysing satellite/drone images and reports quickly – but it needs data, and missing data (e.g. areas with no internet) can leave some communities out.
⚠️ Crisis response is only what you do immediately when the crisis happens; crisis management is the full plan before, during and after.

Why are the four stages a cycle? The evaluation of one crisis feeds into the planning for the next one, so organisations keep improving.

StageHow AI can support it
PlanningPredictive modelling and risk maps show which areas are most likely to flood or be damaged
ResponseDetecting damaged roads and buildings from satellite images; finding safe routes for aid
RecoveryEstimating how many people are affected and where resources for rebuilding are needed
EvaluationAnalysing data from the event to see what worked and what was too slow

Thinking about the starter questions:

  • Data AI would need: satellite and drone images, sensor data, social media posts, hospital capacity, road information.
  • Is the data available? Not always – power and internet may be down, so the data can be incomplete.
  • Wrong predictions could send help to the wrong place and waste precious time.
  • Missing communities in the data may be ignored – a fairness problem.
  • Rescue teams should not trust AI completely: AI supports human judgement; it does not replace it.
