Project resource: Space Rover components

The project resource G12_Project_Components contains ready-made 3D CAD models of the parts used to design the Grade 12 Space Rover (with a robotic arm). You place these parts into your own rover design so that sizes and mounting holes are correct.

Component (file)Role in the rover
Raspberry Pi 3 B+ with motor driver HAT ("Ibtikar")Controller (the "brain"): runs the program; the motor driver board supplies the higher current that motors need
HM-13 Bluetooth moduleWireless communication – e.g. controlling the rover from a phone
DC robot motor (single axle) + robot wheelDrive – turns the wheels
SG90 mini servo + cross horn / single hornPrecise angle movement, e.g. robotic arm joints or steering; the horns connect the servo shaft to linkages
Rechargeable batteryPower supply
Solar panel 120 mm × 37 mmRenewable energy to help charge the battery, like real planetary rovers
25 × 25 × 10 mm 12 V brushless cooling fan + fan mountCooling the electronics
G12G Space Rover robotic armThe arm assembly that the servos move
The parts follow the sense → think → act idea: the controller (Raspberry Pi) thinks, motors and servos act, the battery and solar panel power everything, and Bluetooth lets a human send commands.

File formats: .step (STEP) and .iges (IGES) are neutral 3D CAD exchange formats – they can be opened in most CAD programs. Tinkercad (linked in the course folder) imports mesh files such as STL/OBJ, so STEP parts are usually converted to STL first.

⚠️ The Raspberry Pi pins cannot power motors directly – that is why a motor driver is used. A servo turns to an angle; a DC motor spins continuously.

How the parts work together (general knowledge):

  • Raspberry Pi 3 B+ is a small single-board computer with a 40-pin GPIO header; it can run Python programs and also has built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.
  • A HAT ("Hardware Attached on Top") is an add-on board that plugs onto the Pi's pins – here it is a motor driver, which switches larger currents and can reverse motor direction.
  • The SG90 micro servo is controlled by a PWM signal: the pulse width sets the angle (roughly 0–180°). It is light and small, so it suits a small robotic arm with light loads.
  • A DC geared motor gives continuous rotation with enough torque to drive the wheels.
  • A cooling fan stops the controller overheating – important in hot conditions.

Link to the AI lessons: if you add AI (e.g. obstacle or object recognition), think about responsible AI: test it in different lighting (like Activity 4.7), explain its decisions, and keep a human able to take control.
