How AI is reshaping the labour market

Artificial intelligence is changing how work is performed across many industries.

Traditional automationArtificial intelligence
Follows fixed rulesAnalyses data
Performs repetitive tasksLearns from patterns
Cannot adapt to new situationsSupports decision-making and can adapt to changing situations
Before AIAfter AI
Customer service agent answers routine questionsAgent uses AI tools to handle complex issues and give personalised support
Accountant manually reviews recordsAccountant uses AI to analyse large datasets, find patterns and support decisions
AI is transforming jobs rather than simply replacing them – it automates routine tasks so workers can focus on complex, analytical and creative work, and it creates new roles.
More likely to be affected by AILess likely to be fully automated
Repetitive tasks · predictable processes · large amounts of data processing (e.g. data entry, routine administration, simple record keeping)Creativity · complex problem-solving · human interaction · leadership and judgement (e.g. teachers, managers, designers, healthcare professionals)

World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs 2024 (2025–2030): the fastest-growing jobs are technology roles such as Big Data Specialists, FinTech Engineers, AI and Machine Learning Specialists and software developers; the fastest-declining are routine roles such as postal service clerks, bank tellers and data entry clerks.

⚠️ The lesson's message is not "AI will take all jobs" – it is that AI changes jobs and creates new ones; the impact depends on how societies and organisations respond.

Opportunities – AI can: increase productivity by automating repetitive tasks · let workers focus on complex and creative activities · improve decisions through data analysis · create new jobs (AI development, machine learning, data analysis) · improve efficiency and resource allocation.

Challenges – AI can also: displace some jobs · increase demand for advanced technical skills · cause skill gaps if workers are not prepared · raise concerns about fairness, bias and inequality · create uncertainty for workers whose roles change.

Activity 2.2 – future job analysis (engineer, teacher, healthcare worker, business manager, government employee). Example for a teacher: AI could automate marking of simple quizzes and attendance; human skills still needed for motivating students, pastoral care and judging creative work; new skills include using AI tools wisely and teaching AI literacy.

New roles created: AI specialists, data analysts, AI system trainers, AI ethics and governance roles. Tasks automated: data entry, scheduling, routine reporting.
