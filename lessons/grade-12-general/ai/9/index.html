AI governance, regulation, compliance & frameworks

🤖 الذكاء الاصطناعي والتكنولوجيا🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: AI L03 · slides 21–37

AI governance = the system of policies, processes and frameworks that guide the responsible development and use of AI. It involves governments, organisations and international bodies and addresses safety, ethics, privacy, security and accountability.

AI regulations (اللوائح التنظيمية) = laws and legal requirements that govern how AI is developed, deployed and used. They are needed to make AI safe, ethical, transparent and accountable; protect privacy, security and fundamental rights; reduce risks (bias, misinformation, cyberattacks, misuse of personal data); and help organisations use AI responsibly.

Level of governanceWhat it includes
NationalStrategies, laws and regulators within one country
OrganisationalInternal policies, ethics boards, risk registers and AI management systems
InternationalShared, mostly voluntary principles across borders

AI compliance = making sure an AI system follows relevant laws, regulations, standards and organisational policies (e.g. a healthcare AI protects patient data and confidentiality). AI auditing = reviewing and evaluating an AI system to check whether it meets compliance requirements.

An AI audit checks…Question
1 DataIs the data accurate, secure and free from unfair bias?
2 PerformanceDoes the system produce reliable results?
3 FairnessDoes it treat different groups equally?
4 TransparencyCan its decisions be explained?
5 ComplianceDoes it follow applicable regulations and policies?

A framework is a structured approach that organises principles, processes or guidelines – like the foundation and structural plan of a house that shows builders how the parts fit together. An AI governance framework provides principles, guidance and mechanisms for responsible development, deployment, use and oversight of AI.

  • National frameworks – made by individual countries (e.g. the UAE) to align AI with national priorities, laws and values.
  • International frameworks – made by global organisations such as UNESCO, OECD and the European Union to promote shared principles and cooperation, because AI operates across borders.
UAE: the Artificial Intelligence and Data Authority was established in June 2026 to unify national efforts in AI, data and digital government. It brings together the Office of Artificial Intelligence, Digital Economy and Remote Work Applications; the UAE Data Office; and the Digital Government Sector at TDRA. It reports directly to the UAE Cabinet and is led by H.E. Omar Sultan Al Olama, Minister of State for Artificial Intelligence.
⚠️ Governance = the rules and oversight system; auditing = checking a specific AI system against those rules. International principles are mostly voluntary; national laws are binding.

International frameworks aim to: use AI ethically · protect human rights and privacy · reduce bias and discrimination · increase transparency and accountability · cooperate internationally on AI safety and governance.

Activity 3.3 – compliance audit:

  • Scenario 1 (hospital waiting times): good – patients are told AI is used; accuracy checked occasionally. Gaps – trained on old data but now serves patients of different backgrounds (fairness/accuracy risk), no explanation of how predictions are made (transparency), no full independent review since deployment.
  • Scenario 2 (crowd monitoring): good – tested before deployment, encrypted storage, public information about its purpose, regular technical reviews. Gap – fewer cameras in some areas and little evidence it performs equally across locations and groups (fairness / data risk).
