AI auditing & trustworthy AI

🤖 الذكاء الاصطناعي والتكنولوجيا🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: AI L04 · slides 28–43

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي
AI auditing examines how AI systems use data, make decisions and comply with legal and ethical requirements. Auditors identify risks (bias, errors, security issues) and recommend improvements.

Why it matters: detects bias before harm · verifies compliance over time · builds public trust · protects organisations from legal consequences.

How auditing makes AI trustworthy: Transparency (documented record of how AI works) · Fairness (regular bias checks) · Safety (find vulnerabilities and failures) · Accountability (audit reports show who knew what and when) · Continuous improvement (detect changes in performance over time).

AI audit plan (what, who, when):
1) Define the purpose and scope – what the audit will examine (e.g. is a school grading AI fair and accurate?).
2) Select auditors – qualified internal teams or independent auditors (more objective).
3) Identify areas to examine – data, performance, explainability, compliance.
4) Collect and analyse evidence – datasets, records, performance reports, outcomes; test different scenarios.
5) Report findings and improve – document problems, recommend improvements, keep monitoring after changes.
⚠️ An audit is not a one-time event: systems are monitored again after changes. Comparing approval rates between groups = fairness audit; checking personal data is used legally = compliance audit.

Activity 4.6 – welfare benefits AI (deployed 2 years ago, never independently audited, staff review a random 5% sample, applicants from certain areas rejected far more often):

  • Auditing failures: (1) no independent audit in two years – no objective check; (2) a small random sample is unlikely to reveal a pattern affecting specific areas, so bias went undetected.
  • Audit first: an ethical/fairness audit (compare rejection rates by area for similar cases), supported by a data audit (is area data acting as an unfair factor?).
  • Basic plan: independent auditors; check data, fairness, explainability and compliance; audit regularly (e.g. every year) plus continuous monitoring.

Practical activity 4.7 – audit your own AI:

  1. Build with bias on purpose: choose two classes (e.g. apple vs orange), collect 10–15 images per class, make one class biased (only bright light, one angle or one colour), train an image classifier.
  2. Swap and test: another group tests with new images, records wrong answers and works out which training-data pattern caused them.
  3. Audit report: what you tested · problems (bias, errors, low accuracy) · why · recommended fix (more varied data, retrain, human review).
