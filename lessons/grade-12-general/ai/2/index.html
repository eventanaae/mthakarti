AI in crisis response

🤖 الذكاء الاصطناعي والتكنولوجيا🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: AI L01 · slides 12–16

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

During a crisis, responders must answer urgent questions: Which areas are most damaged? Who needs medical help first? How much food, water and medicine is required?

AI helps emergency teams by:

  • Processing large amounts of data quickly
  • Analysing satellite, drone and sensor data
  • Predicting risks such as floods or disease spread
  • Estimating the number of people affected
  • Supporting resource allocation (توزيع الموارد) and decision-making
AI supports human decision-making – it does not replace human judgement.

Examples of AI applications: detecting damaged roads and buildings from satellite images · predicting flood or wildfire spread · forecasting disease outbreaks · finding the safest routes for delivering aid · monitoring environmental changes.

Benefits: faster emergency response · better use of resources · improved coordination between response teams.

How AI helps organisations like the United NationsWhat it does
Real-time digital impact assessmentsCombine satellite images, social media and light data to assess damage quickly after a disaster
Early crisis warning systemsMonitor risks such as hate speech, economic issues or forced migration to predict crises early
Digital assistantsAI chatbots and co-pilots help crisis workers find information, write reports and plan responses
Crisis training and simulationAI and augmented reality train responders through virtual simulations with personalised learning
Earthquake: AI analyses satellite images and social media to find severely damaged areas that have not yet received aid; AI-powered robots search collapsed buildings for survivors.
Wildfires: AI models predict how the fire will spread, suggest evacuation routes and help teams decide where to send medical staff and equipment.
⚠️ AI gives recommendations; trained humans still make the final, critical decisions.

Both examples follow the same four-step pipeline:

  1. Collect data – satellite images, social media posts, weather, terrain, sensors, drones.
  2. AI analysis / prediction – the model finds heavily damaged areas or predicts how a fire will spread.
  3. Actionable insights / recommendations – maps of priority areas, evacuation routes, where to deploy resources.
  4. On-the-ground action – rescue robots, fire crews and medical teams act faster and go where help is needed most.

Why speed matters: in the first hours after a disaster, people trapped under rubble have the best chance of survival. AI can scan thousands of images in minutes – work that would take humans days.

Why humans stay in control: data after a disaster is often incomplete or noisy (smoke, clouds, no internet). A human checks whether the AI output makes sense in the local context before acting.
