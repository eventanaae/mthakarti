Humanitarian aid, AI in healthcare & ethical risks

🤖 الذكاء الاصطناعي والتكنولوجيا🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: AI L01 · slides 17–25

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Humanitarian aid is assistance designed to protect and support people affected by crises such as natural disasters (earthquakes, floods, hurricanes) and disease outbreaks (pandemics and epidemics such as COVID). Humanitarian organisations provide food, shelter, medical care, education and protection.

AI helps humanitarian organisations by: analysing satellite imagery, weather, mobile and health data · identifying affected communities · predicting changing risks and needs · prioritising aid and resources · supporting faster, better-informed decisions.

ApplicationHow AI helps
Disaster predictionAnalyses weather, seismic and satellite data; predicts floods, storms and landslides; gives early warnings
Mapping affected areasAnalyses satellite and drone images; maps damaged roads and flooded areas; uses NLP (معالجة اللغة الطبيعية) to analyse social media posts and detect urgent needs
Tracking populationsTracks population movements; plans shelters and aid. UNHCR predicts displacement using rainfall, food prices and aid data; the Red Cross uses facial recognition to reconnect separated families

AI in healthcare (public health): analyse health and environmental data quickly · detect outbreaks earlier · predict the spread of infectious diseases · support early warning · identify communities needing more resources · plan medical staff, equipment and vaccines. Examples: predicting influenza outbreaks; analysing air quality data.

Disease surveillance: AI analyses health records, weather patterns and migration data to predict outbreaks (flu, COVID-19) before they spread widely.
Vaccination support: AI tracks vaccination schedules, sends reminders to families and finds communities with low vaccination rates.

Risks and ethical challenges

RiskMeaning
Bias (التحيز)Incomplete or unrepresentative training data → AI may overlook certain communities
MisinformationAI can produce incorrect information with high confidence
PrivacySensitive personal and medical data must be protected
Lack of transparencySome models do not explain how they reached a decision
Unequal accessCommunities with limited internet or digital infrastructure benefit less
Human oversight is essentialAI should support human decision-making, not replace it
Humanitarian professionals must check the accuracy of AI outputs, consider cultural and local context, protect people's rights and dignity, and make the final decisions. The principle of "do no harm" remains central.
⚠️ "High confidence" does not mean "correct" – AI can be confidently wrong (misinformation).

Activities 1.1 and 1.2 – robots in humanitarian work. You research real AI-powered robots (preferably from the UAE) that search for survivors in collapsed buildings, monitor hospital patients, or deliver medical supplies. For each robot ask:

  • What problem does it solve?
  • How does it collect data? (cameras, sensors, patient vital signs)
  • What actions does it perform?
  • Compared with traditional response, what are the advantages (speed, safety for rescuers, works 24/7) and limitations (cost, battery life, needs good data, cannot replace human care)?

Then you redesign one robot for an earthquake scenario: add a new feature, decide what extra data the AI needs, explain how it helps responders or patients, and list ethical or practical challenges (privacy of patient data, reliability in dust and rubble, cost).

Linking risks to solutions: bias → use representative data from all communities; misinformation → humans verify outputs; privacy → protect and limit data; lack of transparency → prefer explainable systems; unequal access → offer offline or low-tech options.
