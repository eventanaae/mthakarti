Exam Map & Strategy (End-of-Term 1)

📗 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Exam Coverage 12G T1 2026-27 · Test Specs Stage 8 (B2.1)

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

The End-of-Term 1 English exam for Grade 12 General (Grade 12 GLG, Stage 8, CEFR B2.1; the same curriculum as Grade 11 Advanced) is electronic (SwiftAssess), lasts 150 minutes, has 28 questions and is out of 100 marks: Reading 60 + Writing 40.

Grammar list (Part 2): verb forms (to-infinitive / bare infinitive / -ing), present simple passive, present continuous passive, non-defining relative clauses, reported speech, adverbs of degree, subordinating conjunctions.
⚠️ Questions may appear in a different order in the real exam. Every reading question is worth 2 or 3 marks, so never leave one blank.

The idea in detail

Before you study, you need a map (خريطة) of the exam. The official Exam Coverage document for Grade 12 General shows five parts and 28 questions. The exam is done on a computer (SwiftAssess), including the writing, and you have 150 minutes.

  • Part 1 – Vocabulary (Q1–Q5): one text of about 170 words with 5 gaps. Each gap has three options. The words come from two unit lists: Money Talks (broke, income, make ends meet, raise capital, student loan, scholarship, put aside, withdrawal, take risks, risk-taker, influence, persuade) and Nail That Job! (career, occupation, labor, internship, adaptability, communication, experience, shortlisted, background, flexible, rigid). 2 marks each.
  • Part 2 – Grammar (Q6–Q10): an expository (نص تفسيري) text about career trends with 5 gaps. 2 marks each.
  • Part 3A – Reading (Q11–Q18): an expository text (B2.1) about consumerism and the brain or money and motivation. 2 specific-information questions, 3 detail questions, 1 main point, 1 inference, 1 overall meaning. 3 marks each = 24.
  • Part 3B – Reading (Q19–Q26): a harder text (B2.2) about whether jobs belong to men or women. 3 detail questions, 1 mood/tone/attitude, 2 inference, 1 connection between ideas, 1 overall meaning. 2 marks each = 16.
  • Part 4 – Writing (Q27–Q28): 4.1 read a text of about 180 words and write its main ideas in your own words in 4–5 sentences (15 marks); 4.2 an opinion essay with three prompts, at least 200 words in paragraphs (25 marks). Themes: Essential Skills for Your Future Career or The Effects of Advertising.

Why / how it works

Reading is worth 60 marks and writing 40. Notice that 3A questions are worth 3 marks each, more than any other MCQ, so read that text carefully. Part 3B is "beyond the Grade Level Goal" – it is the hardest reading, but each question is worth only 2 marks, so do not spend too long on one question. The writing is 40% of the total, so keep at least 50–60 minutes for Part 4.

Suggested timing (150 min): Part 1 – 10 min; Part 2 – 10 min; Part 3A – 25 min; Part 3B – 25 min; Part 4.1 – 20 min; Part 4.2 – 45 min; final check – 15 min.

Worked example 1: a Part 1 gap

"Salem's part-time job gives him a small monthly ____, so he must spend carefully." A) withdrawal B) income C) occupation
Step 1 – read the whole sentence: it is about money he receives.
Step 2 – part of speech: after "a small monthly" we need a noun; all three are nouns.
Step 3 – meaning: a withdrawal is money you take out of a bank; an occupation is a job; income is money you receive. Answer: B.

Worked example 2: a Part 2 gap

"Nowadays, many routine tasks ____ by computer programs." A) are being done B) are doing C) do
Tasks cannot do anything themselves – they receive the action, so we need a passive. "Nowadays" shows a change in progress → present continuous passive. Answer: A.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Reading only the sentence with the gap and ignoring the context. 2) Choosing an answer in 3A/3B because it "sounds true" in real life, not because the text says it. 3) Writing fewer than 200 words in the essay or forgetting one of the three prompts. 4) Copying sentences from the text in Part 4.1 – the rubric gives low marks for copied wording.

Exam corner

Numbers to remember: 28 questions · 100 marks · 150 minutes · 170-word texts in Parts 1–2 · 300–330-word texts in Parts 3A–3B · 3A = 3 marks each, 3B = 2 marks each · summary 4–5 sentences (15) · essay 200+ words, 3 prompts (25).

Quick summary

  • Parts: Vocabulary 10 · Grammar 10 · Reading 3A 24 · Reading 3B 16 · Writing 40.
  • All questions except writing: multiple choice, A/B/C.
  • Units: Money Talks + Nail That Job!
  • Gap strategy: read all → part of speech → collocation/grammar clue → test each option.
