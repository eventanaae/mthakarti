Money Talks Vocabulary A: Managing Money

📗 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 1 · Money Talks · broke, income, make ends meet, put aside, withdrawal

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي
Word / phraseMeaningExample
broke (adj, informal)having no moneyI spent everything on a new phone, so now I'm broke.
income (n)money you receive from work or any other sourceWith two part-time jobs, her monthly income is AED 4,000.
make ends meetto have just enough money to pay for what you needIt is hard to make ends meet when prices keep rising.
put aside (phrasal v)to save money for laterHe puts aside AED 200 every month for emergencies.
withdrawal (n)taking money out of a bank accountThe statement shows a withdrawal of AED 500 from the ATM.

Related course words: budget (a plan / limited amount of money to spend), earn (receive money for work), cost an arm and a leg (be very expensive), drop out of (leave without finishing), wages (money paid for work, usually weekly), expenses (money you spend).

Word families: income (n) – earn (v) – earnings (n); withdraw (v) – withdrawal (n); save (v) – savings (n). Collocations: a monthly / low / steady income; make a withdrawal; go on a budget; stick to a budget; put money aside.
⚠️ broke is an adjective here ("I'm broke"), not the past of break. make ends meet is fixed: never "do ends meet" or "make end meets".

The idea in detail

The unit Money Talks starts with a student, Jeff, who keeps asking his father for money. His story uses the key words of managing money.

WordMeaningOpposite / partner
broke (مفلس / بلا نقود)with no money at allwell-off, rich
income (دخل)money coming IN (salary, wages, allowance)expenses (مصاريف) = money going OUT
make ends meet (يغطي احتياجاته بالكاد)income just covers expensesbe in debt
put aside (يدّخر / يضع جانبًا)save for laterspend, waste
withdrawal (سحب (من الحساب))money taken OUT of a bank accountdeposit (إيداع) = money put IN

Why / how it works

Think of a money "flow": income comes in → you make a budget → you put aside some money → you pay expenses (sometimes by making a withdrawal) → if income is enough you make ends meet; if not, you are broke. When you see a gap, ask: is money coming in, going out, being saved, or finished?

Grammar notes. put aside is separable: "put aside AED 100" or "put AED 100 aside" or "put it aside" (not "put aside it"). Income is usually singular: "a high income". Withdrawal is countable: "two withdrawals last week".

Worked example 1

"After paying rent and buying groceries, Hamad has almost nothing left. He can only just ____." A) make ends meet B) take risks C) raise capital
"only just" + "almost nothing left" → income barely covers needs = make ends meet.

Worked example 2

"The bank statement shows a ____ of AED 300 that Mariam cannot remember." A) scholarship B) withdrawal C) background
Something on a bank statement that took money out = withdrawal.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Mixing income (money in) and withdrawal (money out). 2) "I am broken" = something is damaged; for money say "I am broke". 3) "I put aside" with no object – you must say what you save: "put aside money". 4) Using earn for a scholarship – you receive or win a scholarship.

Exam corner

How it is asked: Part 1 gap-fill in a story about a student's budget. Look for signal words: monthly, salary, allowance → income; save, emergencies, each week → put aside; ATM, account, statement → withdrawal; just enough, only just, struggle → make ends meet; no money left → broke.

Quick summary

  • income = money in; withdrawal = money out of the bank.
  • put aside = save; make ends meet = just enough; broke = no money.
  • Budget words: go on / stick to a budget; tight budget.
