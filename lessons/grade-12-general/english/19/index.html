Part 4.2: The Opinion Essay (200+ words, 3 prompts)

📗 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 4.2 · 25 marks · FL.16, FL.24, FL.46 · Writing Rubric G11A/12G

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Task: one sentence introduces the topic, then: "Write an opinion essay in which you: • prompt 1 • prompt 2 • prompt 3. Write at least 200 words in paragraphs." Themes: Essential Skills for Your Future Career or The Effects of Advertising.

ParagraphContent
IntroductionIntroduce the topic in your own sentence (don't copy the prompt) → two views → your opinion → what the essay will cover.
Body 1Prompt 1: topic sentence + explanation + example.
Body 2Prompt 2: evidence-based argument (reasons, evidence, examples, possibly the other side).
Body 3Prompt 3: advice (FL.24).
ConclusionRestate your opinion in different words + final thought.
Rubric criterion (×5 = 25)Top band
Task CompletionAll parts of the prompt, clear detailed ideas, reasons and explanations.
StructureWell-developed paragraphs with topic sentences; intro and conclusion; suitable tone; range of connectors.
GrammarRange of simple and complex sentences (relative clauses, conditionals, passive, reported speech).
VocabularyWide, topic-specific, some less common words; little repetition.
Spelling & PunctuationAlmost all words correct; commas, apostrophes, colons used accurately.
Opinion (FL.16): In my view… / I would argue that… / It seems to me that… Evidence (FL.46): Evidence suggests… / For instance… / This shows that… / As a result… Advice (FL.24): Teenagers should… / I would advise… to… / It is a good idea to… / If I were you, I would…
⚠️ Missing one prompt drops Task Completion to 3 or lower. Copying from the question or using AI tools = 0 for the whole script.

The idea in detail

The opinion essay (مقال رأي) is worth 25 marks – a quarter of the exam. The marker uses five criteria: Task Completion (إنجاز المهمة), Structure (البنية والتنظيم), Grammar (القواعد), Vocabulary (المفردات) and Spelling & Punctuation (الإملاء وعلامات الترقيم), each from 0 to 5.

Why / how it works

Plan first (5 minutes). Copy the three prompts as headings in your notes; each one becomes a body paragraph. Under each, write: point → reason → example. This guarantees full task completion.

Essay starter (from the course): "These days, … . Some people think that …, while others believe that … . In my opinion, … because … . In this essay, I will …"

Building an evidence-based argument (FL.46): make a claim → give a reason → support it with evidence (a fact, a study, a statistic you know, a real example) → explain how the evidence supports the claim. Example: "Advertising strongly shapes teenagers' choices. This is because adverts link products with popularity. For instance, influencers who promote luxury lifestyles can encourage young people to overspend. This suggests that adverts sell feelings, not just products."

Show grammar range (Grammar 5/5 needs complex sentences): a defining relative clause ("adverts that target teenagers"), a non-defining clause ("…, which is why…"), the passive ("young people are persuaded…"), a conditional ("If teenagers wait a day, they…"), subordinating conjunctions ("Although adverts can be useful, …").

Worked example: planning the advertising prompt

Prompt: (1) explain how advertising influences what young people buy; (2) discuss whether the effects are mainly positive or negative, giving evidence; (3) give advice to teenagers on making wise choices.
Plan → B1: influencers + targeted online ads + urgency messages. B2: positive (information, comparing features) vs negative (overspending, self-image) → mainly negative. B3: budget, wait 24 hours, read independent reviews, ask "need or want?"

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Under 200 words. 2) One long paragraph. 3) No clear opinion in the introduction. 4) Informal style ("gonna", "u", too many exclamation marks). 5) Contractions everywhere – prefer "do not". 6) Repeating "good" and "bad" – use beneficial, harmful, persuasive, misleading.

Exam corner

Time: about 45 minutes (5 plan, 35 write, 5 check). Aim for 230–280 words – enough to develop all three prompts without rushing. Final check: 3 prompts ✔ paragraphs ✔ opinion ✔ connectors ✔ spelling/capital letters ✔ word count ✔.

Quick summary

  • 5 paragraphs: intro + 3 prompts + conclusion; 200+ words.
  • Opinion language, evidence language, advice language.
  • Five rubric criteria × 5 marks.
