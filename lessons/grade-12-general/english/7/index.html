Grammar 1: Verb Forms (to-infinitive, base verb, -ing)

⚠️ try doing = experiment to see if it works ("Try cutting down on snacks"); try to do = make an effort, it may be difficult ("I tried to call, but the line was busy"). suggest never takes "someone to": ❌ "I suggest you to save" ✔ "I suggest saving / I suggest that you save".

The idea in detail

When two verbs come together, the first verb decides the form of the second (الفعل الأول يحدد شكل الفعل الثاني). There is no logic you can guess – you must learn the groups. In this unit the patterns appear mostly in advice language (FL.24).

Why / how it works

  • + to-infinitive (المصدر مع to): verbs about the future, plans and decisions – want, hope, plan, decide, agree, refuse, promise, afford, manage, learn; also be willing to, be able to.
  • + object + to-infinitive: verbs where one person pushes another – advise, persuade, encourage, allow, ask, tell, expect, want someone to.
  • + base verb (المصدر بدون to): after modals (should, could, must, can), had better, let / make someone, Why don't you…?
  • + -ing (صيغة -ing): suggest, recommend, avoid, enjoy, consider, keep, finish, mind; and after every preposition (look into getting, interested in working, instead of spending, before launching).
  • suggest / recommend that + subject + base verb: "He suggested that I find cheaper accommodation." (base verb even after he/she: "that he put", not "puts").

Worked example 1

"Students leaving college should be willing ____ their technical knowledge." A) to upgrade B) upgrading C) upgrade → A: be willing + to-infinitive.

Worked example 2

Complete: "Why don't you look into ____ (get) a part-time job?" → getting (after the preposition into). "He advised me ____ (apply) for the grant." → to apply. "I suggest ____ (make) a budget." → making.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) "I suggest you to save" → I suggest saving / I suggest (that) you save. 2) "You should to go" → You should go. 3) "He decided buying" → decided to buy. 4) "She avoided to spend" → avoided spending. 5) "advised him don't drop out" → advised him not to drop out.

Exam corner

In Part 2 the three options usually are: to do / doing / do. Look at the word just before the gap: a modal → base; a preposition → -ing; willing / want / decide / plan / hope / afford → to-infinitive; suggest / avoid / enjoy / consider → -ing.

Quick summary

  • Plans/decisions → to-infinitive; person + to (advise, persuade).
  • Modals, had better, Why don't you → base verb.
  • suggest, recommend, avoid, enjoy, prepositions → -ing.
  • suggest that + subject + base verb.
