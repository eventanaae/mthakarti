Model Essays: The Effects of Advertising & Career Skills

📗 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 4.2 · EOT Essay Practice (Effects of Advertising) · Practice paper prompt

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Prompt: Advertising is a huge industry, and companies spend more money than ever trying to attract young customers. Write an opinion essay in which you: • explain how advertising influences what young people buy • discuss whether the effects of advertising on young people are mainly positive or negative, giving evidence • give advice to teenagers on how to make wise choices when they see adverts.

Model answer (about 290 words)

These days, young people see adverts everywhere, from their phones to the walls of shopping malls. Some people think that advertising simply helps customers discover new products, while others believe that it pushes teenagers to buy things they do not need. In my opinion, the effects of advertising on young people are mainly negative because adverts sell feelings rather than facts. In this essay, I will explain how adverts work, discuss their effects and give some advice.

Advertising influences young buyers in several clever ways. Firstly, many products are promoted by influencers whom teenagers admire, so a pair of trainers can seem like a key to popularity. Secondly, online adverts are targeted at users who have searched for similar items. Finally, messages such as "only two left" create urgency, which persuades people to buy quickly without thinking.

Admittedly, advertising has some benefits. Honest adverts allow consumers to compare features and learn about useful services. However, evidence suggests that the negative effects are stronger. Many teenagers spend more than their allowance on fashion and games, and some feel unhappy when they compare their lives with the perfect images they see online. This shows that advertising can damage both budgets and self-image.

So what should teenagers do? I would advise them to set a monthly budget and stick to it. It is also a good idea to wait at least a day before buying anything that was not planned, and to read reviews written by people who are not paid to promote the product. Most importantly, they should ask themselves whether they really need the item.

In conclusion, although advertising can provide useful information, it mostly encourages young people to spend unwisely. If teenagers learn to recognise these techniques, they can become smarter and more confident consumers.
Highlights: opinion in paragraph 1 · one paragraph per prompt · sequencing (Firstly, Secondly, Finally) · concession (Admittedly… However…) · relative clauses (whom teenagers admire, users who have searched, which persuades) · passive (are promoted, are targeted) · advice language · conditional in the conclusion.

The idea in detail

The model above answers the official practice prompt The Effects of Advertising. Notice that every prompt has its own paragraph and that the opinion is clear from the first paragraph to the last. Useful topic vocabulary: promote (يروّج), target (يستهدف), urgency (الإلحاح / الاستعجال), influencer (مؤثر), overspend (يُسرف في الإنفاق), misleading (مُضلّل), impulse buy (شراء اندفاعي), self-image (صورة الذات).

Second theme: Essential Skills for Your Future Career

Practice prompt: Are practical skills more important than qualifications when preparing for a future career? Write an opinion essay in which you: (1) explain which skills employers value and why; (2) discuss the importance of qualifications, using examples or evidence; (3) advise UAE students on how to prepare for the changing job market.

Plan + key sentences
Intro: "The job market is changing faster than ever, and students often wonder whether a certificate is enough. I would argue that qualifications open the door, but practical skills keep it open."
B1 (skills): communication, adaptability, teamwork, digital skills – "Employers value adaptability because technology changes the way jobs are done." Example: an internship where you solve real problems.
B2 (qualifications): "Qualifications remain important. For instance, doctors and engineers cannot work without a recognised degree. However, a degree alone does not show how someone deals with deadlines or customers."
B3 (advice): "UAE students should look into internships and volunteering, upgrade their digital skills through online courses and practise presenting ideas in English. If I were a Grade 12 student today, I would also join a school club that develops leadership."
Conclusion: "In short, the strongest candidates combine solid qualifications with flexible, practical skills."

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Treating prompt 2 as "say both are important" with no evidence – give an example. 2) Advice for "students" in general when the prompt says UAE students – mention local opportunities. 3) Using the model word for word in the exam: examiners see memorised essays; adapt ideas to the actual prompt.

Exam corner

Learn a flexible "frame": intro formula, concession formula (Admittedly… However…), evidence formula (Evidence suggests… This shows that…), advice formula (I would advise… to… / It is a good idea to…), conclusion formula (In conclusion, although…, …). Fill it with ideas from the prompt.

Quick summary

  • One paragraph per prompt; opinion stated and restated.
  • Use evidence + examples, concession + counter-argument.
  • Show range: relative clauses, passive, conditionals, advice patterns.
