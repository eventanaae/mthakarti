Part 3B Practice: 'Pink Jobs, Blue Jobs?'

📗 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 3B · Nail That Job! · A Man's or a Woman's Job? (B2.2, ±300 words)

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Practice text (original, expository, about 300 words)

[1] Ask a group of young children to draw a pilot, a nurse and an engineer, and many will draw a man, a woman and a man. By the age of six, most children have already absorbed ideas about which jobs "belong" to which gender. These ideas rarely come from a single source; they are built slowly by toys, television, conversations and the adults children see at work.

[2] Such labels can quietly narrow a young person's future. A girl who loves solving technical problems may never consider engineering if nobody suggests it to her. Equally, a boy who is patient and caring may reject nursing because he fears that friends will tease him. In both cases, talent is lost – not because of ability, but because of expectation.

[3] There are signs that this picture is changing. In the UAE, for example, women now study engineering and science in large numbers, and many of the scientists behind the country's space programme are women. Some hospitals are also actively recruiting male nurses, partly because patients benefit from a workforce that reflects society.

[4] Nevertheless, progress is uneven. Surveys in many countries still show that certain occupations remain dominated by one gender, and people who enter "unusual" fields sometimes report feeling isolated. Changing laws and policies is relatively quick; changing attitudes takes far longer.

[5] What, then, can be done? Schools can invite speakers who break stereotypes, and parents can praise skills rather than gender-based interests. Employers, meanwhile, can make sure that interviews focus on competence, experience and communication. None of this will transform society overnight. However, if each generation grows up with slightly wider choices than the one before, the question "Is this a man's job or a woman's job?" may one day sound as strange as asking whether a book is suitable for left-handed readers.

Expect: 3 detail + 1 tone/attitude + 2 inference + 1 connection + 1 overall meaning (2 marks each).
⚠️ The writer is neither completely positive nor negative: paragraph 3 = progress, paragraph 4 = "progress is uneven", paragraph 5 = practical hope. Tone: cautiously optimistic / hopeful but realistic.

The idea in detail

Part 3B is "beyond the Grade Level Goal" (B2.2), so the language is richer and more is implied. Useful words: absorb (يستوعب / يتشرّب) ideas, narrow (يُضيّق) a future, tease (يسخر من), recruit (يوظّف / يستقطب), dominated by (تهيمن عليه فئة), isolated (معزول), stereotype (صورة نمطية), competence (الكفاءة), overnight (بين ليلة وضحاها).

Worked example: connection question

"Which situation best reflects the writer's ideas?" A) A school careers day where a female pilot and a male nurse talk to students. B) A company that hires only men for technical posts. C) A parent who tells a daughter engineering is too difficult for girls. → A: paragraph 5 recommends "speakers who break stereotypes".

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Choosing "angry" or "completely negative" for the tone because paragraph 4 lists problems – read the conclusion. 2) Thinking the writer wants everyone to choose unusual jobs – the point is fair choice. 3) Taking the left-handed comparison literally.

Exam corner

8 questions × 2 marks = 16. Linkers guide the structure: Equally (adds a parallel example), Nevertheless (contrast with the progress before), What, then, can be done? (introduces solutions), However (the final hopeful point).

Quick summary

  • Labels start early and narrow choices; talent is lost.
  • Progress in the UAE and elsewhere, but uneven; attitudes change slowly.
  • Solutions: role models, praise skills, hire for competence.
  • Tone: hopeful but realistic.
