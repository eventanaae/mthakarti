Grammar 6: Adverbs of Degree & Comparatives

📗 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 · G.2.8 (+ G.1.2 comparatives) · R&W pp. 24–31

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Adverbs of degree tell us how much or to what extent. They usually go before an adjective, adverb or verb.

"Salaries in some countries are much lower than in others." · "Flexible hours are slightly more common than fixed hours (51% vs 49%)." · "Telecommuting is significantly less stressful than commuting."
too + adj = more than is good ("too expensive to buy") · adj + enough ("old enough to work") · so + adj / such (a) + adj + noun ("so useful", "such a useful skill").
⚠️ ❌ "very cheaper" / "very more expensive" → ✔ much cheaper, far more expensive. ❌ "enough old" → ✔ "old enough". ❌ "very essential / very unique" – use absolutely essential or just essential.

The idea in detail

Adverbs of degree (ظروف الدرجة) make meaning stronger or weaker: quite good, extremely difficult, slightly higher. In the course they are used to describe data and comparisons (career trends, salary charts).

Why / how it works

With comparatives (cheaper, more flexible, less secure), we cannot use very. Instead:

  • small difference → slightly, a bit, a little: 51% vs 49% → "slightly more common".
  • big difference → much, far, a lot, considerably, significantly: $450 vs $3,200 → "much lower".

Form: degree modifier + comparative adjective + than. Stress falls on the modifier and adjective: "Salaries are MUCH LOWer than…"; than is weak /ðən/.

too / enough / so / such: too shows a problem ("too rigid"), enough comes after an adjective ("flexible enough") but before a noun ("enough experience"), so + adjective, such + (a) adjective + noun.

Worked example 1

Data: Flexible workers 91% satisfied vs rigid workers 58%. → "Flexible workers are much / considerably happier than rigid workers." (Not "slightly" – the difference is large.)

Worked example 2

"Although this saves time, telecommuting can make teamwork ____ difficult unless managers communicate clearly." A) quite B) enough C) such → A: quite + adjective. (difficult enough would need "enough" after; "such" needs a noun).

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) "very better" → much better. 2) "enough experienced" → experienced enough. 3) "so a good idea" → such a good idea. 4) Choosing "slightly" for a big difference in a chart – check the numbers.

Exam corner

In Part 2 look for a comparative (-er / more / less) after the gap: the answer is much / far / slightly / considerably, never very. In Part 3 a writer's choice of degree adverb (extremely, slightly) can show their attitude.

Quick summary

  • slightly (small) ↔ much / far / considerably / significantly (large) + comparative.
  • very / extremely + normal adjective, not comparative.
  • adj + enough; too + adj; so + adj; such (a) + adj + noun.

Salaries in India ($450 a month) are ____ lower than in the US ($3,200 a month).

✅ الجواب: much

A large difference + comparative → much (not very).

Flexible hours (51%) are ____ more common than fixed hours (49%).

✅ الجواب: slightly

A small difference → slightly.

Telecommuting (20% high stress) is ____ less stressful than commuting (88% high stress).

✅ الجواب: significantly

A large difference → significantly / much / far.
