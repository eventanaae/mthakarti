Grammar 4: Non-defining Relative Clauses

📗 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 · G.4.2 (and G.4.1 defining) · R&W pp. 15–20

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

A non-defining relative clause adds extra information about a noun we can already identify. It sits between commas.

whosepossessionJeff, whose bike broke, asked his father for money.

that is possible; pronoun can be omitted if it is the objectnever "that"; pronoun can never be omitted
⚠️ After a comma, choose which (things) or who (people) – never that and never what: ❌ "telecommuting, that allows…" ❌ "telecommuting, what allows…"

The idea in detail

A relative clause (جملة الصلة / الوصل) gives information about a noun. There are two kinds:

  • Defining (محددة) – necessary to know which person or thing: "Advertisements that target teenagers should be controlled." Without the clause, the meaning changes.
  • Non-defining (غير محددة (معلومة إضافية)) – extra information; the noun is already clear (a name, "my father", a unique thing): "The Burj Khalifa, which is 828 metres tall, is in Dubai." You could remove the clause and the sentence still makes sense.

Why / how it works

The commas work like brackets: they show the information is optional. Because it is "extra", the rules are stricter: use who / which / whose / where / when, never that, and you can't drop the pronoun.

Which can also refer to a whole idea in the previous clause: "Many people now telecommute, which saves travel time." (which = the fact that people telecommute).

From the course texts (defining): those who like to dream bigger; a situation in which people cause a prediction to come true; a job that will provide security.

Worked example 1

"Some companies offer telecommuting, ____ allows employees to work from home for part of the week." A) that B) what C) which → C: comma + thing → which.

Worked example 2

Combine: "Mr Ahmed is our careers adviser. He has worked in five countries." → Mr Ahmed, who has worked in five countries, is our careers adviser.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) "that" after a comma. 2) Missing the second comma: "My sister, who is a nurse works at night" → …a nurse, works…. 3) Repeating the subject: "My father, who he is an engineer," → who is. 4) "which" for people: "the manager, which…" → who.

Exam corner

Look at the punctuation before the gap. Comma + gap → who / which / whose / where. Then check: person → who; thing or whole idea → which; "her/his/their" meaning → whose.

Quick summary

  • Non-defining = extra info between commas.
  • who (people), which (things/ideas), whose, where, when – no "that".
  • Defining = no commas, "that" allowed.

Some companies offer telecommuting, ____ allows employees to work from home.

✅ الجواب: which

Non-defining clause about a thing → which (never that after a comma).

My uncle, ____ runs a small business, raised capital from his family.

✅ الجواب: who

Non-defining clause about a person → who.

Jeff, ____ bike broke, had to ask his father for money.

✅ الجواب: whose

Whose shows possession (Jeff's bike).
