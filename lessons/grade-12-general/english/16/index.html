Part 3A Practice: 'Why Does Shopping Feel So Good?'

📗 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 3A · Money Talks · Consumerism and the human brain (B2.1, ±290 words)

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Practice text (original, expository, about 290 words)

[1] Every day, the average teenager sees hundreds of advertisements on screens, buildings and buses. We are all consumers, and the global economy depends on our spending. Yet many of the things we buy are not really necessary. So why do we keep buying?

[2] Brain scientists have found part of the answer. When we expect a reward, the brain releases dopamine, a chemical linked to pleasure and motivation. Surprisingly, the strongest feeling often comes before we buy – while we are looking at a product and imagining owning it. Once the item is ours, the excitement fades quickly, and the brain starts looking for the next reward.

[3] Marketers use this knowledge carefully. Shops play slow music so that customers walk more slowly and notice more products. Popular items are placed at eye level, and prices often end in .99 so that they seem lower than they are. In one well-known study, shoppers judged two identical pairs of trainers differently: the pair in a room that smelled of flowers was rated more highly.

[4] Our self-image matters too. Many people choose a brand not because it works better but because of what it says about them. A famous logo can make a buyer feel successful or accepted, and social media strengthens this effect, since people constantly compare their lives with others'.

[5] This does not mean that we are helpless. Understanding how marketing works is the first step towards controlling it. Writing a shopping list, waiting two days before an expensive purchase and asking "Do I need this, or do I just want the feeling?" can protect both our wallets and our wellbeing. Money can buy products, but it cannot buy lasting happiness.

Expect: 2 specific-information + 3 detail + 1 main point + 1 inference + 1 overall meaning (3 marks each). Paragraph map: 1 question → 2 brain/dopamine → 3 shop techniques → 4 self-image → 5 solutions.
⚠️ Paragraph 2: the strongest pleasure comes before buying, not after. Paragraph 5 gives the writer's own view.

The idea in detail

This practice text follows the exam theme Consumerism and the human brain. It is expository: it explains causes (the brain, marketing, self-image) and ends with practical advice. Key words: consumer (مستهلك), dopamine (الدوبامين), reward (مكافأة), fade (يتلاشى), identical (متطابق), self-image (صورة الذات), brand (علامة تجارية), wellbeing (الرفاهية / الصحة النفسية).

Worked example: avoiding a trap

"According to paragraph 2, when is the feeling of pleasure strongest?" A) after we pay B) while we imagine owning the product C) when we use the product for the first time → B. Option A is the opposite trap ("Once the item is ours, the excitement fades").

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Choosing "People should never buy brands" for overall meaning – too extreme; the writer only wants control. 2) Thinking the writer is against all shopping – paragraph 5 says "this does not mean we are helpless". 3) Confusing "identical" (the same) with "famous".

Exam corner

3A questions are worth 3 marks each – the highest in the exam. Spend about 25 minutes. For every answer, find the sentence that proves it.

Quick summary

  • Pleasure peaks before buying (dopamine).
  • Techniques: slow music, eye-level placement, .99 prices, scent.
  • Brands feed self-image; social media increases comparison.
  • Solution: awareness + list + wait + ask "need or feeling?"
