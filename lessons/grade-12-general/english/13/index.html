Grammar 7: Subordinating Conjunctions

Comma rule: subordinate clause FIRST → comma ("Although it was expensive, I bought it."). Subordinate clause SECOND → usually no comma ("I bought it although it was expensive."). whereas normally takes a comma in either position.
⚠️ Don't double up: ❌ "Although the store had a sale, but the jacket was expensive." ❌ "As the payment failed, so Tariq paid cash." despite / in spite of are prepositions (+ noun / -ing), not conjunctions: ❌ "Despite it was cheap" ✔ "Despite the low price" / "Although it was cheap".

The idea in detail

A subordinating conjunction (أداة ربط تبعية) joins a dependent clause (it cannot stand alone) to a main clause, and shows the relationship: contrast, reason, condition, time or purpose. "Although Zoe calculated everything three times" is not a full sentence; it needs "…, things didn't add up."

Why / how it works

  • although / even though (مع أن / رغم أن) – the main clause is a surprise: "Although Company A offers more money, I'd choose Company B."
  • whereas / while (بينما) – two different facts side by side: "The statement showed a withdrawal of $250, whereas they paid the movers $215."
  • since / as / because (لأن / بما أن) – reason: "I work harder since I feel a sense of pride."
  • unless (إلا إذا) – "if … not": "You can't make ends meet unless you budget" = "…if you don't budget".

Pronunciation: when the subordinate clause comes first, the voice rises slightly at the comma ↗, pauses, then falls ↘ at the end.

Worked example 1

Combine with whereas: "Salem saved half of his allowance. His brother spent everything on clothes." → Salem saved half of his allowance, whereas his brother spent everything on clothes.

Worked example 2

Error clinic: "As the card payment failed twice, so Tariq had to pay with cash." → remove so: "As the card payment failed twice, Tariq had to pay with cash."

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) although … but / because … so (double linker). 2) "unless you don't save" – unless already contains "not" → unless you save. 3) "Despite it was…" → Although it was…. 4) Missing comma when the subordinate clause comes first.

Exam corner

In Part 2 read BOTH clauses to see the relationship. Contrast + surprise → although; two facts compared → whereas/while; cause → since/as/because; "if not" → unless. In Part 3, conjunctions help you find the writer's real point: the idea after "although…," is usually the main point.

Quick summary

  • although (surprise) · whereas/while (contrast) · since/as/because (reason) · unless (if not).
  • Clause first → comma. Never although…but.
  • despite + noun/-ing (preposition).
