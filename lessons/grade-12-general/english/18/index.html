Part 4.1: Writing the Main Ideas (4–5 sentences)

📗 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 4.1 · 15 marks · Main Idea Writing Rubric G11A/12G

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Task: read a text of about 180 words that states facts and presents arguments. Write its main ideas in your own words in 4–5 sentences. (15 marks)

Rubric criterion (×5)For 5/5 you need…
Task CompletionThe main idea identified accurately and paraphrased; ideas from different parts combined (not retold in order); main points separated from minor details.
Language UseA range of simple and complex language, used flexibly and concisely in your own words; only occasional errors.
Organization & ClarityMain idea stated clearly and directly; points in a logical order, linked with connectors (because, also, however, as a result).
Recipe: S1 = topic + overall main idea · S2–S3 = the key supporting points / the two perspectives · S4 = the writer's conclusion · (S5 = result or link). No examples, numbers, quotations or personal opinion.
⚠️ A response wholly copied from the source text (or from AI tools) scores 0 in all criteria. Change words AND structure.

The idea in detail

Part 4.1 tests the outcome "summarise, paraphrase and synthesise" (يلخّص ويعيد الصياغة ويدمج). You are not retelling the text paragraph by paragraph; you are telling the reader what the text is mainly saying in a short, connected paragraph.

Practice text (original, about 180 words): "Should Teenagers Manage Their Own Money?"

Many parents now give their teenage children a bank card and a monthly allowance. Supporters of this idea believe that young people learn financial responsibility best through real experience. When teenagers have to make their allowance last a whole month, they quickly discover the value of budgeting and saving, and small mistakes at fifteen cost far less than big mistakes at twenty-five. However, critics worry that digital payments make spending feel unreal. With a card or a phone, money disappears with a single tap, and online shops are designed to encourage instant purchases. Some teenagers end the month broke and borrow from friends, which can create bad habits. Some banks now offer special teenage accounts that let parents see every payment and set daily spending limits, although these controls do not teach young people to make decisions on their own. Most experts agree that the answer lies somewhere in between. Teenagers can benefit from managing money if their parents first teach them basic skills, such as tracking expenses and setting saving goals, and then gradually give them more independence.

Model answer (5 sentences, own words)

The text discusses whether teenagers should be responsible for their own money. On the one hand, supporters argue that handling a monthly allowance teaches young people to plan and save, and that early mistakes are less costly than later ones. On the other hand, critics point out that cards and apps make spending too easy, so some teenagers run out of money and develop poor habits. Most experts therefore suggest a balanced approach. Parents should first teach key money skills and then allow their children more freedom step by step.

Why / how it works: paraphrasing tools

  • Synonyms: financial responsibility → planning and saving; disappears → is spent.
  • Word form: independence (n) → independently (adv) / more freedom.
  • Structure: active ↔ passive; two sentences → one with because / so / which.
  • Generalise: "a card or a phone" → digital payments; leave out "at fifteen / at twenty-five".
  • Reporting verbs: argue, claim, point out, suggest, believe, warn.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Copying whole sentences. 2) Adding "I think…" – the task asks for the text's ideas. 3) Including an example as if it were the main idea. 4) Writing 8–10 sentences or only 2. 5) Missing one of the two perspectives.

Exam corner

Time: about 20 minutes. Steps: (1) read and underline the topic sentence of each part; (2) cross out examples and numbers; (3) note the main point of each part in 3–4 words; (4) close the text and write from your notes; (5) count sentences and check connectors.

Quick summary

  • 4–5 sentences, own words, main ideas only.
  • Topic → perspective 1 → perspective 2 → conclusion.
  • Rubric: Task Completion, Language Use, Organization & Clarity (5 each).
