Vocabulary: A Picture of Health (2) and Stress

Handling stress (class vocabulary): calm down = become less worried or excited; negative effect = a harmful result; causes of stress = things that make a person feel worried or under pressure.

"Before the test, Sara took a deep breath to calm down. Too much stress can have a negative effect on sleep."
stress can be a noun (He is under stress) and a verb (Don't stress about it). Nervous describes a feeling; stress is the pressure causing it.
⚠️ Say an upset stomach (not "a stomach upset"), toothache is one word, and use a remedy FOR an illness.

The idea in detail

This lesson continues the theme A Picture of Health and adds words about stress. Learn each word with its meaning, its word class and its common partners (words that often go with it).

  • remedy (n): a treatment that helps cure or reduce an illness or a problem. A home remedy is a natural treatment you make at home, like warm water with honey for a sore throat. Do not confuse it with a prescription: a prescription is an official order from a doctor; a remedy can be any treatment.
  • nervous (adj): worried or a little afraid about something that will happen, for example an exam or a speech. Noun: nerves. People often say "I feel nervous about..."
  • stress (n and v): the feeling of too much pressure and worry. As a noun: "Exams cause stress." As a verb: "Don't stress yourself!" The adjective is stressed (a stressed student) or stressful (a stressful job).
  • toothache (n): pain in a tooth. It is one word. Other "ache" words: headache, stomachache, earache.
  • upset stomach (n phrase): when your stomach feels sick or uncomfortable, often after eating something bad or eating too fast.

Why it works: cause and effect

Stress is a good example of cause → effect. Cause: exams, deadlines, problems. Effect: feeling nervous, headaches, an upset stomach, poor sleep. The class vocabulary for coping with stress is also useful: calm down (become less worried), take a break, talk to someone, get enough sleep, exercise. In a gap-fill text, look for this chain of cause and effect to guess the missing word.

WordClassTypical sentence
remedynounRest is the best remedy for tiredness.
nervousadjectiveShe felt nervous before the interview.

toothachenounA toothache needs a dentist.

Example 1. "Before the exam, Mariam felt very ____ and her hands were shaking." Options: (nervous / remedy / toothache). The clues "before the exam" and "hands shaking" show worry → nervous (an adjective is needed after "felt very").
Example 2. "My grandmother says that mint tea is a good ____ for an upset stomach." The gap follows "a good" and needs a noun meaning a treatment → remedy.
Example 3 (word class check). "Exams can cause a lot of ____." A noun is needed after "a lot of"; "nervous" is an adjective, so the answer is stress.
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) Using nervous as a noun: "I have a nervous" is wrong; say "I feel nervous" or "I have nerves". (2) Writing "tooth ache" as two words. (3) Confusing stress (noun) with stressed (adjective): "I am stress" is wrong; say "I am stressed". (4) Using remedy for a person; a remedy is a treatment, not a doctor.
Exam corner: These words appear in Part 1 gap-fill (2 marks per gap) and in the writing theme "Talking about Stress". In Part 1, decide the word class first (adjective after "felt", noun after "a/the/much"), then test the meaning. In the essay, using words such as stress, nervous, calm down and remedy accurately shows a wide vocabulary and earns better marks.

Quick summary

  • remedy = treatment or cure; nervous = worried; stress = pressure and worry.
  • toothache = tooth pain (one word); upset stomach = sick, uncomfortable stomach.
  • Check the word class: nervous (adj), stress (n/v), remedy (n).
  • Stress words help in both Part 1 and the writing task.
