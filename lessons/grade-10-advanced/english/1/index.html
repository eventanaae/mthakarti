Exam Guide: Format, Marks & Strategies

🌦️ English🎓 الصف العاشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: EOT Spec pp. 19-23 - Parts 1, 2, 3A, 3B, 4 (100 marks)

The idea in detail

The End-of-Term 1 exam is a 100-mark test in two big skills: Reading & Viewing (60 marks) and Writing & Representing (40 marks). Knowing the shape of the paper is the first step to a high mark, because you can plan your time and your effort before you start. The exam lasts about 120 minutes.

Part by part

  • Part 1 - Vocabulary (10 marks). A short text of about 160 words with 5 gaps. For each gap you choose A, B or C. Each gap is worth 2 marks. The words come from two themes: A Picture of Health and Infotainment.
  • Part 2 - Grammar (10 marks). A 160-word news report about an airplane crash with 5 gaps (A/B/C, 2 marks each). The gaps test the seven grammar topics in this course.
  • Part 3A - Reading (24 marks). A text of 270-300 words about Longevity, Medical advice and the Internet. There are 8 questions, 3 marks each. This is the "at grade level" text.
  • Part 3B - Reading (16 marks). A harder text of 270-300 words about Commercials, Visual Media and language. There are 8 questions, 2 marks each. This is the "beyond grade level" text.
  • Part 4 - Writing (40 marks). Task 1.1: write the main ideas of a 170-word text in 3-4 sentences (15 marks). Task 1.2: write an opinion essay of at least 170 words that answers three prompts (25 marks).

Why the marks matter for your plan

Reading is 40 of the 100 marks, and Part 3A alone is 24 marks. Writing is 40 marks, and the essay (25 marks) is the biggest single task. So: do not waste time on a 2-mark question while a 25-mark essay is waiting. A good time plan for 120 minutes is below.

The S-S-C reading strategy

Skim = read fast to get the topic and the main idea. Scan = look for key words, names, numbers and facts that match the question. Check = go back to the text and prove your answer with evidence. Think of it like finding a shop in Dubai Mall: first look at the map (skim), then walk to the right floor (scan), then confirm the shop name on the door (check).

Worked example (gap-fill thinking). "The doctor wrote a ____ for the medicine." Options: A description B prescription C subscription.
Step 1: the gap needs a noun after "a". Step 2: the sentence is about a doctor and medicine. Step 3: a prescription is a doctor's written order for medicine. Answer: B.
Another: "She ____ been studying since 7 a.m." Options: A have B has C is. Subject = she, and "been studying" needs have/has; with she we use has. Answer: B.
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) leaving a gap empty (with three options, a guess has a chance to score, a blank scores nothing); (2) spending too long on the 2-mark questions in 3B; (3) choosing an answer because it is "true in real life" and not because the text says it; (4) writing less than 170 words in the essay; (5) running out of time before Part 4.
Exam corner: This lesson matches the "Exam Guide" outline item (EOT Spec pp. 19-23). You will not get a question about it directly, but every question in the paper follows these formats. To get full marks: read the whole sentence before choosing, check subject + tense + meaning for grammar gaps, support every reading answer with a line from the text, and leave time to count words in the essay.

Quick summary

  • Total 100 marks: Reading 60 + Writing 40.
  • Parts 1 and 2: 5 gaps each, 2 marks per gap.
  • 3A = 8 questions x 3 marks; 3B = 8 questions x 2 marks.
  • Writing = 15 marks (main ideas) + 25 marks (essay, 170+ words).
  • Use Skim - Scan - Check, and answer every question.
