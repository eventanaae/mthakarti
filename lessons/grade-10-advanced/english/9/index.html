Grammar: Participle Clauses

🌦️ English🎓 الصف العاشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 Grammar - G.4.5 Clauses and phrases (participle clauses)

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

A participle clause shortens a sentence by using a participle (-ing or -ed) instead of a full clause. The subject of the participle must be the same as the subject of the main clause.

TypeMeaningExample
-ing (present participle)active; actions at the same time or one after anotherHearing the noise, the witness ran outside. (= When she heard the noise...)
-ing in relative clausereplaces who/which + active verbThe man standing near the door saw everything. (= who was standing)
-ed (past participle)passive meaningThe passengers injured in the crash were taken to hospital. (= who were injured)
Having + V3an earlier actionHaving landed safely, the pilot thanked the crew.
Full: "The plane, which was carrying 120 passengers, landed safely." Reduced: "The plane, carrying 120 passengers, landed safely."
⚠️ Dangling participle: "Walking down the street, the plane crashed" (the plane was not walking!). The subject of the main clause must be the one doing the participle action. Active meaning = -ing; passive meaning = -ed.

The idea in detail

A participle clause is a shorter way to write a longer clause. Instead of a full clause with a subject and a verb, we use a participle: the -ing form (present participle) or the -ed / V3 form (past participle). Writers use participle clauses to make sentences shorter, smoother and more formal. News reports use them all the time.

Golden rule: the person or thing that does the participle action must be the same as the subject of the main clause.

The four types

TypeMeaningFull → short
-ing (active, same time or one after another)when / while / because + active verbWhen she heard the noise, the witness ran out. → Hearing the noise, the witness ran out.
-ing in a relative clausewho/which + active verbThe man who was standing near the door saw everything. → The man standing near the door saw everything.
-ed (passive meaning)who/which + was/were + V3The passengers who were injured were taken to hospital. → The passengers injured in the crash were taken to hospital.
Having + V3 (earlier action)After + had + V3After the pilot had landed safely, he thanked the crew. → Having landed safely, the pilot thanked the crew.

How to decide between -ing and -ed

Ask: "Does the noun do the action, or does it receive it?" The plane carrying 120 passengers (the plane carries: active → -ing). The passengers injured in the crash (the passengers are injured by something: passive → -ed).

Example 1. Join with a participle clause: "The pilot saw smoke. He decided to land." Same subject (the pilot) → Seeing smoke, the pilot decided to land.
Example 2. Reduce: "The building, which was damaged by the storm, is closed." Passive relative clause → The building, damaged by the storm, is closed.
Example 3 (gap-fill). "The passengers ____ in the crash were taken to hospital." Options: A injuring B injured C injure. The passengers receive the harm → past participle → injured. Answer: B.
Example 4. "____ the match, the team celebrated." (win - an earlier action done by the team) → Having won the match, the team celebrated.
⚠️ Dangling participle. "Walking down the street, the plane crashed." Wrong: the plane was not walking! The subject of the main clause must be the one who does the participle action: "Walking down the street, I saw the plane crash." Other mistakes: using -ing for a passive idea ("the passengers injuring"), and forgetting the comma after an introductory participle clause.
Exam corner: G.4.5 Clauses and phrases (participle clauses), Part 2. Gap options will usually be the same verb in different forms (carrying / carried / carry). Decide active or passive, then pick -ing or -ed. Also check the time: an earlier action may need "having + V3".

Quick summary

  • Participle clause = a shortened clause using -ing or -ed.
  • -ing = active; -ed = passive; having + V3 = earlier action.
  • Same subject for both parts of the sentence.
  • Avoid dangling participles.
