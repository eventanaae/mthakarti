Vocabulary: Infotainment (2)

🌦️ English🎓 الصف العاشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 1 Vocabulary - informative, witness, investigation, injuries, scene

Word families: inform - information - informative; investigate (v) - investigation (n); injure (v) - injury (n) - injuries (plural: y becomes ies).
News report: "A ____ saw the plane land. Rescue teams reached the ____ within minutes, and there was an ____ into the cause." = witness, scene, investigation.
⚠️ scene (place of an event) sounds like seen. A witness sees; the scene is the place. Injuries = harm; damage is for things.

The idea in detail

These five words are very common in news reports, which is why they are useful for Part 1 and Part 2. Imagine a short news story about an accident: a witness saw it, the rescue teams arrived at the scene, some people had injuries, and an investigation began. A good report is informative.

  • informative (adj): giving useful facts and information. Family: inform (verb) → information (noun) → informative (adjective).
  • witness (n and v): as a noun, a person who sees an event and can tell others what happened. As a verb, to see something happen: "Many people witnessed the accident."
  • investigation (n): an official, careful search for the facts about an event. The verb is investigate. The police or experts carry it out.
  • injuries (n, plural): harm to a person's body, such as a broken arm. Singular: injury. Spelling rule: consonant + y becomes ies in the plural. The verb is injure; injured is an adjective (an injured passenger).
  • scene (n): the place where something happens, such as "the scene of the accident". It sounds like seen.

Why / how: the logic of a news story

Think in order: 1) an event happens at a scene; 2) a witness reports it; 3) people may have injuries; 4) an investigation finds the cause. When you see a gap in a news text, ask "Which step of the story am I in?" That gives the answer. Note: injuries are for people; damage is for things (a car has damage, a passenger has injuries).

VerbNounAdjective

Example 1. "A ____ told the police that the car was moving very fast." The gap needs a person who saw something → witness.
Example 2. "Rescue teams reached the ____ in ten minutes, and the passengers' ____ were treated at once." Gap 1: place of the event → scene. Gap 2: plural noun, harm to people → injuries.
Example 3. "The documentary was so ____ that I learned many new facts." After "so" we need an adjective about giving facts → informative.
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) scene / seen: "the seen of the accident" is wrong. (2) injuries not "injurys". (3) Using investigation where an adjective is needed. (4) Writing "an informative" with the wrong article: say "an informative report" (vowel sound = an). (5) Using injuries for objects; use damage.
Exam corner: These words appear in Part 1 (5 gaps, 2 marks each) and again in the Part 2 news report about an airplane crash, where they are context words. Read the sentence before and after the gap: if the text says "police began..." the missing word is probably investigation; if "people were hurt", think injuries.

Quick summary

  • witness = person who sees an event; scene = place of an event.
  • investigation = official search for facts; injuries = harm to people.
  • informative = giving useful information.
  • Follow the logic of a news story to choose the answer.
