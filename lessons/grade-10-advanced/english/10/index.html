Grammar: Demonstratives (this, that, these, those)

  • this / these: things close to the speaker in place, time or thought: This morning, I read these reports.
  • that / those: things far away or in the past: Those passengers were lucky. That day, nobody was seriously hurt.
  • As pronouns: This is my prescription. Those are the witnesses.
  • Referring back to an idea: The plane crashed. This caused panic. / That was a shock.
Pointing at a pile of reports on your desk: "These are the reports." Pointing at buildings across the street: "Those are the offices."
The demonstrative must agree in number with the noun: this/that + singular, these/those + plural.
⚠️ "these problem" and "this books" are wrong. Also: these days = nowadays, but those days = in the past.

The idea in detail

Demonstratives are the words this, that, these, those. They point to things and show distance (near or far) and number (one or many). They can be determiners (before a noun: this book) or pronouns (alone: This is my book).

How they work

  • Place: this/these are near the speaker (in your hand, on your desk); that/those are far (across the room, across the street).
  • Time: this morning, these days (present or very recent); that day, those days (the past, or a time away from now).
  • Referring back to an idea: "The plane crashed. This caused panic." / "That was a shock." We often use this for something we have just said and that for a reaction or comment.
  • On the phone: "Hello, this is Maryam" (not "that is").

Think of daily life in the UAE: in a shop, you point at the juice in your hand: "This one, please." You point at the shelf far away: "That one." For many items near you: "These dates are delicious." Many items far away: "Those towers are very tall."

Example 1. "(This / These) injuries are serious." The noun injuries is plural and the speaker sees them near → These.
Example 2. "A witness saw the crash. (This / That) helped the investigation." Referring to the idea just mentioned → This (the idea has just been mentioned, so it feels close).
Example 3. "Do you remember (that / those) day when it rained all morning?" A single day in the past → that.
Example 4. "(Those / That) passengers were lucky." The noun is plural and far in time → Those.
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) Number mismatch: "these problem" ✗ → "this problem" or "these problems" ✓; "this books" ✗ → "these books" ✓. (2) Mixing these days (nowadays) with those days (in the past). (3) Writing those with the wrong spelling ("thoes"). (4) Using a demonstrative when an article is better: "I saw this accident yesterday" is odd if the listener does not know it; "an accident" is better.
Exam corner: G.7.4 Determiners (demonstratives), Part 2. Ask two questions: (1) is the noun singular or plural? (2) is it near/present or far/past? In the news-report text, a sentence beginning "___ was a frightening day" is a pointer to the earlier events, so That is right (singular verb "was" + the past).

Quick summary

  • this/these = near; that/those = far.
  • this/that + singular; these/those + plural.
  • They can be determiners or pronouns.
  • these days = now; those days = long ago.
