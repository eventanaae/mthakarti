Grammar: Subordinating Conjunctions

🌦️ English🎓 الصف العاشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 Grammar - G.6.2 Conjunctions (subordinating)

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

A subordinating conjunction joins a dependent clause to a main clause. The dependent clause cannot stand alone.

MeaningConjunctionsExample
Contrastalthough, even though, while, whereasAlthough he was injured, he helped others.
Reasonbecause, since, asWe stayed inside because it was raining.
Timewhen, while, after, before, as soon as, untilAs soon as the plane landed, the crew helped passengers.
Conditionif, unlessUnless you rest, you won't recover.
Purposeso thatHe wrote it down so that he wouldn't forget.
If the dependent clause comes first, use a comma: Although it was late, we kept working. If it comes second, usually no comma.
"Although the passengers were frightened, no one panicked." (contrast)
⚠️ Do not use although and but together: "Although he was tired, but he continued" is wrong. Do not confuse because (conjunction + clause) with because of (+ noun). Despite / in spite of are followed by a noun or -ing, not a clause.

The idea in detail

A sentence can have a main clause (it can stand alone) and a dependent (subordinate) clause (it cannot stand alone). A subordinating conjunction is the linking word that starts the dependent clause and joins it to the main clause. "Although he was injured" is not a complete sentence; it needs a main clause: "Although he was injured, he helped others." (أدوات الربط التابعة)

MeaningConjunctionsExample
Contrastalthough, even though, while, whereasAlthough it was windy, the plane landed safely.
Reasonbecause, since, asWe stayed in because it was very hot.

How to choose the right one

Do not guess; ask what the relationship between the two ideas is. (1) Do they go against each other? → contrast (although). (2) Is the second idea the reason? → because/since. (3) Is it about when? → when/after/before. (4) Is it "if" something happens? → if/unless. (5) Is it the aim? → so that.

Punctuation: when the dependent clause comes first, put a comma after it: Although it was late, we kept working. When it comes second, usually no comma: We kept working although it was late.

Example 1. "____ the passengers were frightened, nobody panicked." Two ideas that contrast (frightened vs. no panic) → Although.
Example 2. "The match was cancelled ____ the weather was terrible." The second idea is the reason → because.
Example 3. "I will call you ____ I arrive at the hospital." A time relationship → when / as soon as.
Example 4. "You will miss the bus ____ you hurry." If you do NOT hurry → unless.
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) Double connectors: "Although he was tired, but he continued" ✗. Use one: "Although he was tired, he continued." (2) because + clause but because of + noun: "because of the rain" ✓, "because of it rained" ✗. (3) despite / in spite of are followed by a noun or -ing, not a clause: "Despite the rain" ✓; "Despite it rained" ✗. (4) Missing comma after a first dependent clause. (5) Using a fragment: "Because it was raining." is not a complete sentence alone.
Exam corner: G.6.2 Conjunctions (subordinating), Part 2. In the news report, a gap such as "___ the weather was bad, the pilot managed to land safely" needs a contrast word: Although. Read both clauses, name the relationship, then match it with the conjunction.

Quick summary

  • Subordinating conjunction + clause = dependent clause.
  • Contrast: although; reason: because; time: when/after; condition: if/unless; purpose: so that.
  • First clause dependent → use a comma.
  • Never use although and but together.
