Gap: "She went to the doctor because she had several ____ (A symptoms B remedies C scenes)." The best answer is symptoms: it fits the meaning (signs of illness).
⚠️ Do not confuse prescription (written order for medicine) with description, and symptoms (signs of illness) with symbols.

The idea in detail

Part 1 of the exam tests whether you know the meaning and the correct use of key words. For the theme A Picture of Health (a "picture" of health means a general view of how healthy people are) there are five core words. For each word you must know three things: the meaning, the word class (noun, adjective, verb) and how it is used in a sentence.

The five words, explained

  • symptoms (n, plural; singular symptom): the signs that show you are ill, such as a fever, a cough or a headache. Symptoms are what you feel or notice. The doctor then finds the illness.
  • prescription (n): a paper (or electronic note) from a doctor that tells the pharmacist which medicine to give you. The verb is prescribe. (وصفة طبية)
  • fraudulent (adj): done in a dishonest way to cheat people, usually to get money. A fraudulent website might sell a "miracle cure" that does not work. The noun is fraud.
  • dishonest (adj): not telling the truth. The prefix dis- means "not", so dishonest = not honest. A dishonest person lies or cheats.
  • syndrome (n): a group of symptoms that often happen together and describe one medical condition.

Why do these words belong together?

They tell one story: a person has symptoms → they may have a syndrome → the doctor writes a prescription. On the internet, however, a dishonest seller may offer a fraudulent cure. When you meet them in a gap-fill text, ask: is the sentence about illness, medicine, or cheating? That meaning clue will pick the right option.

Example 1. "After a week of coughing, Ali visited the clinic. The doctor listed his ____ and then wrote a ____ for cough medicine." Options: (symptoms / prescription / syndrome).
Step 1: the first gap follows "listed his" and needs a plural noun: signs of illness → symptoms. Step 2: the second gap follows "wrote a" and the object is medicine → prescription.
Example 2. "The advert promised to cure every illness in one day, but it was ____ and only wanted people's money." Clue: "only wanted people's money" and "promised everything" → fraudulent (or dishonest if listed alone; if both are options, fraudulent is stronger because it is about cheating for money).
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) prescription / description look alike but mean different things. (2) symptoms / symbols: symbols are signs like the "+" sign. (3) Writing symptom (singular) when the sentence needs the plural symptoms. (4) Mixing the adjective fraudulent with the noun fraud: "a fraud website" is wrong; "a fraudulent website" is right. (5) Spelling: prescription has "scr", syndrome has "y".
Exam corner: Part 1 (Vocabulary) gives a 160-word text with 5 gaps, A/B/C, 2 marks each. Method: skim the text, decide the word class of each gap, test each option in the whole sentence, then re-read the finished text. Full marks come from using both meaning and grammar: only one option will fit both.

Quick summary

  • symptoms = signs of illness; syndrome = group of symptoms.
  • prescription = doctor's written order for medicine (verb: prescribe).
  • fraudulent = cheating for money (noun: fraud); dishonest = not honest.
  • Use meaning clues around the gap, then check the word class.
