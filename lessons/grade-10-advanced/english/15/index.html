Reading Skills: Question Types for Parts 3A and 3B

🌦️ English🎓 الصف العاشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 3A / 3B - ENG.07.RV.CS.1.1, 2.1, 3.1, 4.1, 4.2, 5.1

S-S-C: Skim for the main idea → Scan for key words from the question → Check your answer in the text.
  • Specific information / details: find the key words of the question in the text; the answer is near them, often paraphrased.
  • Vocabulary in context: read the sentence before and after; replace the word with each option.
  • Inference: combine clues; the best answer is supported by the text, not just possible.
  • Overall meaning: choose the option covering the whole text, not one paragraph.
⚠️ A wrong option is often true in real life but not in the text, or uses the same words with a different meaning. Always find evidence.

The idea in detail

In Parts 3A and 3B you read an expository text (a text that explains or informs) and answer 8 multiple-choice questions. Every question belongs to a type, and each type needs a different way of thinking. If you know the type, you know how to find the answer.

TypeWhat it asksHow to answer
Specific information (CS.2.1)One fact: name, number, date, placeScan for the key word or number in the question; the answer is next to it.
Details (CS.5.1)Supporting facts, reasons, examplesFind the paragraph; the text may use different words (paraphrase).
Word meaning (CS.4.1)Meaning of a word in contextRead the sentence before and after; use word parts; test each option in the sentence.
Inference / prediction (CS.4.2)What the text suggests but does not sayCombine clues; choose the option supported by the text.
Connections (CS.3.1)Link ideas, viewpoints or your own experienceCompare viewpoints; look for contrast words (whereas, critics, supporters).
Overall meaning (CS.1.1)Main idea of the whole textChoose the option that covers the whole text, not one paragraph.

Distribution: 3A has 3 specific-information, 3 details, 1 word meaning and 1 overall question. 3B has 4 details, 1 word meaning, 1 inference/prediction, 1 connection and 1 overall question.

S-S-C in action

  1. Skim the text (title, first sentence of each paragraph) to get the main idea.
  2. Scan: take the key words from the question and find them in the text. The correct option is often a paraphrase (same meaning, different words).
  3. Check: put your finger on the line that proves your answer.
Worked example (details). Text line: "Many students sleep badly because they use phones late at night." Question: "According to the text, why do many students sleep badly?" A They drink too much tea. B They use phones late. C They study too early.
Key words: sleep badly → the sentence gives "because they use phones late at night". Option B is a paraphrase. A and C are not in the text. Answer: B.
Worked example (word meaning). "Some websites are unreliable; they often publish false information." Question: "unreliable" means: A new B cannot be trusted C very popular. The clue "false information" and the prefix un- (not) + reliable (can be trusted) → B.
Worked example (overall). A text that explains why exercise helps the body, the mind and sleep has the main idea "Exercise has many health benefits", not "Exercise helps sleep" (too small).
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) Choosing an option that is true in real life but is not in the text. (2) Choosing an option that uses the same words as the text but changes the meaning. (3) Answering "overall meaning" with a detail from one paragraph. (4) Not reading the words NOT, EXCEPT or "according to the text". (5) Choosing extreme answers (always, never, all) without text support.
Exam corner: Codes: ENG.07.RV.CS.1.1, 2.1, 3.1, 4.1, 4.2, 5.1. In 3A each question is 3 marks, in 3B 2 marks. Full marks come from evidence: for each question you should be able to point to the sentence in the text that proves the answer. Do the easy specific-information questions first, then the harder ones.

Quick summary

  • Six types: specific info, details, word meaning, inference, connections, overall meaning.
  • Skim - Scan - Check.
  • Correct options are often paraphrases of the text.
  • Always find evidence in the text; do not use general knowledge.
