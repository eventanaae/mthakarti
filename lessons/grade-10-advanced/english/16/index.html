Reading 3A: Longevity (Living a Long Life)

Longevity = long life. Part 3A uses a 270-300-word expository text. Practise with this model text:

Model text (shortened): "In recent decades, average life expectancy has been rising. In 1900, many people lived to about 50; today, in many countries, it is over 80. Researchers say the main reasons are better healthcare, clean water and safer food. However, scientists in the 'Blue Zones', regions where many people live past 100, also point to lifestyle. People there eat mostly plants, walk daily, stay close to family, and have a sense of purpose. Experts warn that longevity is not only about adding years but about adding healthy years. Although genes play a part, studies show that lifestyle choices influence health more than inheritance."

The idea in detail

Longevity means a long life. Part 3A uses expository texts (they explain facts and ideas) of 270-300 words. This lesson explains the content of the topic and shows you how to read a text about it step by step.

Key ideas of the topic

  • Life expectancy = the average number of years a person is expected to live. It has been rising: about 50 in 1900 to over 80 today in many countries (as in the model text).
  • Reasons for longer life: better healthcare, clean water, safer food.
  • Blue Zones: regions where many people live past 100. Common habits: eating mostly plants, walking every day, staying close to family and having a sense of purpose.
  • Healthy years: longevity is not only about adding years to life but also about adding life to years, meaning healthy years.
  • Genes and lifestyle: genes (what you inherit from your parents) play a part, but lifestyle choices influence health more, according to the studies in the text.

How to read an expository text

Expository texts usually have: an introduction (the topic and a fact), a body (reasons, examples, expert opinions) and a conclusion (the main message). Words like however, although, also, but show where the writer changes direction. In the Longevity text, "however" signals a new point: lifestyle in the Blue Zones.

Example 1 (NOT question). "According to the text, all of the following help people live longer EXCEPT: A clean water B a sense of purpose C eating mostly meat D safer food." The text says Blue Zone people eat mostly plants, so C is the exception; A, B and D are all mentioned.
Example 2 (inference). "Experts warn that longevity is not only about adding years but about adding healthy years." What does this suggest? A Living long is the only goal. B Quality of life matters as well as length. C Healthcare is not useful. The sentence compares years with healthy years → B.
Example 3 (cause). "Why has life expectancy risen?" Look for reason signals: "Researchers say the main reasons are..." → better healthcare, clean water and safer food.
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) Using your own knowledge: a "NOT/EXCEPT" option may be true in real life but be mentioned in the text, or the opposite. (2) Confusing "life expectancy" with "longevity" in a detail. (3) Choosing a title that covers one paragraph only. (4) Ignoring "although/however", which change the meaning of a sentence.
Exam corner: Part 3A theme: A Picture of Health | Longevity. 8 questions x 3 marks. Typical set: 3 specific information, 3 details, 1 word meaning, 1 overall meaning. For full marks use S-S-C and write nothing from memory: every answer must have evidence in the text. Topic words to know: life expectancy, lifestyle, genes, inheritance, diet, healthcare, influence, regions.

Quick summary

  • Longevity = long life; life expectancy has risen thanks to healthcare, clean water, safe food.
  • Blue Zones: plant diet, daily walking, family, purpose.
  • Healthy years matter, not only more years.
  • Lifestyle influences health more than genes (in the text).
