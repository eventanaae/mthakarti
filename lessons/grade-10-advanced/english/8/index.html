Grammar: Present Perfect Continuous

⚠️ 1) Use been + -ing, never "have being". 2) for (length of time) vs since (starting point). 3) Stative verbs (know, like, own) are not used in the continuous: I have known him for years. 4) With he/she/it use has.

The idea in detail

The present perfect continuous connects the past with the present. It shows an action that started in the past and is still going on, or that has just stopped and we can see the result now. The focus is on the activity and its duration, not on how many times or on the final result.

Form: subject + have/has + been + verb-ing. With I, you, we, they use have; with he, she, it use has.

  • Negative: She hasn't been sleeping well.
  • Question: How long have you been waiting?

Two main uses

  1. Still continuing: "Rescue teams have been working since 6 a.m." (they started at 6 and are still working).
  2. Just stopped, with a present result: "My eyes hurt because I have been looking at a screen all day." (the looking may have stopped, but the result is visible).

Example 1. "Police ____ (investigate) the crash since Monday." Subject = police (plural), "since Monday" = starting point still continuing → have been investigating.
Example 2. "Salem is sweaty and out of breath. He ____ (run)." The result is visible now → has been running.
Example 3 (choose for or since). "The injured passengers have been waiting ____ three hours." Three hours is a length → for. If it were "____ 2 p.m." we would use since.
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) "have being" is wrong; the form is been + -ing. (2) Mixing for (length) and since (start point). (3) Stative verbs (know, like, own, believe, want) are not used in the continuous: say "I have known him for years", not "I have been knowing him". (4) Using have with he/she/it: "She have been waiting" ✗, "She has been waiting" ✓. (5) Using a finished past time word (yesterday, last week) with this tense; use the simple past instead.
Exam corner: G.13.4 Present time (present perfect continuous) in Part 2. In the news-report gap-fill, look for since, for, all morning and the idea "started earlier and still going on". Typical correct option: "have been working / has been raining". Check three things: the auxiliary (have/has), been, and the -ing form.

Quick summary

  • have/has + been + verb-ing.
  • Started in the past, still continuing, or just stopped with a result.
  • for = length of time; since = starting point.
  • No continuous with stative verbs (know, like, own).
