Functional Language: Asking for and Giving Advice

🌦️ English🎓 الصف العاشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 4 Writing - FL.24 Asking for and giving advice

The idea in detail

Functional language means "language for a job": asking for advice, giving advice, comparing and so on. In the writing task and in the class activities you need to ask for and give advice clearly and politely. Advice is a suggestion about what someone should do. The topic often appears with the theme of stress and health.

FunctionPhrases
Asking for adviceWhat should I do? / What would you advise me to do? / Could you give me some advice about...? / Do you think I should...? / What do you suggest?
Giving advice (normal)You should + verb / If I were you, I would + verb / Why don't you + verb? / It's a good idea to + verb / Try + -ing
Giving advice (strong)You'd better (not) + verb / I strongly advise you to + verb
Giving advice (polite/soft)Maybe you could... / Have you thought about + -ing? / I suggest (that) you + verb

The structures, one by one

  • should / shouldn't + base verb: "You should drink more water."
  • had better (not) + base verb: stronger, warns of a bad result: "You'd better leave now, or you'll be late."
  • If I were you, I would + base verb: gives a personal suggestion. Use were for all persons in this phrase.
  • Why don't you + base verb?: friendly suggestion.
  • I suggest (that) you + base verb / -ing: "I suggest that you rest" or "I suggest resting".

Tip: good advice has a reason: "You should exercise because it reduces stress."

Dialogue (stress).
Hessa: I feel nervous because I have three exams next week. What should I do?
Noor: If I were you, I would make a study plan. You should take short breaks, and you 'd better not stay up all night, because sleep helps memory. Why don't you talk to your teacher about the topics you find difficult?
Analysis: Hessa uses the question "What should I do?" (asking for advice). Noor uses four different structures (if I were you, should, had better not, why don't you) and gives reasons.
Example in writing: "To reduce stress, I suggest that students sleep early, exercise regularly and talk to someone they trust."
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) "You should to go" ✗ → "You should go" ✓ (no "to" after should or had better). (2) "If I was you" is informal; write "If I were you". (3) "I suggest you to rest" ✗ → "I suggest you rest / I suggest resting". (4) "You had better to go" ✗. (5) Sounding rude: "You must do this" is a command, not advice; use "should" or "Maybe you could".
Exam corner: Part 4 Writing, FL.24 Asking for and giving advice. In the essay you often write a solution prompt ("suggest how to..."). Using "I suggest that...", "It's a good idea to..." or "Students should..." in your essay shows range and answers the prompt directly. In the exam, model such sentences in your conclusion or body paragraph, always with a reason.

Quick summary

  • Ask: What should I do? / What do you suggest?
  • Give: should, had better, If I were you, Why don't you, I suggest.
  • After should / had better use the base verb (no to).
  • Add a reason to make advice strong.
