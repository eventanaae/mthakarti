Vocabulary Strategies: Gap-Fill, Context and Word Parts

The idea in detail

Part 1 gives you a text with five gaps. The best students do not simply "feel" the answer; they follow a method. This lesson gives you a complete method and shows you how to use context and word parts.

The 4-step method

  1. Skim the whole text first so you know the topic (health? media?).
  2. For each gap decide the word class: does it need a noun, an adjective, a verb? Look at the words around it: after a/an/the a noun or adjective is likely; after is/was/felt an adjective is likely; after to a verb is likely.
  3. Test every option in the whole sentence. Do not stop at the first one that "sounds right".
  4. Re-read the finished text to check that it makes sense.

Context clues: what to look for

Clue typeHow it helpsExample
Definition / synonymThe meaning is given near the gap"She felt nervous, worried about her speech."
Contrast (but, however, although)The missing word is the opposite"The first advert was boring, but the second was memorable."
Cause and result (so, because)The cause explains the missing word"He was injured, so he needed medical help."
ExampleThe example shows the meaning"Remedies, for example honey and lemon, ease a cough."

Word parts: prefixes and suffixes

A prefix comes at the start of a word and a suffix at the end. Knowing them helps you work out new words.

PartMeaning / jobExamples
dis-, un-, in-not, oppositedishonest, unhealthy, incorrect
-able, -ibleable to bememorable, visible
-iveadjective: tending toinformative, productive
-ity, -tion, -mentnoun endingsconnectivity, investigation, treatment
Worked example. Text: "Many websites sell health products. Some are honest, but others are ____ and try to cheat customers. People who follow their advice may get worse." Options: A dishonest B informative C memorable.
Step 1: the gap is after "are", so we need an adjective. All three are adjectives. Step 2: the contrast word but tells us the missing word is the opposite of honest. Step 3: "dis-" means not, so dishonest = not honest. "Informative" and "memorable" do not describe cheating. Step 4: re-read: "others are dishonest and try to cheat customers" makes sense. Answer: A.
Second example. "The speaker was so ____ that the audience listened to every word and learned a lot." Clue: "learned a lot" → informative.
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) Choosing a word only because you recognise it. Two options may be real English words, but only one fits the grammar and the meaning. (2) Ignoring the word after the gap. (3) Not reading the sentence before the gap: the clue is often there. (4) Leaving a gap blank.

Quick summary

  • Skim → decide word class → test all options → re-read.
  • Look for definitions, contrast, cause-result and example clues.
  • dis-/un-/in- = not; -able = able to be; -ive = adjective; -ity/-tion/-ment = noun.
  • Never choose a word just because you know it.
