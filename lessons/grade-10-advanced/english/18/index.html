The idea in detail

Part 3B is the beyond grade level text (about B2.2). It is 270-300 words, with 8 questions worth 2 marks each: 4 details, 1 word in context, 1 inference/prediction, 1 connection and 1 overall meaning. The theme is Infotainment: how commercials use pictures, sounds and language to influence us.

Content of the topic: how commercials work

  • A short commercial (for example 30 seconds) must grab attention fast.
  • Advertisers combine images, music and words.
  • Slogans are short and memorable, and they are repeated until they "stick" in the mind.
  • Visual techniques: bright colours and smiling faces make us feel positive.
  • Language techniques: phrases like "limited offer" create urgency (a feeling that you must act quickly). Emotive language appeals to feelings rather than facts.
  • Two viewpoints: critics say adverts can persuade people to buy things they do not need; supporters say they simply inform consumers about choices.
  • Critical consumers understand the techniques and question what they see.

Skills for the harder questions

Inference means reading "between the lines": the text does not say it directly, but the clues lead to it. Example: the text says "limited offer" creates urgency. Why would an advertiser use it? To make viewers buy quickly. That is an inference supported by the text. Connection means linking the text to other viewpoints or to your own experience: contrast words such as whereas, while, critics, supporters show two opinions, and you may be asked to compare them or relate them to adverts you know.

Example 1 (inference). "Which statement would the writer most probably agree with?" A Commercials never give useful information. B Viewers who understand advertising techniques make wiser choices. C All commercials are dishonest. The text ends by saying that understanding the techniques helps viewers become critical consumers → B. A and C are extreme.
Example 2 (connection). "Which view do critics hold?" Critics argue adverts can persuade people to buy things they do not need. A supporter's view is the opposite: adverts inform consumers about choices. The contrast word whereas marks the switch.
Example 3 (overall). Do not choose "Slogans are repeated" (a detail); the whole text covers images, music, slogans, language and viewpoints.
⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) Inference answers must be supported by the text; avoid extreme words such as always, never, all, nobody. (2) Mixing up the critics' view and the supporters' view. (3) Spending too long: each question is only 2 marks. (4) Treating a detail as the main idea.
Exam corner: Part 3B theme: Infotainment | Commercials, Visual Media and language; inference and connections. 8 questions x 2 marks = 16 marks. Because 3B is harder and worth less per question than 3A, do 3A first, then 3B. Use S-S-C, and for every inference question ask: "Which sentence in the text supports this?"

Quick summary

  • Commercials combine images, music and words to grab attention.
  • Slogans are repeated; "limited offer" creates urgency; emotive language appeals to feelings.
  • Critics and supporters give two viewpoints.
  • Inference = supported by the text, not extreme.
