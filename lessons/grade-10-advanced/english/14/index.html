Grammar Part 2: Solving the News-Report Gap-Fill

The idea in detail

Part 2 of the exam is a 160-word news report about an airplane crash with 5 gaps (A/B/C, 2 marks each). Each gap tests one of the seven grammar topics: present simple passive, present perfect continuous, participle clauses, demonstratives, subordinating conjunctions, question tags and adverbs of degree. The text also uses the news words you know: injuries, witness, scene, suffered, investigation, fortunately.

Why news reports look the way they do

  • The main event uses the past tense (crashed, landed, was forced).
  • The doer is often unknown or not important, so reporters use the passive (were taken to hospital, was reported).
  • Reporters link ideas with conjunctions (although, because, as soon as) and shorten sentences with participle clauses (passengers injured in the crash).
  • Time words (since, for, all morning) show that rescue and investigation are still going on, so the present perfect continuous is common.

The 5-step method

  1. Read the whole text quickly (skim).
  2. Look at the words before and after each gap.
  3. Name the grammar topic.
  4. Check subject, tense and meaning against each option.
  5. Read the full sentence with your answer.
Worked example 1. "A small plane (1) ___ forced to land in a desert area near Al Ain on Saturday. Passengers, (2) ___ in the crash, were taken to hospital. (3) ___ the weather was clear, the cause of the accident is not known. Experts (4) ___ at the scene since dawn."
Gap 1: the plane did not choose; it "received" the action → past passive: was (forced). Gap 2: passengers receive the harm → past participle: injured. Gap 3: the two ideas contrast (clear weather, unknown cause) → Although. Gap 4: "since dawn" and still working → have been working.
Worked example 2. "A witness said the plane was flying ____ low, wasn't it?" (adverb of degree + tag). Only extremely / very fits before "low"; the tag "wasn't it" matches "was flying". For a final sentence beginning "___ was a frightening day", a singular, past, pointing word is needed: That.

⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) Reading only the sentence with the gap and ignoring the earlier sentences, which fix the tense. (2) Choosing "is forced" when the story is in the past: use "was forced". (3) Missing "since/for" and choosing the simple past. (4) Forgetting that the participle -ed has a passive meaning. (5) Choosing an answer that is grammatical but does not fit the meaning.
Exam corner: Part 2 - 160-word narrative, 5 gaps. To score 10/10: identify the topic, check the three things (subject, tense, meaning), and read the finished sentence aloud in your head. If two options look possible, look again at the clue words around the gap.

Quick summary

  • News report: past tense for events, passive for unknown doers.
  • since/for → present perfect continuous.
  • Opposite ideas → although; reason → because.
  • Check subject + tense + meaning for every gap.
