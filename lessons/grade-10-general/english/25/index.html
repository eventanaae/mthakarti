Grammar: Adverbial Linkers

The idea in detail

Adverbial linkers (أدوات الربط) are words or phrases that connect ideas between sentences or clauses and show the relationship between them: adding, contrast, result, example, order or conclusion. They make a text smooth, like bridges between ideas. Without them, sentences feel like a list.

Choosing the right linker

First, look at the two ideas and ask: how are they related? If the second adds to the first, use moreover; if it is the opposite, use however; if it is a result, use therefore. Example: "Solar power is clean. ____, it saves money." The second sentence adds a benefit, so use Moreover. "Summer is hot and humid. ____, winter is mild." The second sentence is a contrast, so use In contrast.

Punctuation

Put a full stop (or semicolon) before the linker and a comma after it: "The wind was strong. Therefore, the flight was cancelled." or "The wind was strong; therefore, the flight was cancelled." A linker can sometimes move inside the sentence, between commas: "The flight, therefore, was cancelled."

Example 1. "The UAE has little rainfall. ____, it uses cloud seeding to get more water." Relationship: result. Answer: Therefore / As a result.
Example 2. "Many students prefer living in a dormitory. ____, others rent flats because they want more privacy." Relationship: contrast. Answer: However / On the other hand. Another: "Dubai is humid. ____, Al Ain is drier." Answer: In contrast.
⚠️ (1) Do not join two sentences with only a comma and a linker ("The wind was strong, therefore the flight was cancelled" is a comma splice). (2) but, so, because, although are conjunctions, not adverbial linkers; they do not need a comma after them. (3) However and although are different: "Although it was hot, we went out" versus "It was hot. However, we went out". (4) "Moreover" and "furthermore" add a point of the same kind, so do not use them for an opposite idea. (5) "In conclusion" belongs at the end only.
Exam corner. Part 2 Grammar (G.2.5): a gap with 3 linkers; decide the relationship first. In Writing 1.2 use at least four different linkers (opinion, addition, contrast, result, conclusion) for cohesion marks. Do not use the same linker repeatedly.

Quick summary

  • Linkers show relationships: add, contrast, result, example, order, conclusion.
  • Full stop (or semicolon) before, comma after.
  • Decide the relationship, then choose the linker.
  • but/so/because/although are conjunctions, not linkers.
