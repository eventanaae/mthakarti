Listening: Time Management Note-Taking

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: L&S Ch1 Listening 2 — freshman workshop

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

In Listening 2 "Freshman workshop – you are on your own!" a speaker gives first-year students advice about studying and time management. You listen for main ideas and specific information, and take notes.

Vocabulary
manage (time) = control and organise it · schedule = a plan of times and tasks · on my own = alone, without help · deadline = the last date to finish something · tip = a useful piece of advice · tutor = a teacher who helps one student or a small group · cross it off = draw a line through an item when it is done · procrastinate = delay work until later

The three main tips from the workshop:

  1. Write down your weekly schedule for everything.
  2. Write down everything you need to do each day and cross it off when you do it.
  3. Stick to your schedule — do what you planned.

Note-taking strategies

  • Write key words only (nouns, verbs, numbers), not full sentences.
  • Use abbreviations and symbols: e.g., &, →, =, b/c (because), w/ (with).
  • Listen for signal words: first, the most important, another tip, finally.
  • Organise notes as main idea + details (outline or two columns).
⚠️ A deadline is a date/time limit, not a test. A tip here means advice, not money.

The idea in detail

In Listening 2, "Freshman workshop – you are on your own!", a speaker gives practical advice to first-year students who are now living away from their families. Nobody tells them when to wake up or study, so they must manage their time. You will listen for main ideas and specific information, and you will take notes.

Key vocabulary

manage time = organise and control it; schedule = a plan with times and tasks; deadline = the last date to finish; on my own = without help; tip = a useful piece of advice; tutor = a teacher who helps a student or a small group; cross off = draw a line through a finished task; procrastinate (يؤجل) = delay work until later.

The three tips of the speaker

  1. Write down your weekly schedule for everything: classes, study, sport, meals.
  2. Write down everything you need to do each day and cross it off when finished. Crossing off gives a feeling of success.
  3. Stick to your schedule (keep following it).

Why note-taking needs a method

The speaker does not wait for you. If you try to write every word, you miss the next sentence. So you write only the important words: nouns, verbs and numbers. Use symbols and abbreviations: & (and), → (leads to), = (is/means), w/ (with), b/c (because), e.g. (for example). Listen for signal words that say "important point coming": first, the most important thing, another tip, finally. Then organise the notes in two columns: main idea on the left, details on the right.

Example 1 (notes). The speaker says: "The first thing you should do is write your weekly schedule. Put your classes, your study time, even time for sport." Your notes:
Tip 1: weekly schedule → classes + study + sport.
This is short, clear and has the main idea and details.
Example 2 (question). "What should students do when they finish a task?" Answer from notes: cross it off the list. The speaker said to write each day's tasks and cross them off when done.
⚠️ (1) A deadline is a time limit, not a test and not a "dead line". (2) A tip here is advice, not money for a waiter. (3) Do not write full sentences while listening. (4) Do not write your opinion in the notes: write only what the speaker says.
Exam corner. The vocabulary of this lesson (manage, schedule, deadline) appears in Part 1 gap-fill; remember the clues: "manage to do", "meet a deadline", "follow a schedule". Time management also supports writing and speaking tasks about study habits.

Quick summary

  • Tips: weekly schedule, daily to-do list with crossing off, stick to the plan.
  • Notes = key words + symbols, not sentences.
  • Signal words: first, most important, another tip, finally.
  • Words: manage, schedule, deadline, tip, tutor, procrastinate.
