The idea in detail

Reading 1 of Chapter 2, "Feeling under the weather", explains that the atmosphere (الغلاف الجوي) and the weather can affect our moods (feelings) and our health. The idiom feeling under the weather means "feeling a little sick", and it is a good example of how weather and health are linked in English.

Weather and its effects

WeatherEffect
Thunderstorma lot of rain in a short time; flooding and damage to homes
Hurricanevery strong winds; falling trees, injuries, damaged homes
Changes in weather or temperatureheadaches, asthma attacks
Cold weatherpneumonia (التهاب رئوي), heart attacks
Long periods of darknesssad moods

Why does this happen? The body works best in a stable environment. When the temperature or air pressure changes quickly, the body must adapt, and some people feel pain or have breathing problems. Little sunlight can change the chemicals in the brain that control mood, so people may feel sad in long dark winters.

Desert heat and the human body (UAE)

In the UAE, summer temperatures are about 35 to 45 degrees Celsius, and the coast is humid. The body cools itself by sweating, but sweat means water loss.

  • Dehydration: the body loses too much water. Signs: thirst, headache, tiredness.
  • Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea.
  • Heatstroke: the most serious; the body temperature is very high and the person may stop sweating or become confused. It is an emergency: call for help, move to a cool place, cool the body.
  • Prevention: drink water regularly, avoid the midday sun, wear light and loose clothes, rest in the shade or in air conditioning.

Reading skill: making connections

Connect the text to yourself (text-to-self: "I get a headache before a storm"), to other texts (text-to-text) and to the world (text-to-world: news about floods). Connections help you understand and remember.

Example 1. Question: "According to the text, what can changes in weather cause?" Scan for the key words "changes in weather". Answer: headaches and asthma attacks. (Do not choose "heart attacks"; that is linked to cold weather.)
Example 2 (connection). "Last August my uncle worked outside for hours, drank no water and felt dizzy. He had heat exhaustion. This connects to the text because heavy sweating and dizziness are signs of it. He recovered after resting in the shade and drinking water."
⚠️ (1) Weather is the condition on a day; climate is the usual weather over many years. (2) Do not take "feeling under the weather" literally; it means slightly ill. (3) Heat exhaustion and heatstroke are different: heatstroke is more dangerous. (4) Match each effect to the right weather; options often mix them.
Exam corner. Part 3A/3B: "specific information" (scan for the weather word), "details", and in 3B "connection" questions: choose the option that links the idea to a similar situation, not the one that repeats the words. Vocabulary: humidity, temperature, hazardous, severe.

Quick summary

  • Weather affects mood and health: changes cause headaches and asthma; cold causes pneumonia and heart attacks; darkness causes sad moods.
  • Dehydration, heat exhaustion, heatstroke: from mild to serious.
  • Prevention: water, shade, light clothes, avoid midday sun.
  • Connections: text-to-self, text-to-text, text-to-world.
