Reading: A Shared-Living Experience

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Ch1 Reading 1 — topic, main idea, details

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

In Reading 1 "Ready for a shared-living experience?" you read about students living together away from home (roommates, dorms). The skill is to find the topic, the main idea and the details.

Topic = what the text is about in 1–3 words (e.g. shared living).
Main idea = the most important point the writer makes about the topic — a full sentence (e.g. Shared living is easier when roommates respect each other's privacy and rules.).
Details = facts, examples and reasons that support the main idea.
  • The main idea is often in the first sentence (topic sentence) or in the heading. Paragraph headings are supposed to give the main idea of the paragraph.
  • Use the text structure (headings, paragraphs, lists) to organise your notes: heading → main idea → 2–3 details.

Key vocabulary: advice, attitudes, challenges, community, cultural background, habits, lifestyle, major (area of study), personalities, privacy, rules, security, strangers, variety; verbs: get along (with) [يتفاهم مع], get through, relate (to), stay out of, survive; adjectives: common, considerate [يراعي الآخرين], private, shared, similar; phrase: away from home.

Paragraph: "Roommates often come from different cultural backgrounds. One may sleep early; the other may study late. Talking about rules in the first week helps them get along."
Topic: roommates · Main idea: talking about rules helps different roommates get along · Detail: one sleeps early, the other studies late.
⚠️ A detail is NOT the main idea. If an option is too narrow (one example only) or too general (the whole world), it is not the main idea.

The idea in detail

Every reading text has three layers. Think of a tree: the topic is the name of the tree, the main idea is the trunk (the strong central point), and the details are the branches and leaves that grow from it.

  • Topic (الموضوع): a short label, 1 to 3 words. It answers: "What is this text about?" Example: roommates.
  • Main idea (الفكرة الرئيسية): a complete sentence that says what the writer wants you to understand about the topic. It answers: "What is the writer's most important message?"
  • Supporting details (التفاصيل الداعمة): facts, reasons, examples, numbers and explanations that prove or explain the main idea.

Why it works and how to find them

Writers organise a paragraph so that readers can follow it. The topic sentence is usually the first sentence, and the other sentences support it. In a longer article, each paragraph has a heading that tells you its main idea. If you read only the headings and first sentences, you can understand most of the text. This is called skimming.

A quick test for the main idea: cover one sentence. If the paragraph still makes sense, that sentence was a detail. If the paragraph becomes confusing, you may have covered the main idea.

Shared living is a good topic because it is common in the UAE: students from Ras Al Khaimah or Fujairah may live in a university residence in Abu Dhabi, far from their families. They share rooms, kitchens and rules with strangers (people they do not know) who have different habits and cultural backgrounds. The reading explains the challenges and gives advice such as respecting privacy, agreeing on rules and being considerate (يراعي مشاعر الآخرين).

Example 1. "Living with a roommate is not always easy. Some people stay up late, and others wake up early. Some like a quiet room, and others like music. Clear rules about sleep and noise make shared living easier."
Topic: living with a roommate.
Main idea: clear rules about sleep and noise make shared living easier.
Details: late and early sleepers; quiet versus music.
Note: the details come first here, and the main idea is in the last sentence. Always read the whole paragraph.
Example 2 (choose the main idea). Text: "Many first-year students feel nervous in a new place. Joining a club or a study group helps them meet people. Talking to an adviser can also help them solve problems."
A) Clubs are fun. (too narrow: only one idea)
B) University is important in life. (too general)
C) First-year students can feel less nervous by joining groups and asking for help. (correct: it covers the whole paragraph)
The answer is C.
⚠️ Three common mistakes: (1) choosing a detail because it appears in the text; (2) choosing an option that is too general, such as "Students are important"; (3) confusing the topic (a short label) with the main idea (a full sentence about the topic).
Exam corner. This lesson trains Part 3 questions: "What is the main idea of the text?", "What is the best title?" and "specific information" questions. How to get full marks: (1) skim the title and first sentences; (2) read each option and ask "Does this cover the whole text?"; (3) delete options that are too narrow, too wide or not in the text; (4) for detail questions, scan the text for the key word and match the meaning, because options often use different words (paraphrase). For vocabulary, remember these words: privacy, major, strangers, community, challenges, get along with, considerate, away from home.

Quick summary

  • Topic = short label; main idea = full sentence; details = support.
  • The main idea is often in the heading or the first sentence, but not always.
  • Skim first, then read for details.
  • A good main idea is not too narrow and not too general.
  • Key words: get along with, privacy, considerate, strangers, community.
