Grammar: Verb + to-infinitive / infinitive / -ing

The idea in detail

In English, when two verbs come together, the second verb takes a special form. The form depends on the first verb. There is no logic to guess it; you must learn the groups. There are three forms: to + infinitive (to go), -ing form (going) and infinitive without to (go).

Memory help: the to-infinitive often looks to the future or a plan (want, hope, plan, decide). The -ing form often means an activity that is real, ongoing or finished (enjoy, finish, avoid, keep).

Verbs that change meaning

  • stop to do = stop in order to do something: He stopped to drink water.
  • stop doing = finish an activity: He stopped drinking coffee.
  • remember to do = not forget to do it: Remember to lock the door. remember doing = recall a memory: I remember visiting Oman.
  • try to do = make an effort; try doing = test something as an experiment.
Example 1. Choose the correct form: "We hope ____ (visit) Jebel Jais this winter." Answer: to visit (hope + to). "She avoids ____ (drive) in a sandstorm." Answer: driving (avoid + -ing). "You should ____ (wear) a hat." Answer: wear (modal + base verb).
Example 2 (correct the sentences). "He enjoys to swim after school and decided going to the beach." Correct: "He enjoys swimming after school and decided to go to the beach." Another: "I'm interested in to learn Spanish." Correct: "I'm interested in learning Spanish" (preposition + -ing).
⚠️ (1) "enjoy to swim", "decide going", "suggest to stay" are wrong: enjoy swimming, decide to go, suggest staying. (2) After a modal do not add "to": "can to swim" is wrong. (3) After a preposition always use -ing: "good at swimming", "before leaving". (4) "Let" and "make" take the base verb: "Her mother made her study", not "to study" (in the active form). (5) Do not mix the meaning of "stop to" and "stop -ing".
Exam corner. Part 2 Grammar (G.19.2 verb forms). A gap after a verb: look at the verb before the gap, then recall its group. If you are not sure, think of a similar verb you know: want/need/hope (to) and enjoy/finish/avoid (-ing). Also look for a preposition before the gap (-ing) or a modal (base verb).

Quick summary

  • want, need, decide, hope, plan, manage + to-infinitive.
  • enjoy, avoid, finish, suggest, keep, mind + -ing.
  • modals, let, make + base verb; prepositions + -ing.
  • stop to do versus stop doing: different meanings.
