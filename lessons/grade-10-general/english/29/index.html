Functional Language: Past Experiences, Opinion & Obligation

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 4 Writing — FL.31, FL.16, FL.20

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

FL.31 Describing past experiences and events (Talking About Seasons)

  • Past simple for finished events: Last summer we travelled to Oman. Past continuous for background: It was raining while we were driving.
  • Time expressions: last winter, a few years ago, when I was ten, the first time I…, at that moment, afterwards.
  • Feelings and reasons: I felt terrified because the wind was so strong. It was the best day of my life because…
  • used to + base verb for past habits: We used to play outside in winter.

FL.16 Expressing opinion

  • In my opinion / From my point of view / I (strongly) believe that / I think / I feel that / It seems to me that…
  • Agree / disagree: I agree with… because… · I don't agree that… · I'm not sure that…

The idea in detail

"Functional language" means the phrases we use to do something with language: describe the past, give an opinion, or say what is necessary. The exam Writing task uses three functions: FL.31, FL.16 and FL.20.

FL.31: Describing past experiences and events (Talking About Seasons)

  • Past simple for finished events: Last summer we travelled to Oman.
  • Past continuous for the background: It was raining while we were driving.
  • Time expressions: last winter, a few years ago, when I was ten, the first time I..., afterwards.
  • Feelings and reasons: I felt terrified because the wind was so strong.
  • used to + base verb for past habits that stopped: We used to play outside in winter.

FL.16: Expressing opinion

In my opinion, From my point of view, I (strongly) believe that, I think that, It seems to me that. To agree or disagree: I agree with... because..., I do not agree that..., I am not sure that... A good opinion always has a reason.

FL.20: Expressing necessity and obligation (Shared living rules)

MeaningLanguageExample
Necessary, strong obligationmust, have to, need to, it is essential to, be required toResidents have to follow the dormitory regulations.
Past obligationhad toI had to finish the assignment last night.
Not necessarydon't have to, don't need toYou don't have to bring bedding.
Forbiddenmustn't, can't, not allowed toYou mustn't play loud music after 10 p.m.
Adviceshould, ought toRoommates should agree on rules early.

Why "had to"? The modal must has no past form, so we borrow had to from "have to".

Example 1 (past experience). "Last winter, my family went camping near Jebel Jais. It was cold, so we wore warm jackets. While we were cooking dinner, it started to drizzle, but we did not mind. It was the best weekend of the year because we spent time together."
Example 2 (opinion and obligation). "In my opinion, students who share a room should agree on rules at the start. They have to respect each other's quiet time, and they mustn't use the other person's things without asking. They don't have to become best friends, but they need to be polite."
⚠️ (1) mustn't (forbidden) is not the same as don't have to (not necessary). (2) must has no past; write "had to". (3) Opinion phrases need a full clause: "In my opinion, summer is too hot", not "In my opinion, too hot". (4) After "used to", use the base verb: "used to play", not "used to playing". (5) Do not use "I think" many times; vary the phrases.
Exam corner. In Writing 1.2 the themes (seasons and shared living) call for these functions. Use at least one phrase from each: an opinion phrase in the introduction, a past experience as a reason or example, and a modal of obligation when you speak about rules or responsibilities. Examiners reward range and accuracy.

Quick summary

  • Past experience: past simple, past continuous, time expressions, used to.
  • Opinion: In my opinion, I believe that, I agree/disagree because...
  • Obligation: must/have to; had to (past); mustn't (forbidden); don't have to (not necessary); should (advice).
  • Always give reasons.
