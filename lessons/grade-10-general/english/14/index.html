Writing: A Seasonal Experience & Wild Weather

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Ch2 Writing — narrative, past tenses

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

A narrative tells a true or imagined story in time order, e.g. A Seasonal Experience (a winter camping trip) or Wild Weather (a sandstorm, a thunderstorm).

Structure: 1. Beginning – when, where, who (setting) · 2. Middle – events in order, the problem/climax · 3. End – what happened finally + how you felt / what you learned.

Grammar

  • Past simple for completed main events: We drove to Jebel Jais. It started to rain.
  • Past continuous (was/were + -ing) for background or an action in progress: The wind was blowing. We were hiking when the storm hit.
  • when + past simple (short action) · while + past continuous (long action).

Sequence words: First, At first, Then, Next, After that, Suddenly, Later, In the end, Finally.

Descriptive weather words: pouring, freezing, scorching, humid, foggy, a sandstorm, thunder and lightning, gusts of wind.

Model: Last January, my family went camping in the desert. At first, the sky was clear and the air was cool. While we were cooking dinner, a strong wind started. Suddenly, sand filled the air and we couldn't see. We ran to the car and waited. In the end, the storm stopped after an hour. I learned to always check the weather forecast.
⚠️ Irregular past forms: go → went, see → saw, take → took, begin → began, feel → felt, hit → hit. Don't write "goed".

The idea in detail

A narrative (قصة) tells a true or imagined story in time order. "A Seasonal Experience" could be a winter camping trip; "Wild Weather" could be a sandstorm or a thunderstorm. A good narrative makes the reader see and feel the events.

Structure

  1. Beginning: the setting (when, where, who). Sets the scene.
  2. Middle: events in order, the problem or climax (the most exciting moment).
  3. End: how it finished and how you felt or what you learned.

Past tenses

  • Past simple for completed main events: We drove to Jebel Jais. The storm started.
  • Past continuous (was/were + verb-ing) for the background or for an action in progress: The wind was blowing. We were hiking.
  • Use them together: when + past simple (a short action interrupts); while + past continuous (a long action).

Think of a film: the past continuous is the background scene (what was happening) and the past simple is the action that interrupts it.

Sequence words: First, At first, Then, Next, After that, Suddenly, Later, In the end, Finally. Descriptive words: pouring rain, freezing, scorching, humid, foggy, gusts of wind, thunder and lightning.

Example 1. "We were hiking in the mountains when it started to rain heavily. While we were running to the car, a gust of wind took my hat." Analysis: "were hiking" = background; "started" = interrupting event; "were running" = action in progress; "took" = a short event.
Example 2 (model narrative). "Last January, my family went camping in the desert. At first, the sky was clear and the air was cool. While we were cooking dinner, a strong wind started. Suddenly, sand filled the air and we could not see. We ran to the car and waited there. In the end, the storm stopped after an hour. I learned to always check the weather forecast before a trip."
Beginning (when, where, who), middle (wind, sand, run), end (storm stopped, lesson).
⚠️ (1) Irregular past forms: go-went, see-saw, take-took, begin-began, feel-felt, hit-hit, run-ran, make-made. Do not write "goed" or "taked". (2) Do not change tense without a reason, for example from past to present. (3) "When I was sleeping the storm came" is correct, but "While I slept..." is less natural for an interrupted action. (4) Keep the story in order and do not add too many unrelated events. (5) Use specific adjectives rather than only "bad" or "nice".
Exam corner. Past continuous is in the Part 2 grammar list; the text is a narrative about wild weather (like this lesson). In a gap: if two actions are in the sentence and one is long, choose the past continuous; for a finished short event, the past simple. In Writing 1.2 you may include a short experience as an example, but do not write a whole story.

Quick summary

  • Narrative = beginning (setting), middle (events and problem), end (result and feeling).
  • Past simple for main events; past continuous for background.
  • when + past simple; while + past continuous.
  • Learn irregular verbs and sequence words.
