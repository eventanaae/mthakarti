Grammar: Defining Relative Clauses

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 Grammar — G.4.1 Clauses and phrases: defining relative clauses

A defining relative clause gives essential information that tells us which person or thing we mean. It has no commas.

Two sentences → one: "I met a student. He studies climate." → I met a student who studies climate.
⚠️ Do not repeat the subject/object: "The storm which it hit us" ✗ → "The storm which hit us" ✓. Use who for people, not which. whose ≠ who's (who is).

The idea in detail

A relative clause (جملة الصلة) is a small clause that gives more information about a noun. A defining relative clause gives essential information: it tells us which person or thing we mean. Without it, the sentence would be unclear.

Compare: "The student who won the prize is my cousin." Which student? The one who won the prize. The clause is needed, so there are no commas.

PronounUsed forExample
who / thatpeopleThe man who reports the weather is on TV.
which / thatthings and animalsThe storm which hit Dubai was severe.
whosepossession (+ noun)She is the girl whose project won first prize.

How to build one

Start with two simple sentences about the same noun and join them: "I met a student. He studies climate." The second sentence describes the student, so replace "he" with who and attach it directly after the noun: "I met a student who studies climate."

Omitting the pronoun

If the pronoun is the object of the clause (another subject comes after it), you can leave it out: "The jacket (that) I bought is waterproof" (I = subject, so "that" can go). If the pronoun is the subject of the clause (a verb comes directly after it), you must keep it: "The jacket that keeps me dry is expensive" (no subject after "that", so it stays).

Example 1. Join: "The bus was late. It goes to Sharjah." -> "The bus which goes to Sharjah was late." (thing = which; or that). Join: "That is the woman. Her car was stolen." -> "That is the woman whose car was stolen."
Example 2 (choose). "The place ____ we spent our holiday was beautiful." Answer: where (place). "The day ____ we arrived was windy." Answer: when (time). "The teacher ____ taught us yesterday is kind." Answer: who (person).
⚠️ (1) Do not repeat the subject or object: "The storm which it hit us" is wrong; write "The storm which hit us". (2) Use who for people, which for things; "that" is possible for both. (3) whose is possession; who's means "who is". (4) No commas in a defining clause: do not write "The man, who lives here, is a doctor" for essential information. (5) Do not use "what" in place of "which".
Exam corner. Part 2 Grammar (G.4.1): a gap in a narrative text. Ask: what is the noun before the gap (person, thing, place, time, possession)? Then choose who, which, where, when or whose. Test the answer by reading the clause alone. In Writing, defining clauses add detail: "A place where I feel relaxed is the beach."

Quick summary

  • Defining clause = essential information, no commas.
  • who (people), which (things), whose (possession), where (place), when (time); that for people or things.
  • Omit the pronoun only when it is the object.
  • Never repeat the subject or object after the pronoun.
