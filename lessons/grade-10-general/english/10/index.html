Grammar: Comparatives & Superlatives

Other comparison structures
• as + adjective + as (equal): Al Ain is as hot as Dubai in July.
• not as … as (less): Winter is not as humid as summer.
• much / a lot / far + comparative: much hotter.
• less + adjective: less humid.
⚠️ Never double: "more hotter" ✗ → hotter ✓; "the most best" ✗ → the best ✓. Use than after a comparative, the before a superlative.

The idea in detail

We use comparatives to compare TWO things and superlatives to compare ONE thing with THREE or more. Example: "Summer is hotter than winter" (two seasons) and "July is the hottest month" (all months).

How to form them

TypeComparativeSuperlative
1 syllable: old, tall, fastold + erthe old + est
ends in -e: wide, nicewid + erthe wid + est
short vowel + one consonant: hot, bigdouble the consonant: hotter, biggerthe hottest, the biggest
ends in -y: easy, sunny, dryy becomes i: easier, sunnierthe easiest, the sunniest
2 or more syllables: successful, importantmore successfulthe most successful
irregular: good, bad, farbetter, worse, farther/furtherthe best, the worst, the farthest

Why double the consonant? To keep the vowel sound short. "hot" has a short o; "hoter" would look like "hote", so we write "hotter".

Other comparison structures

  • as + adjective + as = equal: Al Ain is as hot as Dubai in July.
  • not as ... as = less: Winter is not as humid as summer.
  • much / a lot / far + comparative = big difference: much hotter.
  • less + adjective: less humid.
  • Use than after a comparative and the before a superlative.
Example 1. Fill in: "Abu Dhabi is (big) ____ than Al Ain." Answer: bigger (one syllable with short vowel + consonant: double the g). "Dubai is (famous) ____ city in the UAE." Answer: the most famous (2 or more syllables, and it is a group comparison).
Example 2 (correct the mistakes). "This is the most best phone and it is more cheaper than that one." Correct: "This is the best phone and it is cheaper than that one." (No double forms: best is already a superlative, cheaper is already a comparative.) Also: "She is as tall as her sister" (equal) but "She is not as tall as her father" (less).
⚠️ (1) Never double the form: "more hotter", "the most best". (2) Do not forget than: "better than". (3) Spelling: happy becomes happier; big becomes bigger; but fast becomes faster (no doubling, because the vowel is long or the word has two final consonants, as in "cold, colder"). (4) Two-syllable adjectives ending in -y use -er; others like "tired" use "more tired". (5) "farther" is for distance; "further" can also mean "more".
Exam corner. Comparatives are in the Part 2 grammar list (gap-fill in a narrative text, 5 questions x 2 marks). Read the whole sentence: if there is "than", choose the comparative; if there is "the" and three or more things, choose the superlative. Also use comparatives in Writing 1.2 to compare seasons or housing: "Winter is more pleasant than summer."

Quick summary

  • Comparative = 2 things (+ than); superlative = 3 or more (the ...).
  • Short: -er/-est; long: more/most; y to i; double the final consonant after a short vowel.
  • Irregular: good, better, best; bad, worse, worst.
  • as ... as = equal; not as ... as = less.
