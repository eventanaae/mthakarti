Reading: Question Types & Strategies (Parts 3A & 3B)

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 3A/3B — specific information, details, inference, overall meaning, connections, predictions

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Skim the text first (title, first sentences) for the general idea, then read the question and scan for key words. The exam texts are expository (they explain a topic) of 250–270 words.

Question typeWhat it asksHow to answer
Specific informationWho / what / when / how many…?Scan for the key word or number; the answer is stated in the text.
DetailsAccording to the text, why / how…?Find the sentence; match its meaning (options use different words = paraphrase).
InferenceWhat does the word '…' mean? / What can you infer?Use context clues and word parts (prefixes, suffixes); check the meaning fits the sentence.
Overall meaningWhat is the main idea / best title?Not too narrow (one detail) and not too general; it covers the whole text.
Connections (3B)Link the text to your own experience, other texts or the wider world.Choose the option about the same idea or situation, not the same words.
Predictions (3B)What will probably happen / come next?Use the headings, the last sentence and the text's organisation.
Mini text: "Architects in hot cities are returning to old ideas. Narrow streets create shade, thick walls keep heat out and wind towers catch breezes and send cool air into buildings. As a result, buildings need less air conditioning." → Detail: wind towers send cool air into buildings. Inference: narrow streets keep cities cooler because tall buildings block the sun. Overall meaning: old building ideas can keep hot cities cooler.
⚠️ A true statement is not always the right answer: it must answer this question and be supported by this text. Beware options that repeat words from the text but change the meaning.

The idea in detail

Parts 3A and 3B of the exam use expository texts (نصوص توضيحية): texts that explain a topic, about 250 to 270 words long. Part 3A is level B1.2 and Part 3B is harder (B2.1). Each question tests a specific reading skill, and each skill has its own method.

The general method

  1. Skim the title and the first sentence of each paragraph to get the topic.
  2. Read the question and find the key words.
  3. Scan the text for those words, then read the nearby sentences carefully.
  4. Compare each option with the text. Eliminate wrong options.
Question typeWhat it asksHow to answer
Specific informationWho, what, when, how many?Scan for the name, number or key word; the answer is stated.
DetailsAccording to the text, why or how?Find the sentence; options paraphrase the text.
InferenceWhat does the word mean? What can you conclude?Use context clues and word parts; check the sentence.
Overall meaningMain idea, best titleCovers the whole text; not too narrow or too general.
Connections (3B)Link the text to your experience or the worldChoose the similar idea or situation.
Predictions (3B)What will probably come next?Use headings, the last sentence and the organisation.

Why options are tricky

Exam writers create three kinds of wrong options: (1) the information is in the text but does not answer this question; (2) the text has the same words but changes the meaning; (3) a statement that is true in real life but is not in the text. So the correct answer is the one that is supported by this text and answers this question.

Example 1 (mini text). "Architects in hot cities are returning to old ideas. Narrow streets create shade, thick walls keep heat out and wind towers catch breezes and send cool air into buildings. As a result, buildings need less air conditioning."
Detail: "How do wind towers help?" -> they send cool air into buildings.
Overall meaning: "Old building ideas can keep hot cities cooler."
Prediction (what comes next?): examples of modern buildings that copy these ideas.
Example 2 (inference). "The village was deserted: no people walked in the streets and all the doors were closed." Clue after the colon: no people, doors closed. Meaning: empty, with no people. Test: "The village was empty" makes sense.
⚠️ (1) An option with words copied from the text is not always correct; check the meaning. (2) For overall meaning, do not choose a single detail. (3) Prediction needs support from the text, not your imagination. (4) For specific information, do not rely on memory; go back to the text. (5) Do not spend a long time on one question; leave it and come back.
Exam corner. Part 3A: 8 questions x 3 marks = 24 (4 specific information, 2 details, 1 inference, 1 overall meaning). Part 3B: 8 questions x 2 marks = 16 (4 details, 1 inference, 1 connection, 1 prediction, 1 overall meaning). Because Part 3A questions are worth 3 marks each, accuracy matters. Always give an answer.

Quick summary

  • Skim, then scan, then check the options.
  • Know the six question types and the method for each.
  • Options paraphrase the text; wrong options are tricky.
  • Overall meaning: the whole text; inference: context clues; prediction: use text clues.
