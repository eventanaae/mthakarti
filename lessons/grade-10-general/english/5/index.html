Listening: Nice to Meet You

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: L&S Ch1 Listening 1 — informal speech

The idea in detail

Listening 1 takes place on the first days at university. New freshmen (first-year students) meet each other. You must do two things: (1) listen for specific information (names, places, times, numbers) and (2) understand reduced speech.

What is reduced speech?

In real conversations, people speak fast. Small words are weak and join together. Native speakers do not say "do you want to" slowly; they say "d'ya wanna". This is not wrong. It is normal in informal speech (الكلام غير الرسمي). If you only know the written forms, you cannot hear the words, so you must train your ear.

Informal expressions

hang out (spend free time with friends), got into town (arrived in the city), grab something to eat (get a quick meal), stop by (visit for a short time), suite (a set of rooms for several students), in person (face to face), catch you later (see you later).

How to listen well

  1. Before listening: read the questions and underline key words (a name? a time? a place?). This tells you what to listen for.
  2. While listening: write only key words, numbers and names. Do not try to write full sentences.
  3. After listening: check your answers and use the context to choose.
Example 1. You hear: "Hey, when didja get into town?" Meaning: "When did you arrive in the city?" The answer may be "Yesterday" or "Last night". The speaker is asking about arrival time, not about a city called Town.
Example 2. You hear: "I gotta go, but let's hang out later." Written form: "I have got to go, but let us spend time together later." Meaning: the speaker must leave now and wants to meet again.
⚠️ (1) Do not copy reduced forms in formal writing: write "going to", not "gonna". (2) Do not stop on a word you did not catch: keep listening, because the next sentence gives clues. (3) "Suite" is not "sweet". (4) The numbers are tricky: 13 and 30 sound similar; listen to the stress (thir-TEEN vs THIR-ty).
Exam corner. The end-of-term exam is read and written; there is no listening paper. But this skill helps in speaking and in understanding informal dialogues, and these words (freshman, stop by, hang out) may appear in reading texts. Always remember the formal and informal difference in writing tasks.

Quick summary

  • Reduced speech joins words: did'ya, gotta, gonna, d'ya wanna.
  • Reduced forms are for speaking and listening, not formal writing.
  • Read questions first; write key words only.
  • Learn: freshman, hang out, grab, stop by, suite, in person.
