Grammar: Indirect Questions

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 Grammar — G.17.4 Questions (indirect questions)

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

We use indirect questions to be polite or when we introduce a question with a phrase such as Could you tell me…, Do you know…, I wonder…, I'd like to know….

Direct questionIndirect question
Where is the library?Could you tell me where the library is?
What time does the storm start?Do you know what time the storm starts?
Where do the students live?I wonder where the students live.
Is it going to rain? (yes/no)I wonder if / whether it is going to rain.
Rules: 1) use statement word order (subject before verb) after the question word; 2) no do / does / did; 3) for yes/no questions add if or whether; 4) the end punctuation follows the opening phrase: Could you tell me…? (a question → ?) but I wonder… (a statement → .).
⚠️ "Could you tell me where is the library?" ✗ → where the library is ✓. "Do you know what time does it start?" ✗ → what time it starts ✓.

The idea in detail

A direct question asks immediately: "Where is the library?" An indirect question (سؤال غير مباشر) is placed inside another phrase: "Could you tell me where the library is?" We use it to be more polite (with strangers, teachers, officials) or when we begin with a phrase like Do you know...?, I wonder..., I'd like to know....

Why the word order changes

In a direct question, English puts the helping verb first (Where is the library? What time does the storm start?). In an indirect question, the question becomes part of a statement, so we go back to statement word order: subject + verb. And the helper do / does / did disappears, because it is not needed any more; the main verb carries the tense and the -s or -ed.

DirectIndirect
Where is the library?Could you tell me where the library is?
What time does the storm start?Do you know what time the storm starts?
Where do the students live?I wonder where the students live.
Where did she go?Can you tell me where she went?
Is it going to rain?I wonder if / whether it is going to rain.

Rules

  1. Opening phrase + question word + subject + verb.
  2. No do / does / did (the tense moves to the main verb: did she go -> where she went).
  3. For a yes/no question add if or whether.
  4. End punctuation follows the opening phrase: "Could you tell me ...?" has a question mark, while "I wonder ..." is a statement and ends with a full stop.
Example 1. Change to an indirect question: "What time does the bus leave?" -> "Do you know what time the bus leaves?" (remove does; leave becomes leaves). "How much did the ticket cost?" -> "Could you tell me how much the ticket cost?"
Example 2 (yes/no). "Does the library open on Friday?" -> "I'd like to know if the library opens on Friday." (statement. Note: full stop since "I'd like to know" is a statement). Also: "Do you know whether the weather will be sunny tomorrow?"
⚠️ (1) "Could you tell me where is the library?" is wrong; write "where the library is". (2) "Do you know what time does it start?" is wrong; write "what time it starts". (3) Do not use "if" after a question word: "where if" is wrong. (4) A question mark after "I wonder where he lives?" is wrong. (5) Do not forget the -s or -ed on the main verb after removing do/does/did.
Exam corner. Part 2 Grammar (G.17.4). In a gap, you may have to choose a correct indirect question or the correct word order. Check: is there a helper (do/does/did) after the question word? If yes, it is wrong. Does the subject come before the verb? In writing, indirect questions make your speech polite and formal.

Quick summary

  • Indirect question = opening phrase + question word + subject + verb.
  • No do/does/did and no inversion.
  • Yes/no questions need if or whether.
  • Punctuation follows the opening phrase.
