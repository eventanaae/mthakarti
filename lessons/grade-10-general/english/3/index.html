Writing: Informational Paragraph

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Ch1 Writing — paragraph structure

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

An informational paragraph gives facts and explanations about one topic. It does not argue; it informs.

Structure
1. Topic sentence – states the topic + main idea.
2. Supporting sentences – 3–4 facts, examples, explanations.
3. Concluding sentence – restates the main idea in new words.

Linking words to add information: First, Also, In addition, Furthermore, Moreover, For example, such as, Finally.

Model: Living in a residence hall has several useful features. First, most halls are close to classrooms, so students save travel time. In addition, they offer amenities such as study rooms, laundry and Wi-Fi. Furthermore, students can make friends from different cultural backgrounds. In conclusion, a residence hall is a practical choice for new students.

Avoid repetition and empty sentences. The class slide showed a weak paragraph: "Learning is very essential. It is of a paramount importance. I think it helps us greatly..." Every sentence says the same thing with no facts. Fix it by adding specific details (who? what? how? example?).

⚠️ The topic sentence must not be too narrow (one detail) or too general. The concluding sentence must not add a new idea.

The idea in detail

An informational paragraph explains a topic with facts. The writer does not try to persuade the reader; the aim is to inform. A paragraph is a group of sentences about ONE idea. It is like a sandwich: the top bread is the topic sentence, the filling is the supporting sentences, and the bottom bread is the concluding sentence.

  1. Topic sentence (جملة الموضوع): names the topic and states the main idea, for example "Living in a residence hall has several useful features."
  2. Supporting sentences (3 to 4): give facts, reasons, examples or explanations. Each one adds NEW information.
  3. Concluding sentence (جملة الختام): repeats the main idea in different words and finishes the paragraph. It must not add a new idea.

Why this structure works

The reader first knows what to expect (topic sentence), then receives proof (details), then remembers the point (conclusion). Without a topic sentence the reader is lost. Without details the paragraph is empty. Linking words (transitions) act like road signs: First, In addition, Furthermore, Moreover, For example, such as, Finally, In conclusion. Use such as and for example to give examples; also, in addition to add a point.

Empty sentences and repetition

A weak paragraph repeats the same idea: "Learning is very essential. It is of a paramount importance. It helps us greatly." These sentences give no facts. To improve, answer the questions who? what? where? how? why? For example: "Learning English helps students read university textbooks, talk to people from other countries and find better jobs."

Example 1 (building a paragraph about the UAE summer).
Topic sentence: Summer in the UAE is very hot, so people use several methods to stay cool.
Support 1: First, most buildings have air conditioning.
Support 2: In addition, many people avoid the midday sun and go out after sunset.
Support 3: They also drink water often and wear light cotton clothes.
Conclusion: In conclusion, simple habits help people stay comfortable in the summer heat.
Check: one idea, three different supports, 5 sentences, no new idea in the conclusion.
Example 2 (fixing a weak paragraph). Weak: "Sharing a room is good. It is very good. I like it." Better: "Sharing a room has some benefits. First, it reduces the rent, because two students pay for one room. Also, roommates can help each other with homework. For these reasons, sharing a room is useful for many students."
⚠️ Avoid: (1) a topic sentence that is too narrow ("My roommate has a blue bag") or too general ("Life is hard"); (2) a conclusion with a new idea; (3) repeating the same word or idea; (4) using "First" and then forgetting to use "Finally"; (5) mixing two topics in one paragraph.
Exam corner. This lesson supports the Part 4 writing marks: organisation, linking words, correct grammar and spelling. In the exam, begin every paragraph with a clear topic sentence, support it with reasons and examples, and use at least 3 different linking words. For marks, the examiner looks for: clear ideas, paragraphs, variety of vocabulary, and accuracy.

Quick summary

  • Informational paragraph = topic sentence + 3 to 4 supports + concluding sentence.
  • One paragraph = one idea.
  • Use linking words: First, In addition, Furthermore, For example, In conclusion.
  • Replace empty sentences with specific facts and examples.
