Speaking: Introductions, Study Habits & Goals

Speaking 2 – Talking about study habits. Words: tend to procrastinate, study party (studying in a group), can't concentrate, cram (study a lot just before a test), take breaks.

Grammar for habits and opinions
• Present simple for habits: I usually study at night. She follows a schedule.
• Adverbs of frequency go before the main verb and after be: I usually study… / I am never late.
• Present perfect (I've read…), future (we'll talk), gerund as subject (Studying with music helps me).
• Adverbs to sound natural: actually, honestly, perfectly.

Opinions on study habits: I think / I feel that…, For me, it works better to…, I agree / I disagree because…

Discussing skills and goals: I'm good at + noun/-ing (I'm good at solving problems). I need to improve my… My goal is to + verb. I'd like to / I'm planning to + verb. In five years, I hope to…

⚠️ good at + -ing (good at writing), but goal is to + base verb (my goal is to become an engineer).

The idea in detail

This lesson covers three speaking skills: introducing yourself and other people, talking about study habits, and talking about skills and goals.

1. Introductions

Choose formal language for teachers, officials and new adults, and informal language for friends and classmates.

FormalInformal
Good morning. I am Sara Ahmed.
Pleased to meet you.
I would like to introduce my roommate, Ali.		Hi, I'm Sara.
Nice to meet you. / You, too. / Same here.
This is my roommate, Ali.

After introducing, add one fact and one question to keep the conversation going: "I'm from Al Ain. Where are you from?"

2. Study habits

A habit is something you do regularly. We use the present simple and adverbs of frequency (always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never). Position: before the main verb (I usually study at night) and after the verb "be" (I am never late). Useful words: procrastinate (delay), cram (study a lot just before a test), study party (group study), concentrate (focus), take breaks.

To give an opinion: "I think...", "I feel that...", "For me, it works better to...", "I agree/disagree because...".

3. Skills and goals

A skill is something you can do well. A goal is something you want to achieve.

  • I am good at + noun / -ing: I'm good at solving problems.
  • I need to improve my + noun: I need to improve my writing.
  • My goal is to + base verb: My goal is to become an engineer.
  • I'm planning to / I'd like to + base verb; In five years, I hope to...
Example 1 (mini speech). "Hi, I'm Mariam. I study at night because it is quiet, and I usually finish my homework before midnight. I am good at organising my time, but I sometimes procrastinate when I use my phone. My goal is to become a doctor, so I need to improve my science grades."
It has: introduction, habit with frequency adverbs, a skill (good at + -ing), a weakness (need to improve) and a goal (My goal is to + verb).
Example 2 (introduce a friend). Formal: "Dr. Salem, I would like to introduce my roommate, Khalid. He is a first-year engineering student." Informal: "Khalid, this is Omar. He's in my class." Reply: "Nice to meet you." / "You, too."
⚠️ (1) good at + -ing ("good at writing"), but goal is to + base verb. (2) "I study usually" is wrong: say "I usually study". (3) After "he/she/it" add -s: "She follows a schedule." (4) "Pleased to meet you" is formal; do not use it only with close friends in a funny way. (5) Do not answer with only "yes" or "no": add a reason.
Exam corner. The same language is used in writing tasks about study habits or shared living (functions: giving an opinion, describing). Use frequency adverbs, "I believe that...", and a goal sentence with the right verb pattern. Grammar accuracy (verb forms, word order) gives marks in Part 2 and Writing.

Quick summary

  • Formal: Pleased to meet you; informal: Nice to meet you.
  • Present simple for habits; frequency adverbs before the main verb, after be.
  • good at + -ing; my goal is to + base verb.
  • Words: procrastinate, cram, concentrate, study party.
