Reading: Predicting Housing Challenges

The idea in detail

This lesson has two parts: a reading skill (predicting) and language (housing words and collocations).

1. Predicting (التوقع)

Good readers do not start with a blank mind. Before reading, they look at the title, headings, pictures and the first line, and they ask: "What will this text probably say?" Then they read and check. This works like a weather forecast: you make a guess from clues and then see if it was right. Predicting makes reading faster because your brain is already looking for the information.

Steps: (1) read the title and headings; (2) think about what you already know; (3) write 2 or 3 predictions; (4) read; (5) confirm or change your predictions.

2. Housing types and challenges

The reading "Unique housing for three college students" compares ways to live while studying. A residence hall (dormitory) is a university building with student rooms. Special-interest housing groups students with the same interest. A co-operative is a house where the residents share work and costs. Amenities (مرافق) are useful facilities, such as a gym, laundry or Wi-Fi.

Long travel timeIt takes time and money every dayUse public transport, share rides
Feeling lonelyLiving far from familyJoin clubs, invite friends

3. Collocations

A collocation (تلازم لفظي) is a pair of words that natives use together. There is often no logical reason; you just learn them. The four most common verbs are make, do, take, have.

  • make: a decision, a mistake, friends, progress, money, noise (you create or produce something).
  • do: homework, housework, the dishes, research, your best (activities and tasks).
  • take: a class, a break, a test, notes, a shower.
  • have: fun, a problem, breakfast, a party, a good time.
Example 1 (prediction). Title: "Unique housing for three college students". Picture: a student carrying boxes next to a bus stop. Prediction: the text will describe different kinds of housing and problems such as transport. After reading, you find "Long travel time" in the text, so your prediction was correct.
Example 2 (collocations). Complete: "I need to ____ my homework tonight, and tomorrow I will ____ a test." Answer: do my homework, take a test. Another: "Don't worry if you ____ a mistake. You will ____ progress." Answer: make a mistake, make progress.
⚠️ Learners often say "do a mistake", "make homework" or "make a shower". The correct forms are make a mistake, do homework, take a shower. Also, a prediction is only a guess: do not be upset if it is wrong; change it while reading.
Exam corner. In Part 3B there is one prediction question ("What will the writer probably discuss next?"). Use the headings, the last sentence and the organisation of the text. The correct answer continues the logic of the text. Vocabulary and gap-fill questions can test collocations: the answer is the verb that normally goes with the noun, so read the noun after the gap first.

Quick summary

  • Predict before reading from the title, headings and pictures; check while reading.
  • Housing: residence hall, special-interest housing, co-operative; amenities = facilities.
  • Problems and solutions: travel (transport), loneliness (clubs), rent (part-time work).
  • make a mistake / do homework / take a test / have fun.
