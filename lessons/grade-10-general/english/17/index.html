The idea in detail

The End-of-Term 1 English exam for Grade 10 General (Stage 6, level B1.2) has 100 marks: Reading 60 (online) and Writing 40 (paper-based). If you know the format, you can plan your time and avoid losing easy marks.

PartWhat you doMarks
1 VocabularyGap-fill, 5 gaps, choose A, B or C (themes: Meeting New People, Learning New Things, Come Rain or Shine)5 x 2 = 10
2 GrammarGap-fill, 5 gaps, in a narrative text about wild weather5 x 2 = 10
3A ReadingOne text (B1.2), 8 questions: 4 specific information, 2 details, 1 inference, 1 overall meaning8 x 3 = 24
3B ReadingOne harder text (B2.1), 8 questions: 4 details, 1 inference, 1 connection, 1 prediction, 1 overall meaning8 x 2 = 16
4 Writing1.1 summarise a text (3 to 4 sentences) and 1.2 argumentative text (150 words)15 + 25 = 40

The marks add up: 10 + 10 + 24 + 16 = 60 for Reading, plus 40 for Writing = 100.

Strategies

  • Skim first: read the title, first sentences and last sentences to get the general idea.
  • Scan: look for names, numbers and key words that the question mentions.
  • Use context (the words around), the main message and the organisation of the text.
  • For gap-fill: read the whole sentence, decide the part of speech, test every option.
  • Time management: Reading has four parts; do not spend more than the planned time on one question. Answer easy items first and return to difficult ones.
  • Try to answer every question; do not leave a multiple-choice item empty.
Example 1 (how to spend your time). Give the short Parts 1 and 2 (5 gaps each) a quick, confident pass; they are only 20 marks together. Use more time for Parts 3A and 3B, which carry 40 marks and need reading. Keep the last minutes to check. Follow the exact time limit given on your exam paper.
Example 2 (scan strategy). Question: "How many students said that they left college because of money?" Key words: "how many", "money". Scan for numbers near "money"; the text says 71 percent, so choose the option with 71%. You do not need to read everything again.
⚠️ (1) Do not spend five minutes on one gap. (2) In Writing 1.1, do not copy sentences and do not give your opinion. (3) In Writing 1.2, write at least 150 words in paragraphs and cover all three prompts. (4) An option that is true in real life may still be wrong if it is not in the text. (5) Do not leave blanks.
Exam corner. Grammar in Part 2: past continuous, defining relative clauses, verb patterns (to-infinitive, infinitive, -ing), indirect questions, modals of ability, adverbial linkers, comparatives. Writing functions: describing past experiences and events, expressing opinion, expressing necessity and obligation. Themes: Talking About Seasons, or Ready for a Shared-Living Experience? Use the checklist: topic, structure, grammar, vocabulary, spelling.

Quick summary

  • Total 100: Reading 60 (10 + 10 + 24 + 16) and Writing 40 (15 + 25).
  • Skim, then scan; check the options against the text.
  • Writing 1.1: own words, 3 to 4 sentences; 1.2: 150 words, paragraphs, 3 prompts.
  • Manage time and answer all questions.
