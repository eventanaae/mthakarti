Speaking: Survey Findings & Preferences

Presenting survey findings
• The survey/graph shows that…
• The biggest reason is… – 71% of students said…
• Three of the reasons are related to money.
• Only two reasons are related to personality.
• Quantifiers: almost all (≈ 90–99%), most (> 50%), more than half, about half (≈ 50%), a third, a quarter (25%), some, a few, very few, none.
"According to the survey, most students (71%) left because they needed to work. About a quarter said the classes were too difficult. In my opinion, colleges should offer more scholarships, and students could take evening classes."

Research notes: write notes, not sentences: facts, percentages and the source + year (e.g. OECD "Education at a Glance": only 43% finish a bachelor's degree on time).

Speakers' preferences (e.g. paper notebooks vs iPads): I prefer X to Y. I'd rather + verb than + verb. I'd prefer to… I'm more into…

⚠️ prefer X to Y (not "than"), but would rather … than. "Most of students" is wrong — say most students or most of the students.

The idea in detail

In Speaking 3, "Do all students graduate?", you read a graph or survey about the reasons why students leave university before finishing, and you present the findings to the class. A good presentation reports the data clearly (the numbers), groups them (money, classes, personality) and gives your opinion.

The data

The reasons were in groups: money (the students needed to work and earn money: the biggest reason at 71%; they could not afford fees; it was not worth the money), classes (boring, not useful, too difficult), family and time, and personality (needed a break, did not like sitting in class).

Language for presenting findings

  • The survey / graph shows that...
  • The biggest reason is... (71% of students said...).
  • Three of the reasons are related to money.
  • Only two reasons are related to personality.
QuantifierApproximate meaning
almost allabout 90 to 99%
mostmore than 50%
about halfabout 50%
a third / a quarter33% / 25%
some / a few / very few / nonesmall numbers down to 0%

Research notes should be notes (words and numbers), not full sentences, plus the source and the year, so the audience can trust the data.

Expressing preference

"I prefer paper notebooks to iPads." "I'd rather write by hand than type." "I'd prefer to use an iPad." Give a reason: "because I can search my notes quickly."

Example 1. "According to the survey, most students (71%) left because they needed to work. About a quarter said that the classes were too difficult. Only a few said they needed a break. In my opinion, colleges should offer more scholarships, and students could take evening classes."
Example 2 (preference). "I prefer studying in the library to studying at home because there are fewer distractions. I would rather read a printed book than a screen, as my eyes get tired."
⚠️ (1) prefer X to Y (not "than"), but would rather + base verb than + base verb. (2) "Most of students" is wrong: say "most students" or "most of the students". (3) Do not confuse "a few" (some) with "few" (almost none). (4) 71% is "most", not "about half". (5) Do not read the graph without saying the source and the main trend.
Exam corner. Percentages and quantifiers help in reading questions about details and in Writing 1.1 when you summarise a text with data. Use should and could for suggestions. An accurate verb for data: "show", "report", "say".

Quick summary

  • Presentation: introduce the survey, give the biggest number, group the reasons, give an opinion.
  • Quantifiers: most (more than half), about half, a quarter, a few.
  • prefer X to Y; would rather ... than.
  • Notes = key words, numbers, source and year.
