The idea in detail

This is the vocabulary for a description of wild weather, the theme of the Part 2 narrative text. Learn the words and then practise using them in a description.

How a wild-weather story develops

A typical text follows a sequence: calm weather (cumulus clouds build up) -> a warning is given -> a severe thunderstorm with gale-force winds hits -> the impact (floods, fallen trees, damage) -> people find protection -> the storm ends. When you write, show the change from calm to wild with strong verbs and adjectives and with time words (by evening, suddenly, after an hour).

Example 1 (model description). "In the afternoon, big cumulus clouds built up over the mountains. By evening, a severe thunderstorm hit the city with gale-force winds. The impact was serious: roads flooded and trees fell. Luckily, an early warning gave people time to find protection indoors, so there was little damage."
Example 2 (gap-fill). "The weather office issued a storm ____ at noon, so people stayed at home." Options: A) warning B) protection C) humidity. A message about danger is a warning. Another: "The flood ____ hundreds of cars." Past tense verb: damaged.
⚠️ (1) damage is for things; for people use injure or hurt (the storm injured two people). (2) phenomena is plural; phenomenon is singular. (3) impact can be a noun (a big impact) or a verb. (4) gale-force is hyphenated and used before a noun (gale-force winds). (5) A storm warning is a noun; "to warn" is the verb.
Exam corner. Part 2 is a narrative text about wild weather with 5 grammar gaps. Reading the vocabulary helps you understand the story, and the story style uses past simple and past continuous (for example "A strong wind was blowing when the storm hit"). In Writing 1.2 you can use phrases like "severe weather has a big impact on people" in an opinion text about seasons.

Quick summary

  • cumulus clouds, thunderstorm, gale-force winds, severe, hazardous.
  • warning (message), impact (effect), protection (keeping safe), damage (harm to things).
  • phenomenon singular, phenomena plural.
  • Description order: calm, warning, storm, impact, protection, end.
