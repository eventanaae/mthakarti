Grammar: Modals of Ability

🌦️ اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف العاشر عام📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 Grammar — G.8.1 Modals (present): expressing ability

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي
TimeFormExample
Present abilitycan / can't + base verb (am/is/are able to)Camels can survive for days without water. I can't swim.
General ability in the pastcould / couldn't + base verbWhen I was five, I could already ride a bike.
One specific success in the pastwas / were able to (or managed to)The firefighters were able to rescue everyone yesterday.
Future abilitywill be able toBy next year I will be able to speak English fluently.
Form: modal + base verb. No -s (she can swim), no to (can to swim ✗). Negative: can't / cannot. Question: Can you swim?
⚠️ For a single successful event in the past, use was able to, not could: "The team was able to reach the summit yesterday." (Negative couldn't is fine for both.) can't = no ability; mustn't = forbidden; don't have to = not necessary.

The idea in detail

A modal verb is a helping verb that adds meaning to the main verb. To talk about ability (القدرة), what someone is able to do, we use can and could, or the phrase be able to. The form is always: modal + base verb. A modal has no -s in the third person and no "to" after it.

TimeFormExample
Present abilitycan / can't + base verb (or am/is/are able to)Camels can survive for days without water. I can't swim.
General ability in the pastcould / couldn't + base verbWhen I was five, I could ride a bike.
One specific success in the pastwas / were able to; managed toThe firefighters were able to rescue everyone yesterday.
Future abilitywill be able toBy next year I will be able to speak English fluently.

Why "was able to" for one event?

could describes a general ability over a period ("I could swim when I was six" = I had the ability). But when you talk about one particular success on one occasion ("I passed the test yesterday"), English needs was/were able to or managed to, because it focuses on the result of the effort. The negative "couldn't" is fine for both: "I couldn't open the door" (general or one time).

Also, can has no future form; use will be able to. And can has no infinitive or -ing form; use be able to: "I want to be able to drive."

Example 1. Choose: "Yesterday the rescue team ____ find the lost hikers after a long search." Answer: was able to / managed to (one success). "When she was young, she ____ run very fast." Answer: could (general past ability).
Example 2 (questions and negatives). "Can you swim?" -> "Yes, I can." / "No, I can't." "Could you read when you were four?" -> "No, I couldn't." "Will you be able to join us next week?" -> "Yes, I will be able to come after my exam." A weather example: "Because of the sandstorm, the plane wasn't able to land" (or "couldn't land").
⚠️ (1) "can to swim" and "she cans swim" are wrong: write "can swim", "she can swim". (2) For one past success do not use "could": "The team could reach the summit yesterday" is not natural; use "were able to". (3) can't = no ability; mustn't = forbidden; don't have to = not necessary. (4) Do not use two modals together ("will can" is wrong; use "will be able to"). (5) Question form: put the modal before the subject: "Can you swim?"
Exam corner. Part 2 Grammar (G.8.1). In a narrative text a gap may need "was able to", "couldn't" or "can". Ask: is it present, past general, past one event or future? Choose the matching form. The skill also appears in speaking about skills ("I am good at...", "I can...") and writing about abilities ("Camels have the ability to live without water").

Quick summary

  • can/can't = present; could/couldn't = general past; was/were able to = one past success; will be able to = future.
  • modal + base verb, no -s, no to.
  • Use "be able to" where can has no form.
  • can't is not the same as mustn't.
