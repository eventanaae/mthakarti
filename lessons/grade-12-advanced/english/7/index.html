Grammar 1: Zero Conditionals

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 · 'My roof is alive!'

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Part 2 is about living (green) roofs: rooftops covered with soil and vegetation. They provide insulation, improve drainage, absorb rainwater, reduce urban heat, and create a habitat that increases biodiversity. They are a sustainable design.

Zero conditional = general truths and scientific facts.
Form: If / When + present simple, present simple.
If plants cover a roof, the building stays cooler.
  • If it rains, the soil absorbs the water.
  • When the sun shines on a dark roof, the roof gets very hot.
  • Plants die if they don't get enough water. (the if-clause can come second – no comma)
  • If you don't water a new living roof, the plants don't survive.

⚠️ Third-person -s: 'If the roof absorbs water, it reduces flooding.' No 'will' in a zero conditional describing a general fact. 'If' and 'when' have the same meaning in zero conditionals.

The idea in detail

Conditionals are sentences with if. The zero conditional is used for things that are always true: scientific facts, natural laws, general rules and habits. Because the result is certain every time, we use the present simple in both parts.

Form: If / When + present simple, present simple.
Example: If you heat ice, it melts.

The if-clause can come first (use a comma) or second (no comma): "Plants die if they don't get water."

Why / how it works

Think "always". A zero conditional answers the question: "What always happens?" The "if" can be replaced by "whenever" or "when" without changing the meaning: "When it rains, the soil absorbs water." If the situation is a one-time future possibility ("If we build a roof next year...") it is not zero; that is the first conditional (if + present, will + verb).

Why present simple? The present simple is the tense of facts and routines. Since a zero conditional states a fact, both verbs are in that tense. We never use will for a general fact.

Living-roof context. A living (green) roof is a roof covered with soil and plants. It has facts we can state as zero conditionals: If rain falls, the soil absorbs it. If plants cover the roof, the building stays cooler. If the roof is not watered in summer, some plants die. If water cannot drain, the roof floods.

Negative and question forms. Negative: "If you don't water the plants, they don't survive." Question: "What happens if the soil gets too wet?"

Worked example 1

Complete: "If the sun ____ (shine) on a dark roof, the roof ____ (get) very hot."
Step 1: it is a general fact → zero conditional.
Step 2: both clauses use present simple. Subject "the sun" and "the roof" are third person singular, so add -s.
Answer: shines ... gets.

Worked example 2

Decide which are zero conditionals.
a) "If you mix blue and yellow, you get green." → general fact → zero.
b) "If we build a green roof next year, our bills will fall." → one future situation with will → first.
c) "When the soil is dry, the plants need water." → general truth → zero.
Now join: "Plants / not get light / they / die" → If plants don't get light, they die. (don't, not "doesn't", because "plants" is plural.)

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Adding "will": "If it rains, the roof will absorb water" - this is a prediction, not a general fact. In a zero conditional write "absorbs". 2) Forgetting -s: "If the roof absorb water" ✗ → "absorbs" ✓. 3) Using the past: "If you heated water, it boiled" is not zero conditional for a general fact. 4) Comma: use a comma when the if-clause is first; do not use one when it is second.

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 2 - 'My roof is alive!'" You will see a sentence with a gap such as "If plants cover a roof, the building ____ cooler." and options like stays / will stay / stayed. Full marks: (1) Is the sentence a general truth? (2) If yes, choose the present simple (watch the -s). (3) Eliminate will and past forms. (4) Check subject-verb agreement.

Quick summary

  • Zero conditional = general truths and facts.
  • Form: If/When + present simple, present simple.
  • No will; "if" and "when" are interchangeable.
  • Do not forget third-person -s.
  • Different from first conditional (specific future: if + present, will + verb).
