Grammar 5: Indirect Questions

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 · Indirect questions

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Indirect questions are more polite. They begin with a phrase such as Could you tell me..., Do you know..., I wonder..., I'd like to know..., Can you explain...

In an indirect question the word order is like a statement: question word + subject + verb. Do not use do/does/did, and do not invert.
Direct questionIndirect question
How much does a living roof cost?Could you tell me how much a living roof costs?
Where is the drainage layer?Do you know where the drainage layer is?
Why are green roofs sustainable?Can you explain why green roofs are sustainable?
Does the roof need water?I wonder if / whether the roof needs water.

For yes/no questions use if or whether.

Punctuation: if the opening phrase is a question (Could you tell me...?), end with a question mark. If it is a statement (I wonder..., I'd like to know...), end with a full stop.

⚠️ 'Could you tell me how much does it cost?' ✗ → 'Could you tell me how much it costs?' ✓. 'Do you know where is the habitat?' ✗ → 'Do you know where the habitat is?' ✓.

The idea in detail

A direct question is asked straight away ("Where is the drainage layer?"). An indirect question (also embedded question) is put inside another phrase. It sounds more polite and is used when we ask strangers, teachers or officials, or for information in emails.

Opening phrases: Could you tell me..., Do you know..., Can you explain..., Would you mind telling me..., I wonder..., I'd like to know..., I'm not sure...

The golden rule: after the opening phrase, use statement word order: question word + subject + verb. No inversion. No do / does / did.

How to change a direct question into an indirect one

StepDirect: "How much does a living roof cost?"
1. Add an opening phraseCould you tell me ...
2. Keep the question word... how much ...
3. Put the subject before the verb... a living roof ...
4. Remove do/does/did; put the main verb in the right tense and person... costs
ResultCould you tell me how much a living roof costs?

Why / how it works

In a direct question the helping verb jumps in front of the subject (does a roof cost). When the question is placed inside a statement, the "main" question is the opening phrase, so the inside part goes back to normal statement order. That is why "do" disappears and the main verb takes the -s or the -ed itself: does ... cost → costs.

Yes/no questions: there is no question word, so use if or whether: "Does the roof need water?" → "I wonder if the roof needs water."

Verb "be": the verb moves after the subject: "Where is the habitat?" → "Do you know where the habitat is?"

Punctuation: if the opening phrase is itself a question (Could you tell me..., Do you know...) end with ?. If the opening phrase is a statement (I wonder..., I'd like to know...) end with a full stop.

Worked example 1

Change: "Why are green roofs sustainable?"
Opening: Can you explain ... Question word: why. Subject: green roofs. Verb: are.
Can you explain why green roofs are sustainable? (question mark, because "Can you explain" is a question.)

Worked example 2

Change: "When did the engineers build the roof?"
Remove "did"; verb back to past simple: build → built.
Do you know when the engineers built the roof?
Another: "Does the roof need a drainage layer?" → I'd like to know whether the roof needs a drainage layer. (statement opening → full stop)

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) "Could you tell me how much does it cost?" ✗ → "how much it costs" ✓. 2) "Do you know where is the habitat?" ✗ → "where the habitat is" ✓. 3) Using "that" for yes/no ("I wonder that the roof..." ✗) - use if/whether. 4) Keeping the helping verb "do". 5) Punctuation: "I wonder why it is expensive?" ✗ → full stop.

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 2 - Indirect questions." Choose the correct indirect question from 3 options, or fill the gap: "Can you explain how a living roof ____ the building cool?" (answer: keeps). Full marks: check the word order after the question word; if you see do/does/did or an inverted verb after the question word, the option is wrong.

Quick summary

  • Indirect = polite; opening phrase + question word + subject + verb.
  • No inversion, no do/does/did.
  • Yes/no → if / whether.
  • Question mark only if the opening phrase is a question.
