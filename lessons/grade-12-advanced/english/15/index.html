Part 3B Practice: 'Megacities and Small Towns'

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 3B · Expository text (C1.1)

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Practice text (original, expository)

[1] In 1950, only two cities in the world, New York and Tokyo, had more than ten million inhabitants. Today there are dozens of these megacities, most of them in Asia, Africa and Latin America, and more than half of humanity now lives in urban areas. This rapid urbanisation is one of the defining changes of our time.

[2] The attraction of the megacity is easy to understand. Large cities concentrate jobs, universities, hospitals and cultural life in one place, and they are often centres of innovative business. For a young graduate, a megacity can feel like a door to the world.

[3] Yet size comes at a price. Traffic congestion can turn a ten-kilometre journey into a two-hour ordeal, air pollution harms residents' health, and housing costs push many families to the edges of the city. Governments must constantly invest in infrastructure simply to keep pace with growth.

[4] Small towns offer an almost opposite experience. Life is slower and quieter, homes are cheaper, and neighbours often know one another. However, small towns struggle to keep their young people, who leave in search of work and education. As a result, some towns face shrinking, ageing populations.

[5] Interestingly, technology may be narrowing the gap. Fast internet and remote work allow some professionals to earn a city salary while enjoying small-town life. Whether this trend will reshape where people live remains to be seen, but it suggests that the choice between the big city and the small town may become less absolute in the future.

Question types: details; inference; mood / tone (here: balanced, objective, cautiously hopeful – not angry or humorous); connections between ideas (cause → effect, contrast); overall meaning.
⚠️ Expository texts explain and inform; the writer does not argue strongly for one side. Words like 'may', 'suggests' and 'remains to be seen' show caution.

The idea in detail

Part 3B is an expository text at level C1.1. Expository means that the writer explains a topic and gives information in an organised way, without strongly arguing for one side. The practice text Megacities and Small Towns compares two ways of living. The tone is balanced and objective, with a small amount of caution about the future.

Why / how it works

Tone and mood. Tone is the writer's attitude. Here the writer does not shout or joke; he or she shows two sides fairly. Hedging words (may, suggests, remains to be seen, some) show caution. The final sentence ("may become less absolute in the future") is cautiously hopeful. So the tone is balanced, objective and thoughtful.

Connections are the relations between ideas. Cause → effect: young people leave → shrinking, ageing populations ("As a result"). Contrast: Yet size comes at a price; However, small towns struggle.... Comparison: megacity (fast, expensive, polluted) vs small town (slow, cheap, quiet).

Level C1.1 vocabulary is a bit harder: ordeal (a very difficult experience), concentrate (gather in one place), defining (very typical, important), narrowing the gap (making the difference smaller).

Worked example 1 (connection / detail)

Question: "What is the result of young people leaving small towns?" A The towns become cheaper. B Some towns have shrinking, ageing populations. C Megacities become smaller.
Paragraph 4: "As a result, some towns face shrinking, ageing populations." Answer: B. ("As a result" tells us this is the effect.)

Worked example 2 (tone and inference)

Question: "What is the writer's attitude to the future of where people live?" A Certain that everyone will live in small towns. B Angry about city life. C Interested but cautious.
Clues: "may be narrowing", "remains to be seen", "may become less absolute". Answer: C.
Question: "What can be inferred about a ten-kilometre journey in a megacity?" The text says congestion can turn it into "a two-hour ordeal", so we can infer that traffic is very slow and the journey is unpleasant.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Choosing a strong emotion (angry, delighted) as the tone of an expository text. 2) Mistaking a statistic: in 1950 only TWO cities had more than ten million people. 3) Saying that the writer prefers megacities or small towns; the text is balanced. 4) Missing the contrast word (Yet, However). 5) Treating "may" as "will".

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 3B - Expository text (C1.1)": details, inference, mood/tone, connections, overall meaning. Full marks: (1) Read the first sentence of each paragraph to see the structure. (2) For tone, collect the adjectives and hedges. (3) For connections, look at the linkers and ask "cause, contrast, or example?" (4) For overall meaning, choose the option that includes both big city and small town, and the idea of change.

Quick summary

  • Expository = explains objectively; tone here is balanced, objective, cautiously hopeful.
  • Megacities: opportunities but congestion, pollution, high housing costs.
  • Small towns: quiet and cheap but young people leave.
  • Technology and remote work may narrow the gap.
  • Look for connections: Yet (contrast), As a result (effect).
