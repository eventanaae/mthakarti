Rise to the Challenge! Vocabulary A: Cities

WordMeaningExample
megacity (n)a very large city, usually with more than 10 million peopleTokyo and Cairo are megacities.
urbanisation (n)the process of people moving from the countryside into towns and citiesRapid urbanisation puts pressure on housing.
congestion (n)too much traffic, so that movement is slowTraffic congestion makes journeys longer.
pollution (n)damage to air, water or land by harmful substancesCar exhaust causes air pollution.
infrastructure (n)basic systems a society needs: roads, bridges, water pipes, power networksThe city invested in new infrastructure.
innovative (adj)using new and original ideas or methodsThe metro uses innovative driverless trains.

Collocations: traffic congestion; air / water / noise pollution; reduce pollution; rapid urbanisation; transport infrastructure; an innovative solution / design.

Word families: urban (adj) / urbanise (v) / urbanisation (n); congested (adj) / congestion (n); pollute (v) / pollutant (n) / polluted (adj) / pollution (n); innovate (v) / innovation (n) / innovative (adj).
Problem–solution chain: urbanisation → a city becomes a megacity → congestion and pollution increase → governments build better infrastructure and look for innovative solutions.
⚠️ British spelling 'urbanisation' and American 'urbanization' are both correct; the exam uses -isation. 'Infrastructure' is uncountable: 'new infrastructure', not 'new infrastructures' in most contexts.

The idea in detail

Unit 2 (Rise to the Challenge!) deals with problems we must solve. The first six words describe cities, their problems and their solutions.

WordMeaningRole in the "city story"
urbanisation (n) (التحضر / التوسع الحضري)people moving from the countryside to citiesthe START of the story (cause)
megacity (n) (مدينة عملاقة)a city with more than 10 million peoplethe RESULT of growth
congestion (n) (ازدحام)too much traffic, slow movementproblem 1
pollution (n) (تلوث)damage to air, water or landproblem 2
infrastructure (n) (بنية تحتية)roads, bridges, pipes, power networkswhat the city must build
innovative (adj) (مبتكر)new and originalthe kind of solution needed

Why / how it works

Words become easier to remember when they tell a story. Cause: urbanisation (people want jobs and education in cities). Effect: the city grows into a megacity. Problems: congestion (too many cars on too few roads) and pollution (exhaust gas). Solution: invest in infrastructure (metro lines, bridges, water systems) and use innovative ideas (driverless trains, smart traffic lights). In the UAE you can see this: the Dubai Metro is an example of infrastructure built to reduce congestion.

Grammar notes. Infrastructure is usually uncountable: "The city needs more infrastructure" (not "infrastructures"). Congestion and pollution are uncountable too. A megacity is countable: "Tokyo is a megacity; there are many megacities in Asia." Be careful with the adjective forms: congested (roads are congested), polluted (air is polluted), urban (urban areas).

Worked example 1

"The city's population has grown from two million to twelve million in thirty years, so it is now a (1)____. Rapid (2)____ means that more villagers move into the city every year. As a result, traffic (3)____ is a daily problem."
(1) More than 10 million people = megacity. (2) After "The", something like a process = urbanisation (the word "movement" would be natural English, but of the Unit words, urbanisation fits). (3) Collocation traffic + ____ = congestion.

Worked example 2

Choose the correct word: "The mayor wants an ____ solution, not an old idea." A infrastructure B innovative C polluted.
"An ____ solution" needs an adjective that means new and original. "Infrastructure" is a noun and "polluted" means dirty. Answer: B innovative.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) "infrastructures" and "congestions" - avoid the plural in most exam contexts. 2) Using "pollution" for traffic jams: pollution is about harmful substances; traffic jams are congestion. 3) Spelling: urbanisation (-isation in the exam), infrastructure, congestion. 4) Thinking a megacity is any big city: it usually needs 10 million+ inhabitants.

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 1 - Unit 2 - megacity, urbanisation, congestion..." Gap-fill with 3 options, often in a text about big cities. Full marks: look for collocation clues (traffic ____, air ____, rapid ____, transport ____) and for the story logic (cause - problem - solution). If two nouns seem possible, choose the one that fits the exact meaning in the sentence.

Quick summary

  • urbanisation → megacity (10 million+) → congestion + pollution → infrastructure + innovative ideas.
  • infrastructure, congestion, pollution: normally uncountable.
  • Collocations: traffic congestion, air pollution, rapid urbanisation, innovative solution.
  • Families: urban/urbanise/urbanisation; pollute/pollution; innovate/innovation/innovative.
