Word Formation: Prefixes & Suffixes

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 1 support · Word families

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Prefixes change meaning: un-, in-, im- (before b, m, p), il- (before l), ir- (before r) = not (unsustainable, inefficient, impossible, illogical, irresponsible); mis- = wrongly; re- = again; pre- = before; over- / under- = too much / too little (overpopulation); inter- = between; trans- = across; auto- = self (automation); bio- = life (biodiversity); mega- = very large (megacity).

Suffixes change the part of speech:

Part of speechSuffixesExam-list examples
Noun-tion/-sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ance/-ence, -ageautomation, congestion, pollution, insulation, urbanisation, privacy, drainage, biodiversity
Verb-ise/-ize, -ate, -en, -ifyurbanise, automate, insulate
Adjective-ive, -able/-ible, -al, -ory, -ic, -fulinnovative, heritable, auditory, kinaesthetic, significant
Adverb-lysignificantly, consequently
Clues for gaps: after a/an/the or an adjective → noun; after to or a modal → verb; before a noun or after be → adjective; describing a verb or a whole sentence → adverb.
'The city has made ____ progress.' → significant (adjective before a noun).
'Air ____ is rising.' → pollution (noun after 'air').
⚠️ Never write 'unlogical', 'inmature' or 'unresponsible'. Check the first letter of the root.

The idea in detail

A word family is a group of words built from the same root. If you know the root and the pattern of prefixes (added at the beginning) and suffixes (added at the end), you can understand many new words and choose the correct form in a gap. This is a very strong tool for Part 1 and Part 2.

Prefixes (change the meaning)

PrefixMeaningExamples
un-, in-, im-, il-, ir-notunsustainable, inefficient, impossible, illogical, irresponsible
mis-wronglymisunderstand
re-againrecycle, rebuild
over- / under-too much / too littleoverpopulation, underestimate
inter-, trans-between / acrossinternational, transport
auto-, bio-, mega-self / life / very largeautomation, biodiversity, megacity

Which "not" prefix? im- before b, m, p (impossible, immature, impatient); il- before l (illogical, illegal); ir- before r (irresponsible, irregular); in- or un- elsewhere (inefficient, unhappy). Un- is the most common; you have to learn some by heart (inactive but unusual).

Suffixes (change the part of speech)

  • Noun: -tion/-sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ance/-ence, -age.
  • Verb: -ise/-ize, -ate, -en, -ify.
  • Adjective: -ive, -able/-ible, -al, -ory, -ic, -ful.
  • Adverb: -ly.

Why / how it works

English builds words like Lego. Root pollute (verb) → pollution (noun) → polluted (adjective). Each form has a different job in the sentence. The position in the sentence tells you which job is needed:

  • after a / an / the / adjective or as a subject → noun;
  • after to, a modal (can, must, will) or a subject → verb;
  • after be, or before a noun → adjective;
  • modifying a verb, adjective or whole sentence → adverb.

Worked example 1

Complete with the correct form of innovate: "The company is famous for its ____ designs." (before a noun → adjective) → innovative. "The company introduced an important ____ last year." (after "an important" → noun) → innovation. "Engineers must ____ to solve the problem." (after "must" → verb) → innovate.

Worked example 2

Form the opposite: responsible → starts with r → irresponsible. possible → p → impossible. logical → l → illogical. sustainable → unsustainable. efficient → inefficient.
Now use one: "Burning fuel without limits is ____ (sustainable)." → unsustainable.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) "unlogical", "inmature", "unresponsible": check the first letter of the root. 2) Choosing a noun instead of an adjective: "a significance increase" ✗ → "a significant increase" ✓. 3) Forgetting spelling changes: automate → automation (drop the e), significant → significantly, happy → happily. 4) Choosing the adverb after "be": "The plan is successfully" ✗ → "successful" ✓.

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 1 support - Word families." In the exam, you may see three forms of one root (e.g. consequence, consequently, consequential) and need the one that fits grammatically. Full marks: (1) Look at the words around the gap. (2) Decide noun, verb, adjective or adverb. (3) Choose the option with the right suffix. (4) Reread to check the meaning (sometimes a prefix changes it to the opposite).

Quick summary

  • Prefix changes meaning; suffix changes part of speech.
  • Negative prefixes: un-, in-, im- (b, m, p), il- (l), ir- (r).
  • Noun after the/a/adjective; verb after to/modal; adjective after be or before a noun; adverb describes verbs.
  • Learn the whole family of every exam word.
