Exam Map & Part 1 Strategy (Vocabulary Gap-Fill)

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Overview · Parts 1–4 · Part 1 strategy

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

The End-of-Term 1 exam for Grade 12 Advanced (Beyond Grade 12 GLG, Stage 9, level B2.2) has four parts. All questions except writing are multiple choice with 3 options (A/B/C).

Part 1 strategy: 1) Read the whole text first for the general idea. 2) For each gap, decide the part of speech needed (noun, verb, adjective, adverb). 3) Look at collocations around the gap (traffic ____ → congestion). 4) Try each option in the gap and reread the full sentence.
⚠️ All three options are usually the same part of speech, so grammar alone is not enough: you must know the exact meaning of each word on the list.

The idea in detail

The End-of-Term 1 exam tests four skills: vocabulary, grammar, reading and writing. Knowing the map before you study helps you spend your time on the right things. The exam is based on the Beyond Grade 12 GLG, Stage 9, level B2.2 (B2.2 means upper-intermediate: you can understand the main ideas of complex texts and write clear, detailed text).

  • Part 1 - Vocabulary (gap-fill): one text of about 180 words with 5 gaps. For each gap you choose one word from three options (A, B or C). The words come from two lists: Unit 1 Pushing Boundaries and Unit 2 Rise to the Challenge!
  • Part 2 - Grammar: gap-fill or choose the correct form. The theme is My roof is alive! (living roofs). Seven grammar points: zero conditionals, comparatives, adverbs of quantity, present simple passive, indirect questions, adverbial linkers, sequencing adverbs.
  • Part 3A - Reading (argumentative): 330-350 words, theme Personality: Nature or Nurture?
  • Part 3B - Reading (expository): level C1.1, theme Megacities and Small Towns.
  • Part 4 - Writing: a summary of two points of view (4-5 sentences) and a comparative essay (at least 220 words).

Why this map matters

Think of the exam like a football match with four halves: each half needs a different skill. Parts 1 and 2 reward knowledge (words and rules you can memorise). Parts 3 and 4 reward skills (reading strategies and writing structure you must practise). Because every question except writing is multiple choice with only 3 options, you have a 33% chance even when guessing - but good strategy raises it close to 100%.

Worked example 1: a Part 1 gap

Sentence: "Many families install cameras at home, but some people fear that this is a threat to their ____." Options: A privacy B voyage C drainage.
Step 1 - Read the whole sentence: it is about cameras and fear.
Step 2 - Part of speech: after "their" we need a noun. All three options are nouns, so grammar does not help.
Step 3 - Meaning: a voyage is a journey (no); drainage takes water away (no); privacy = keeping your personal life to yourself (yes).
Answer: A privacy.

Worked example 2: a short gap text

"Rapid (1)____ is moving millions of people into cities. Traffic (2)____ makes journeys slow, so governments must improve (3)____ such as roads and bridges."
(1) After "Rapid" and before "is moving": a noun that means people moving to cities = urbanisation. (2) "Traffic ____" is a collocation = congestion. (3) Roads and bridges are infrastructure.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Reading only the sentence with the gap and ignoring the rest of the text. 2) Choosing a word because it "sounds familiar" without checking its meaning. 3) Leaving a question blank: always choose an answer. 4) In Part 4, writing fewer than 220 words, or forgetting to compare both topics.

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Overview - Parts 1-4 - Part 1 strategy." Typical question: "What is the first step in a vocabulary gap-fill?" or "How many gaps are there?" Full marks: know the numbers (5 gaps, 3 options, 330-350 words, 4-5 sentences, 220+ words) and the 4-step method: (1) read the whole text, (2) decide the part of speech, (3) check collocations, (4) test each option in the sentence.

Quick summary

  • Four parts: vocabulary, grammar, reading (3A + 3B), writing.
  • All questions except writing are 3-option multiple choice.
  • Part 1: 5 gaps, about 180 words, Unit 1 + Unit 2 vocabulary.
  • Part 2 theme: living roofs; seven grammar points.
  • Part 4: summary (4-5 sentences) + comparative essay (220+ words).
  • Gap strategy: read all, part of speech, collocation, test options.
