Pushing Boundaries Vocabulary A: Technology at Home

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 1 · Unit 1 · privacy, freedom, automation...

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Unit 1 explores technology that pushes limits: smart homes and facial recognition software. These topics raise questions about privacy and freedom.

WordMeaningExample
privacy (n)the right to keep your personal life and information to yourselfCameras in every room may threaten our privacy.
freedom (n)the right to make your own choices and live without controlSome fear constant monitoring limits our freedom.
automation (n)the use of machines or computers to do work automaticallyHome automation switches the lights off for you.
cost-effective (adj)giving good results for the money spentLED bulbs are a cost-effective way to cut utility bills.
database (n)an organised collection of information stored on a computerThe software compares your face with a database of photos.
interruption (n)something that stops an activity for a short timeConstant notifications are interruptions to our work.
distraction (n)something that takes your attention awayPhones are a major distraction in class.

Related words from the course: install, remotely, appliance, convenient, efficient, utility bill, security, monitor, recognition, fraud.

Word families: private (adj) / privacy (n); free (adj) / freedom (n); automate (v) / automatic (adj) / automation (n); interrupt (v) / interruption (n); distract (v) / distraction (n) / distracted (adj).
⚠️ interruption = something STOPS you (a call in the middle of a meeting); distraction = something pulls your ATTENTION away (music while studying). cost-effective is not the same as 'cheap': it means good value.

The idea in detail

Unit 1 (Pushing Boundaries) is about people and technology that go beyond old limits. In this lesson we study seven words about technology at home. For each word you must know: the meaning, the part of speech, a collocation (words that often go together) and the word family.

WordThink of it like thisTypical collocations
privacy (n) (الخصوصية)a door you can close on your personal lifeinvade / protect / respect privacy; privacy settings
freedom (n) (الحرية)being able to choose without being controlledfreedom of choice / speech; limit freedom

Why / how it works

These words appear together because they describe one story: a smart home uses automation (lights, air conditioning, locks). It is cost-effective because it saves electricity. But cameras and sensors collect information in a database, so people worry about privacy and freedom. Notifications from smart devices can become distractions and interruptions. When you read a Part 1 text on this theme, you can predict which words will appear.

Daily-life analogy: In Dubai, many villas have app-controlled air conditioning. It is cost-effective if it switches off when nobody is home. But an app that records who enters the villa raises a privacy question.

Worked example 1

"Smart speakers store voice recordings in a huge (1)____. Some users feel that this reduces their (2)____, because someone may listen to their private conversations. However, the devices are very (3)____: they cut electricity bills."
(1) A place where information is stored = database. (2) Listening to private talk threatens privacy (not "freedom" - freedom is about choices, and the sentence is about private information). (3) Saves money, good value = cost-effective.

Worked example 2

Choose: "My brother's phone rang twice during dinner. These ____ spoiled the meal." Options: A distractions B interruptions C databases.
The calls stopped the dinner for a moment, so the best answer is B interruptions. If the sentence said "music in the background made it hard to concentrate", the answer would be distraction.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Mixing interruption (something STOPS the activity) and distraction (attention goes AWAY while the activity may continue). 2) Thinking cost-effective means "cheap": a product can be expensive and still cost-effective if it saves more than it costs. 3) Spelling: privacy (not "privicy"), interruption (double r). 4) Using the wrong word family: "The automatic of lights" is wrong; use "the automation of lights".

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 1 - Unit 1 - privacy, freedom, automation..." You see a gap in a text and three nouns or adjectives of the same type. To get full marks: (1) read the sentence before and after; (2) ask "what is the idea: protection of personal life, storing information, saving money?"; (3) cross out two options that do not fit; (4) reread the sentence with your answer.

Quick summary

  • privacy = keeping personal life/information to yourself; freedom = making your own choices.
  • automation = machines work automatically; cost-effective = good value for money.
  • database = organised information stored on a computer.
  • interruption STOPS an activity; distraction pulls ATTENTION away.
  • Learn families: private/privacy, free/freedom, automate/automatic/automation, interrupt/interruption, distract/distraction/distracted.
