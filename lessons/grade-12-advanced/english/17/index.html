Part 4A: Summarising Two Points of View

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 4 · Summary (4–5 sentences)

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

In Part 4 you first summarise two points of view in 4–5 sentences.

  1. Introduce the topic in one sentence.
  2. Present view 1 and its main reason.
  3. Present view 2 and its main reason, using a contrast linker.
  4. (Optional) one sentence on what both sides agree on, or a neutral closing.
Rules: use your own words (paraphrase); include only main ideas, not examples or details; stay neutral – no 'I think'; use reporting verbs: argues, claims, believes, points out, suggests, maintains.

Contrast linkers: whereas, while, on the other hand, however, in contrast.

Model (Smart Homes): People disagree about whether smart homes improve our lives. Supporters argue that home automation is cost-effective because it reduces energy use and utility bills. They also claim that smart security systems make families safer. Critics, on the other hand, believe that smart devices threaten privacy, since companies may collect data about daily routines. Both sides agree, however, that people should understand how their data is used.
⚠️ Do not copy whole sentences from the texts, do not add your own opinion, and do not exceed the sentence limit.

The idea in detail

The first writing task in Part 4 is a summary of two points of view in 4-5 sentences. A summary is a short version of a text that keeps only the main ideas and is written in your own words. In this task you read (or listen to) two people who disagree, for example about smart homes, and report both opinions fairly.

Structure (4-5 sentences)

  1. Sentence 1: introduce the topic and say that people disagree.
  2. Sentence 2: view 1 and its main reason (who, reporting verb, why).
  3. Sentence 3: (optional) a second reason for view 1.
  4. Sentence 4: view 2 with a contrast linker and its main reason.
  5. Sentence 5: (optional) a neutral closing, e.g. what both sides agree on.

Why / how it works

Main idea or detail? A main idea answers "What is the person's opinion and why?" A detail is an example, a number or a story. Example: "Smart homes reduce bills" is a main idea; "my uncle saved 200 dirhams in March" is a detail - leave it out.

Paraphrasing means saying the same thing in different words: change the vocabulary (use synonyms), change the grammar (verb → noun) and the order. Example: "Smart devices collect data about daily routines" → "Companies may record information about how families live." Never copy a whole sentence.

Neutral language. Do not write "I think" or "In my opinion". Use reporting verbs: argues, claims, believes, points out, suggests, maintains, feels, and "According to ...". Use contrast linkers for the two sides: whereas, while, on the other hand, in contrast, however.

Worked example 1

Input (short): Speaker A: "Home automation is cost-effective. Lights and air conditioning switch off when I leave, so my utility bills are lower. Security cameras also help me protect my family." Speaker B: "I don't want cameras and microphones in my house. Companies store our data in databases and we don't know who sees it. My privacy matters more than saving money."
Summary (5 sentences): People disagree about whether smart homes improve family life. Supporters argue that home automation is cost-effective because it lowers energy bills. They also claim that security cameras make a family safer. Critics, on the other hand, believe that smart devices threaten privacy, since companies may collect and store personal data. Both sides therefore agree that families should think carefully about how smart technology is used.

Worked example 2

Topic: living in a megacity or in a small town.
Speaker A prefers the megacity: jobs, universities, entertainment. Speaker B prefers the small town: quiet, cheaper, friendly neighbours.
Summary (4 sentences): People have different opinions about where it is best to live. Some argue that a megacity offers more opportunities for work and education. Others, however, prefer a small town because life there is calmer, cheaper and more friendly. Each side values a different kind of lifestyle.
Check: 4 sentences, no examples, no "I", linker "however", paraphrased.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Copying sentences from the source. 2) Adding your own opinion ("I think smart homes are good"). 3) Writing more than 5 sentences, or very long sentences. 4) Including examples and small details. 5) Reporting only one side. 6) Forgetting who says what: use "supporters / critics", "the first speaker", "the second speaker". 7) Mistakes in reporting verbs: "She suggests that" ✓, "She says me" ✗.

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 4 - Summary (4-5 sentences)." Full marks: (1) accurate main ideas of BOTH views; (2) your own words; (3) a clear structure with a contrast linker; (4) correct grammar, spelling and punctuation; (5) the sentence limit respected. Before writing, underline the main idea of each view (one sentence each). Check: topic sentence + view 1 + view 2 + neutral ending.

Quick summary

  • 4-5 sentences; main ideas only; own words; neutral.
  • Topic sentence → view 1 + reason → (contrast linker) view 2 + reason → optional closing.
  • Reporting verbs: argues, claims, believes, points out, suggests.
  • Contrast: whereas, while, on the other hand, in contrast, however.
  • No examples, no "I think".
