Pushing Boundaries Vocabulary B: Journeys, Genes & Results

Collocations to remember: a long / space voyage; reach your destination; a popular tourist destination; heritable traits / characteristics; a significant difference / increase / role; serious / long-term consequences; as a consequence (= as a result); face the consequences.

Brain reading link: scientists study whether personality traits are heritable (nature) or shaped by experience (nurture). The teenage brain is still maturing, which has significant consequences for decision-making.
Word families: significant (adj) / significance (n) / significantly (adv); consequence (n) / consequently (adv); heritable (adj) / heredity, heritage (n) / inherit (v).
⚠️ heritage = traditions and culture passed down in a society; heritable = passed down biologically through genes. destination = the place you go TO, not the journey itself (that is the voyage).

The idea in detail

This lesson has five Unit 1 words that link to space exploration, genetics and the brain. They are useful in reading texts as well as in Part 1.

Why / how it works

Voyage and destination. Imagine a flight from Abu Dhabi to London. The voyage is the whole travel (the seven hours in the air); the destination is London. The Hope Probe's voyage took about seven months and its destination was Mars. In the Part 3A practice text, the writer says personality is "a voyage that continues for as long as we live" - the writer uses the word as a metaphor (a comparison without "like").

Heritable. Heritable traits are carried in genes: eye colour, height, blood type. A skill like speaking Arabic is not heritable - you learn it. This word is at the centre of the nature vs nurture debate. Do not confuse it with heritage (traditions, such as falconry or Al Ayyala dance in the UAE) or inherit (receive from a parent: "She inherited her mother's green eyes").

Significant and consequence. These work as cause and effect. Cause: "He stayed awake all night." Consequence: "He could not concentrate in class." A significant consequence is a big one. The adverb consequently and the phrase as a consequence both mean "as a result".

Worked example 1

"The spacecraft's (1)____ lasted seven months. When it finally reached its (2)____, scientists celebrated."
(1) A long journey through space = voyage. (2) The place it arrived at = destination. Check: swapping them gives "The spacecraft's destination lasted seven months" - this makes no sense because a place cannot last seven months.

Worked example 2

"Scientists studied twins and found a (1)____ difference in shyness between the groups. This suggests shyness is partly (2)____. A (3)____ of this finding is that schools should not blame children for being quiet."
(1) Adjective before noun "difference", big enough to matter = significant. (2) After "is partly", passed through genes = heritable. (3) After "A", a noun meaning result = consequence.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) heritable (genes) vs heritage (culture and traditions). 2) destination is the place, voyage is the journey - not the same. 3) significant is not only "big in size": it means "important enough to have an effect". 4) Writing "consequence of" for good results: it can be neutral, but in exams it usually describes a negative result. 5) Spelling: consequence (not "consiquence").

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 1 - Unit 1 - voyage, heritable, consequence..." You may also get a direct question such as "What is the difference between heritable and heritage?" or a word-form gap ("a ____ increase" - answer: significant). Full marks: give the meaning AND the contrast with the confusing word, e.g. "Heritable means passed through genes; heritage means culture and traditions passed down."

Quick summary

  • voyage = long journey; destination = where you are going.
  • heritable = passed on by genes; heritage = culture and traditions.
  • significant = important enough to have an effect; adverb: significantly.
  • consequence = result (consequently = as a result).
  • Collocations: reach a destination, serious consequences, a significant difference.
