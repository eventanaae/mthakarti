Grammar 6: Adverbial Linkers & Sequencing Adverbs

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 2 · Linkers & sequencing

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Adverbial linkers connect ideas between sentences. They usually come at the start of a sentence followed by a comma, or after a semicolon.

FunctionLinkersExample
AddingIn addition, Furthermore, Moreover, AlsoLiving roofs reduce heat. In addition, they absorb rain.
ContrastHowever, Nevertheless, On the other handThey are expensive. However, they save energy.
ResultTherefore, As a result, Consequently, ThusPlants absorb rainwater. As a result, flooding decreases.
ExampleFor example, For instanceMany cities have green roofs. For example, ...

Sequencing adverbs show the order of steps: First / Firstly, Second / Then / Next, After that, Afterwards, Meanwhile, Finally / Lastly.

First, engineers check that the roof is strong enough. Next, a waterproof layer is added. After that, soil is spread. Finally, the plants are planted.
Linkers vs conjunctions: 'However' links two sentences (It is costly. However, it saves money.). 'But' and 'although' join clauses in one sentence (It is costly, but it saves money.).
⚠️ 'At last' = after a long wait (emotional); 'Finally / Lastly' = the last item in a sequence. Do not use 'However' to add a similar idea, and do not use 'Therefore' for a contrast.

The idea in detail

Adverbial linkers (linking adverbs) join the ideas of two sentences and show how they are connected. They tell the reader if the next idea adds, contrasts, gives a result or an example. Sequencing adverbs show the order of steps in a process.

FunctionLinkersUse when...
AdditionIn addition, Furthermore, Moreover, Alsoa new point supports the previous one
ContrastHowever, Nevertheless, On the other hand, In contrastthe new point is opposite or unexpected
ResultTherefore, As a result, Consequently, Thusthe new sentence is the effect
ExampleFor example, For instanceyou give a case

Sequencing: First / Firstly → Second / Then / Next → After that / Afterwards → Meanwhile (at the same time) → Finally / Lastly.

Why / how it works

A linker is a road sign for the reader. "However" says "turn: the opposite idea is coming". "As a result" says "this is the effect". If you use the wrong sign, the reader gets lost. In the exam, the way to find the right linker is to ask: what is the relation between sentence 1 and sentence 2?

Punctuation. A linker usually starts the second sentence and has a comma after it: "Living roofs are expensive. However, they save energy." You can also use a semicolon: "...expensive; however, they save energy." A linker is not a conjunction, so it cannot join two sentences with just a comma ("...expensive, however they save energy" ✗).

Linker vs conjunction. But, although, so, and sit inside one sentence ("It is costly, but it saves money"). However, therefore, in addition connect two sentences.

Worked example 1

"Living roofs are expensive to install. ____, they save money on energy bills."
Relation: the first sentence is a disadvantage, the second an advantage → contrast → However.
"Plants absorb most of the rainwater. ____, there is less flooding."
Relation: effect → As a result (or Therefore).
"Living roofs reduce heat. ____, they provide a habitat for birds."
Relation: another advantage → In addition.

Worked example 2

Put the steps in order and add sequencing adverbs: (a) plants are planted, (b) the roof is checked, (c) soil is spread, (d) a waterproof layer is added.
First, the roof is checked. Next, a waterproof layer is added. After that, soil is spread. Finally, the plants are planted.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Using "However" to add a similar idea. 2) Using "Therefore" for contrast. 3) "At last" (after a long wait) instead of "Finally / Lastly". 4) Missing the comma after the linker. 5) Using "Moreover" or "However" in the middle of a clause without commas, or joining two sentences with only a comma. 6) "On the contrary" is not the same as "On the other hand".

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 2 - Linkers and sequencing." Gap between two sentences with options such as However / As a result / First. Full marks: (1) Read both sentences. (2) Name the relation: add, contrast, result, example or order. (3) Choose the linker with that function. (4) For process descriptions, the first step needs "First", the last needs "Finally".

Quick summary

  • Add: In addition, Furthermore, Moreover.
  • Contrast: However, Nevertheless, On the other hand.
  • Result: Therefore, As a result, Consequently.
  • Example: For example, For instance.
  • Sequence: First, Next, After that, Meanwhile, Finally.
  • Linker + comma; not the same as "but" or "although".
