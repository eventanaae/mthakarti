Reading Skills: Inference, Tone & Connections

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Parts 3A & 3B · Strategies

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي
  • Specific information / details: scan for names, numbers and key words, then read the sentence carefully. Answers are usually paraphrased.
  • Main point of a paragraph: usually in the topic sentence; the answer must cover the whole paragraph.
  • Inference: not stated directly but strongly supported by the text. Ask: 'What must be true if this is true?'
  • Mood / tone: the writer's attitude. Look at adjectives and hedging. Common tones: objective, balanced, critical, optimistic, cautious, enthusiastic, humorous, angry.
  • Connections: how ideas relate – cause/effect (as a result, therefore), contrast (however, yet, whereas), example (for instance), addition (moreover).
  • Overall meaning: the main message of the whole text, often in the introduction and conclusion.
  • Vocabulary in context: substitute each option into the sentence.
'Yet size comes at a price.' → 'Yet' signals a contrast with the advantages in the previous paragraph.
'As a result, some towns face shrinking populations.' → effect of young people leaving.
Argumentative text (3A): the writer takes a position and tries to convince. Expository text (3B): the writer explains a topic objectively.
⚠️ Wrong options often (a) copy words from the text but change the meaning, (b) are true in real life but not stated, or (c) are too extreme ('always', 'never', 'all').

The idea in detail

Reading questions in the exam are not random: each is a "type" with its own best strategy. If you know the type, you know what to do. This lesson gives a method for each type used in Parts 3A and 3B.

Why / how it works

Paraphrase. The exam writers do not copy the text into the correct answer. "Costs push many families to the edges of the city" becomes "Many families cannot afford to live in the centre". A distractor (wrong option) may copy words exactly but change the meaning.

Inference is a logical step. A good inference is strongly supported. If the text says "the town lost half of its young people in ten years", you can infer that the population is getting older, but you cannot infer that "the town will disappear".

Tone words. Positive: enthusiastic, optimistic, admiring. Negative: critical, angry, disappointed. Neutral: objective, balanced, informative. Cautious: uncertain, hesitant. Humorous. Hedging words (may, might, perhaps, seems, suggests) = caution. Words with strong feelings (terrible, amazing, shocking) = emotion.

Argumentative vs expository. Argumentative (3A): the writer takes a position and tries to convince you. Expository (3B): the writer explains a topic objectively.

Worked example 1

Text: "Yet size comes at a price." Question: "What does 'Yet' show?" The previous paragraph described advantages, and now the disadvantages start → contrast.
Text: "As a result, some towns face shrinking, ageing populations." Question: "What is the relationship?" → effect of young people leaving.

Worked example 2

Text: "Fast internet may allow some professionals to earn a city salary while enjoying small-town life. Whether this will reshape where people live remains to be seen."
Question A (tone): cautiously optimistic (may, remains to be seen).
Question B (inference): which is best? (1) Everyone will leave cities soon. (2) The writer is not sure about the long-term effect. (3) Internet is slow in small towns. → (2). (1) is too extreme; (3) contradicts the text.

Common mistakes

⚠️ The three types of wrong answer: (a) copies words from the text but changes the meaning; (b) is true in real life but not stated in the text; (c) is too extreme (always, never, all, only, nobody). Other mistakes: not reading the question carefully ("EXCEPT", "NOT", "according to paragraph 2"), spending too long on one question, and confusing facts with opinions.

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Parts 3A & 3B - Strategies." Every reading question belongs to one of the types above. Full marks: (1) Identify the type. (2) Find the paragraph. (3) Predict the answer before you read the options. (4) Eliminate extremes and unsupported options. (5) Check your answer against the text one last time.

Quick summary

  • Details: scan, then read carefully; answers are paraphrased.
  • Inference = strongly supported, not stated.
  • Tone: check adjectives and hedges.
  • Connections: cause/effect, contrast, example, addition.
  • Avoid options that are too extreme, untrue to the text or only copy words.
