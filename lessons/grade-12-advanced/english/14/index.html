Part 3A Practice: 'Personality: Nature or Nurture?'

📘 اللغة الإنجليزية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Part 3A · Argumentative text

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي

Practice text (original, argumentative, about 330 words)

[1] Why are some people outgoing while others are shy? For more than a century, scientists have argued about whether personality is shaped mainly by nature – the genes we inherit from our parents – or by nurture – the family, culture and experiences that surround us.

[2] Supporters of nature point to studies of twins. Identical twins share all of their genes, whereas non-identical twins share only about half. When identical twins turn out to be more alike in personality than non-identical twins, researchers conclude that traits such as shyness or risk-taking are partly heritable. Remarkably, some identical twins who were raised in different homes have surprisingly similar habits and interests.

[3] Supporters of nurture, however, argue that genes are only a starting point. A child who is naturally anxious may become confident if parents and teachers encourage her to try new things. Culture matters too: in some societies quiet, respectful behaviour is praised, while in others children are rewarded for speaking up. Moreover, the brain remains 'plastic' – able to change – throughout life, which means that experiences can reshape the way we think and behave.

[4] In my view, the debate itself is misleading. Asking whether nature or nurture is more important is like asking whether the length or the width of a rectangle matters more for its area. Genes may give us certain tendencies, but our environment decides whether those tendencies grow or fade. Therefore, rather than blaming our genes for our weaknesses, we should focus on the choices and experiences that we can control. After all, personality is not a fixed destination; it is a voyage that continues for as long as we live.

Question types: specific information (scan), details, main points of each paragraph, inference (what the writer implies), overall meaning (the writer's main argument).
⚠️ Paragraphs 2 and 3 report other people's views; the writer's own opinion is in paragraph 4.

The idea in detail

The practice text Personality: Nature or Nurture? is an argumentative text: the writer discusses a question, presents two sides and then gives a personal opinion. The exam asks five kinds of question: specific information, details, main points, inference and overall meaning. First, learn the structure of the text, then practise with questions.

Paragraph map

ParagraphMain pointUseful words
1Introduces the question: is personality shaped by nature (genes) or nurture (family, culture, experience)?"For more than a century, scientists have argued..."
2The nature side: twin studies; identical twins share all their genes, non-identical about half; some twins raised apart have similar habits"Remarkably", "surprisingly similar"
3The nurture side: genes are only a starting point; encouragement can change an anxious child; culture matters; the brain is plastic"however", "Moreover"
4The writer's own view: the debate is misleading because both matter; we should focus on what we can control"In my view", "Therefore"

Why / how it works

Argument structure. Introduction (question) → argument 1 (one side with evidence) → argument 2 (other side) → conclusion (writer's position). The writer uses an analogy (a comparison to explain an idea): asking whether nature or nurture matters more is like asking whether length or width matters more for the area of a rectangle - both are needed. The last sentence is a metaphor: personality is "a voyage", a journey that never ends.

How to answer each question type.

  • Specific information: find the key word (e.g. "twins"), go to that paragraph, read around it.
  • Details: compare each option with the exact sentence; the correct option is a paraphrase.
  • Main point: the option must cover the whole paragraph, not one example.
  • Inference: what the text suggests without saying it.
  • Overall meaning: the writer's final message.

Worked example 1 (details)

Question: "According to paragraph 2, why do researchers say shyness is partly heritable?" A Twins always live together. B Identical twins are more alike than non-identical twins. C Parents choose the personality of their children.
Paragraph 2: "When identical twins turn out to be more alike in personality than non-identical twins, researchers conclude that traits such as shyness... are partly heritable." Answer: B. A is not in the text; C is not in the text.

Worked example 2 (inference and overall meaning)

Question: "What does the writer suggest by comparing the debate to a rectangle?" A Nature is more important than nurture. B Both factors are needed, just as length and width are both needed for area. C Mathematics can solve the debate.
The writer says asking which matters more is "misleading", so both contribute. Answer: B.
Question: "What is the writer's main message?" Look at paragraph 4: genes give tendencies, the environment decides whether they grow, so we should concentrate on the choices we control. Main message: personality comes from both nature and nurture, and we can still change ourselves.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) Choosing an option from paragraph 2 or 3 as "the writer's opinion": those paragraphs report other people's views. 2) Choosing an option that is true in real life but not in the text. 3) Choosing options with extreme words (always, never, only). 4) Choosing the option that repeats words from the text but changes the meaning. 5) Not reading the question word: "According to paragraph 3" limits where you look.

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 3A - Argumentative text." Five or so 3-option questions with the text of 330-350 words. Full marks: (1) Skim: title, first sentence of each paragraph, last paragraph. (2) Read each question and find the paragraph. (3) Paraphrase the answer in your head before looking at options. (4) Eliminate options that are not in the text or are too extreme. (5) For overall meaning, use the conclusion.

Quick summary

  • Paragraphs 1: question; 2: nature; 3: nurture; 4: writer's view.
  • The writer's view: both nature and nurture matter, so the debate is misleading.
  • Analogy: length and width of a rectangle; metaphor: personality as a voyage.
  • Question types: specific information, details, main points, inference, overall meaning.
  • Correct answers are paraphrases.
