The idea in detail

This lesson covers two small topics from Unit 2: how people learn and how buildings can be greener. The green-building words are very important because the grammar part (Part 2) is about living roofs.

Learning styles

  • kinaesthetic (adj): learning by doing, moving, touching. Example: building a model, doing an experiment.
  • auditory (adj): learning by hearing. Example: listening to a podcast, discussing in a group.
  • visual (adj): learning by seeing. Example: mind maps, colours, diagrams.

Green buildings

WordWhat it does / isPicture it
insulation (n) (عزل)stops heat, cold or sound passing througha thick layer under the roof; the building stays cool, so the air conditioner works less
drainage (n) (تصريف المياه)takes extra water awaypipes and channels that stop flooding
biodiversity (n) (التنوع الحيوي)variety of living things in an areaa park with trees, birds, insects and flowers
vegetation (n) (الغطاء النباتي)plants in generalgrass, shrubs and small plants

Why / how it works

Learning styles: Everyone uses all three styles, but most people have a favourite. Knowing your style lets you study smarter. A kinaesthetic student can walk around while repeating a vocabulary list or write words on cards and sort them. An auditory student can record the word list and listen to it. A visual student can draw a picture for each word. This is also a good example of "rising to the challenge" of learning.

Green buildings: In a hot country like the UAE, a roof can reach very high temperatures. Plants on a roof (vegetation) give shade and cool the air. The soil and plants give natural insulation, so the building needs less air conditioning. In heavy rain, the soil absorbs water and the drainage layer carries the extra away. Insects and birds find food and shelter, so biodiversity increases. These ideas return in Lessons 7-12.

The adjectives' ending is a clue: kinaesthetic and auditory are adjectives (we say "an auditory learner"); insulation, drainage, biodiversity, vegetation are nouns (-ion, -age, -ity).

Worked example 1

Match the student to the style.
Huda draws colourful diagrams of the water cycle → visual.
Khalid repeats formulas aloud and listens to recorded lessons → auditory.
Maryam builds a model of a volcano and learns best in the lab → kinaesthetic.

Worked example 2

"The new school has thick (1)____ in the walls, so the classrooms stay cool. The playground has good (2)____, so puddles disappear quickly after rain. The garden is covered with low (3)____ and attracts many kinds of birds, which increases (4)____."
(1) stops heat passing = insulation. (2) takes water away = drainage. (3) plants in general, uncountable = vegetation. (4) variety of living things = biodiversity.

Common mistakes

⚠️ 1) vegetation (plants) vs vegetables (food). 2) auditory (hearing) vs audience (people who watch). 3) Spelling of kinaesthetic (British: -aes-; American: kinesthetic) - the exam uses the British form. 4) Using "a vegetation" or "vegetations": it is uncountable. 5) Mixing insulation (stops heat or cold) with drainage (water).

Exam corner

How it is asked: "Part 1 - Unit 2 - kinaesthetic, auditory, insulation..." Usually a gap in a paragraph about learning or about a green building. Full marks: identify the topic of the paragraph first (learning, or building/nature). Then choose the word from the matching group. Example: if the sentence is about "water after rain", only drainage fits.

Quick summary

  • kinaesthetic = doing/moving; auditory = listening; visual = seeing.
  • insulation = stops heat/cold/sound; drainage = removes water.
  • biodiversity = variety of living things; vegetation = plants (uncountable).
  • Families: insulate/insulation, drain/drainage, diverse/diversity.
