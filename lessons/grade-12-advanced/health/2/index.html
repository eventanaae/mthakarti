1.2 Parts of a medical word & combining vowels

🩺 العلوم الصحية🎓 الصف الثاني عشر متقدم📌 هيكل الامتحان: Unit 1 · Lesson 1.2 · p13

🎯 ذاكر الدرس وحل الكويز مجانًا في مذاكرتي
Medical words are built from parts: prefix (beginning) + root word (main meaning) + suffix (end), sometimes joined by a combining vowel. Put together, the parts describe the medical condition.
hyper / glyc / aemia
hyper = too much · glyc = sugar · aemia = blood
→ hyperglycaemia = too much sugar in the blood (ارتفاع سكر الدم).

Combining vowel (حرف العلة الرابط) – usually an o (but can be a, e, i, o, u):

  • makes medical terms easier to say;
  • does not change the meaning of the word;
  • is not always used; it links root + root or root + suffix.
RuleExample
Suffix starts with a consonant → keep the vowelcardi + o + logy = cardiology
Suffix starts with a vowel → drop the vowelgastr + itis = gastritis
Joining two roots → keep the vowelgastr + o + enter + itis = gastroenteritis

A root plus its combining vowel is called a combining form, e.g. cardi/o.

⚠️ To find the meaning, read the suffix first, then the prefix, then the root: gastr-itis = inflammation (-itis) of the stomach (gastr).

اختبر نفسك: أسئلة على 1.2 Parts of a medical word & combining vowels

In the word 'hyperglycaemia', what does the root 'glyc' mean?

✅ الجواب: Sugar

hyper = too much, glyc = sugar, aemia = blood.

What does 'hyperglycaemia' mean?

✅ الجواب: Too much sugar in the blood

hyper (too much) + glyc (sugar) + aemia (blood).

Which statement about combining vowels is TRUE?

✅ الجواب: They make words easier to say and do not change the meaning

A combining vowel (usually o) helps pronunciation without changing meaning.
